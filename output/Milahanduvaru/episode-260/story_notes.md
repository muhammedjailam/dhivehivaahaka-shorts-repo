# Milahanduvaru — Episode 260: story notes

Duration 1460.1 s (24 min 20 s) — the longest episode of the series. 160 shots, 54 beats, 53 new images + 1 in-episode reuse.

## Synopsis
The storm recitation at the thundi grows stronger; a jinn in Sakeena's form watches from afar but dares not come near. Late
at night the reciters, with Hassanfulhu going in first, enter Shamaan's empty house. Shamaan's parents return through the
gate, displeased; Najmee insists he saw "Sakeena" at the thundi. Zumra storms in, trembling with fury, demands that Bappa
throw Hassanfulhu out and threatens to get rid of him "even if I have to kill him".
Later Sakeena finds Shamaan asleep. He wakes and finds the bathroom locked: Zumra is inside, hurt all over by the
recitations. "This is what you humans do. Yes, I am a jinn." He tries to flee; she holds him back in tears — she has only
ever given him love and care — and he decides to stay with her.
Next morning a neighbour wails in the yard: Najmee of Alikoi has died. Sakeena and then the father go to the cemetery;
Shamaan, unmoved, refuses. In his room Zumra calls such people wicked ("they want to hurt me"); she vanishes at the
parents' footsteps. The father tells Shamaan (hand on his shoulder) he will bring Hassanfulhu to recite in the house.
Thursday night: Hassanfulhu recites in the house despite Shamaan's pleas. In the sitting room Zumra (red eyes) bars his
way; he sits and recites; she writhes and screams; little Yameen whimpers beside her. Shamaan lunges at Hassanfulhu and his
father pulls them apart. Zumra pleads; Sakeena calls her the jinn destroying the island; the father orders her out;
Shamaan holds her; Zumra warns her family will take revenge, then reveals she carries Shamaan's child. "We don't want a
jinn child!" shouts the father. A blinding flash — Zumra vanishes; Shamaan collapses and is laid on his bed in a wrecked
room; Hassanfulhu revives him with recited water. Hassanfulhu fears the unborn child's revenge; the father, merciless,
wants the line wiped out. Shamaan sobs and pleads for Zumra; his parents are unmoved. "The wall between humans and jinn
rose higher, and the garden of love vanished into the moonlight."
Next morning the island rejoices and thanks Hassanfulhu; Shamaan recovers. Days later a strange little girl is glimpsed at
the mosque at sunset and on the moonlit shore, vanishing when approached; many call it imagination.
A Friday afternoon: Sakeena on the joali with Yameen, who plays alone, laughing at nobody. At sunset Shamaan dozes on the
joali and is twice startled; Zumra calls him — she stands with a little girl. He sweeps the child into his arms. "Look
after our child... I will come every night." She vanishes; Sakeena takes him in. That night he lies on the joali; Zumra and
the girl sit with him; she is named Nazaaha. Sakeena finds him "talking alone" and drags him in while Zumra holds his
other hand; Zumra waits in tears with the child, unwilling to keep the girl from her father.
Next dusk Sakeena secretly watches him gesturing to unseen company; he claims he is singing; she tells the father, who
dismantles the joali. That night Zumra and Nazaaha visit his room (Nazaaha on his lap); Sakeena bursts in: "Let's see who
is stronger, your jinn or me." Zumra weeps: "Don't let anything bad happen to my daughter."
Morning: Hassanfulhu is coming back — this time the parents lead it; he arrives; sceptics call him a fraud, citing the
jinn child. First night's recitation in the yard: Shamaan asks to go to the toilet, bolts it, jumps the wall and runs to
the thundi, overpowering a passer-by who calls out. Exhausted at the waterline, he meets Zumra, who complains the humans
are destroying her world; Hassanfulhu's troop arrives reciting and takes him home by force while she weeps. Shamaan
calms and sleeps. Walking home, the reciter Shirhaan is called by "the jinn Zumra you're looking for"; she seizes him,
merciless, bent on revenge against the whole group. Morning: Sakeena's wailing — "On this island, again..." — "That jinn
wife of yours is harming the people of this island!"

## Tone
Dark, tense and sorrowful: a supernatural thriller built on a doomed love story. Moonlit indigo/silver nights with amber
lamplight; brief warm daylight for the island's relief and the Friday afternoon; tenderness in the father-daughter scenes.

## Image budget
1460 s / 53 new images = 27.5 s of audio per new image (brief target for this episode: 45-55 images). Why above the
general ~45 soft maximum: this is the longest episode (24 min) and the densest in scene changes — about 20 distinct
location/time settings (thundi storm; yard by night, morning, afternoon, dusk and recitation night; sitting room;
corridor; bedroom by night and by day; cemetery lane; village at sunrise; mosque at sunset; moonlit shore; jetty; two
night lanes; dawn sitting room) and many character entrances and action changes (Zumra's arrival, confession, the
Thursday showdown with five distinct actions, the flash, the collapse, Nazaaha's first appearances, the joali taken apart,
the escape over the wall, the lane encounter). Long talky stretches are held with shots instead of new images (beat 10 =
50 s, beat 22 = 52 s, beat 24 = 44 s, beat 33 = 43 s). The sceptics' "proof — the jinn child" returns to the moonlit-shore
image (beat 47 = reuse of beat 26).
