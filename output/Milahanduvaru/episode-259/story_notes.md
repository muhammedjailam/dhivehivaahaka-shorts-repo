## Synopsis
Night: Shamaan, holding his wife Zumra's hurt arm, tells her she wasn't at the recitation; she jokes "if I were a jinni I
could have been there". He wonders how she keeps getting hurt unseen, and whether Hassanfulhu (whom she blames) would
dare such harm in front of the islanders. On the island, Hassanfulhu's name means hope to some, fear to others.
Next day Shamaan's father returns from treatment in Malé, healthy but deeply uneasy. At the house he looks for Zumra
(absent) and demands to meet Hassanfulhu; Sakeena calls the reciter dangerous, Shamaan sobs that "they" want to destroy
the family and begs his father not to confront them. Inside, toddler Yameen runs to hug his grandfather, who finds him
thin; Sakeena (arranging plates) says he won't eat. The father asks where Zumra is — "she comes after sunset". The whole
family eats together; the father sleeps; Sakeena takes the crying Yameen out to the joali. At dusk the father, ready for
Maghrib, greets Zumra and is startled by burns on her arm — "this is Hassanfulhu's doing". Angry, he goes (Sakeena
following) to Hassanfulhu's house; after a wait Hassanfulhu arrives, greets them kindly, and insists Zumra is a jinn
girl, never seen by day, acting only after sunset. The father scorns him ("a man who chases girls") and leaves.
Home again he interrogates Shamaan: why is Zumra never home by day? Sakeena sends Shamaan inside, then argues with her
husband, who now doubts Zumra is human ("look at her arm — no one here did that").
Next sunset, an eerie stillness. After Maghrib, Hassanfulhu and many men go to the moonlit thundi for the kiyevelli.
The recitation brings calm, then a wind rises, leaves thrash, they feel an unseen presence; a squall of sand and heavy
rain hits. Some flee; Hassanfulhu and a few brave youths stay. A furious "Shamaan" appears in the distance — "That is not
Shamaan, don't look", Najmee trembles; the figure vanishes. Then "Sakeena" comes striding with a knife; all rise in
fear, Hassanfulhu stays seated: "That is not Sakeena — that is the jinni Zumra."
