# Milahanduvaru — Episode 256: story notes

Duration 908.4 s (15:08). 100 shots, 33 beats, 30 new images + 3 in-episode reuses.

## Synopsis
Late at night Zumra sits with Shamaan's parents. Sakeena hides her unease and asks where she is from: "a Malé girl"
who often visits her father's uninhabited island nearby; the father, coming out of his room, asks if it is the island
Donmanik looks after — "it's entrusted to Donmanik, father mostly lives in Singapore". She leaves just before dawn
("must reach the island before sunrise"); Shamaan sees her off, happy that Yameen will have a mother. A knock: his mother
"came to see if you were asleep"; when he praises Zumra she says "yes, she seems different from other girls" and walks
off, leaving him uneasy. Morning: alarm at 6:30, he skips the office; Yameen climbs onto the bed; on the joali he dozes
and his mother scolds him. At sunset, bathed and dressed, he waits on the joali; Zumra appears silently behind him and
takes him to the moonlit thundi. They talk of marrying "as soon as possible"; she warns she is busy with business all
day. She suddenly leaves; he runs after her; the sky darkens and heavy rain falls; under the big kaani tree she sits
calm and seemingly untouched by the rain while he shivers. Back at the house, soaked, she insists on leaving for "the
dhoni"; he stops her — "I'm a jinni, nothing will happen to me," she teases. She vanishes into the dark; the rain stops;
an unusual silence.
The wedding: Zumra arrives with her huge, powerful father ("arrived from abroad this afternoon; my mother has passed
away"). In the sitting room he asks Shamaan to be good to her: "she will be working very hard during the day". Wedding
night in the yard: coloured bulbs in the trees, flowers, tables. The qazi arrives; Shamaan radiant, Zumra head bowed
and troubled; the two fathers proud. The qazi sweats, glances at the door, rushes the nikah and leaves ("I'm unwell").
Shamaan's father asks why; Zumra answers harshly ("why should they stay so long?"). Shamaan takes her hand — she
snatches it away as if shocked, staring into a dark corner; Ahmadhufulhu stands and, with a tremor in his voice, calls
for the meal. Nobody eats; the parents are uneasy; Shamaan is spellbound. The party ends well; the family is proud of
the bride. The bedroom has been mysteriously decorated; Zumra: "I don't get tired like you"; her eyes flash; "father
leaves tonight; I don't know when he can come again".
Next morning: golden light but a deathly silent, empty house; no food made, last night's plates still out. On the joali
his frightened mother whispers the island's rumours: Zumra may be a jinni; her father was seen on an uninhabited island
and last night left by walking over the sea. Shamaan is torn: is Zumra really human?
