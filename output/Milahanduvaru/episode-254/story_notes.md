# Milahanduvaru — Episode 254 — story notes

Duration 822.8 s (13:43). 93 shots (from `work/segments.json`), 31 visual beats, 29 new images + 2 in-episode reuses.

## Synopsis
Continuing from 253: in the dusk-lit yard Shamaan weeps before his parents — how can he be patient when his own family
stole his happiness? His father admits aunt Naasira is jealous (she wanted Shamaan to marry her child and was angry at the
refusal) but cannot believe she would commit the great sin of sorcery. The father rises and lays a hand on his shoulder;
the fire in Shamaan does not die down. He shuts himself in his room and resolves to seek a religious scholar's help.
After that day he takes heart for Yameen's sake and cuts ties with Naasira's family.

Yameen's 2nd birthday falls in the Eid holidays: friends and island youths decorate the yard with balloons and banners;
Sakeena cooks; Yameen plays by the big banyan tree, laughing and running round it as if someone chases him — Shamaan
picks him up and, seeing a small cat, thinks the boy was chasing it. Late afternoon Shamaan bathes and dresses; Sakeena
welcomes the arriving children holding Yameen's hand. But Yameen keeps apart from the other children. At Maghrib Sakeena
takes him into the room: he waves and babbles at someone unseen, points and laughs at an empty corner, looks over her
shoulder and bursts into a strange laugh that gives her goosebumps. Shamaan comes in, reassures her (children imagine
things; only the toy box is there) but remembers a dark shadow that seemed to follow Yameen round the tree. That night he
finds Yameen curled at the foot of the bed, cold as ice. In the following days Yameen eats little, talks alone, strange
marks appear; Sakeena and Shamaan conclude it is spiritual.

The Eid games night: the floodlit field, football, volleyball, baibalaa. Walking home at eleven, a girl calls
"Shamaan, wait!" — a group of girls (Aroosha) deny it and tease him ("this girl says salaam"). At midnight his mother is
asleep beside Yameen; he takes a towel to bathe; heavy rain and wind begin. Coming out he feels someone walking behind
him; lying down, the room is full of a sweet flower scent he cannot trace. Before dawn he wakes to footsteps in the
room — no one. Next morning at practice he sits under a tree; his best friend Akram takes him home for tea and urges him
to come to the music show; Shamaan has no interest in fun or girls, only his son's future.

The night music show (bodu beru, crowds on chairs). Shamaan and Akram sit at a quiet edge; Akram goes up front alone.
"Hello… so lost?" — a strange girl, Zumra, unlike any island girl, startles him (narration: she lays a hand on his
shoulder — not shown). Displeased, he asks who she is; she smiles, sits on an empty chair uninvited, says "You'll find
out. Tomorrow night we'll meet right here," and walks off towards the stage. Akram rushes back about pretty girls from
the next island; Shamaan stays; Akram jokes "a jinni will come!" and leaves. Alone, fear creeps in: was Zumra human or
a jinni? Shamaan stands up to go and find Akram.
