# Milahanduvaru — Episode 255: story notes

## Synopsis
The music show ends; Shamaan cannot find Akram in the crowd and decides to walk home alone. A voice behind him —
Zumra again, with an indescribable smile. She tells him Akram is on the beach with some girls and insists on walking him
home; she reaches for his hand, he laughs and pulls free ("you're just a girl, I'm a man"), but the strange glow in her
eyes makes his heart race. They walk to his gate talking; she turns and walks off alone into the late night, and he
marvels at her courage. Inside, his mother and Yameen are asleep. Sleepless, he opens the window to the moonlight, then
goes out and lies on the joali in the yard. Someone covers his eyes from behind — Zumra, with a bag on her shoulder and a
book in her hand: she is "leaving on a trip" and stopped by; "tomorrow night, same place". She vanishes before his eyes.
He wakes on the joali at the Fajr call — real or a dream? He prays, sleeps, and his mother wakes him after nine; he takes
crying Yameen from her. At sunset he grooms at the mirror and lies on the joali, restless with hope. At eight he goes
alone to the show ground and sits where he sat last night; the crowd fills in around him. Akram plops down beside him and
teases him; Shamaan gets rid of him. The show starts, no Zumra; impatient, he walks to the stage and joins Akram and
three girls clapping. Then he sees Zumra at the edge of the ground and runs to her; Akram chases him, but sees no one
where Shamaan points and leaves, scared. On a stone bench Zumra sits with him ("I know you love me") and they go to the
big tree at the sandbank tip in moonlight. She says she is from Malé, staying on the nearby farming island, her
father's island. Seeing Akram's group approach, she jumps up in fear and vanishes into the darkness. Akram and the girls
find Shamaan alone; when he says his sweetheart just left, they exchange startled looks — there is no trace of anyone.
Akram pulls him away; Shamaan still sees her "standing far off". Zumra draws him to a tree as if by an unseen force:
"they can't see me." "You're a jinni, aren't you?" he jokes — her serious face: "Maybe." They sit on his joali; her cool
skin and strange beauty conquer him; he wants her to meet his family; "be patient". She agrees to come tomorrow night and
vanishes again — human, or another creature? Next morning his mother scolds him for coming home late; he announces he is
bringing a mother for Yameen, who will come at ten tonight. In the evening wind and rain break; he runs inside, sure she
can't come — but she is standing under the eaves, dry. He runs out overjoyed and brings her in calling "Mum! Mum!". His
mother comes out with Yameen, his father looks and turns back into his room, and his mother leads Zumra to a chair.
