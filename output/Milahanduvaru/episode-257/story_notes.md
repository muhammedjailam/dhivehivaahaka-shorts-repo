## Synopsis
After the wedding-night rumours, Shamaan feels the ground slip under him; remembering Zumra's words, her unusual
strength and the decorated room, he is afraid. His mother Sakeena is worried by the island gossip; Shamaan tells her it is
envy of his good wife. Sakeena remembers that before her own marriage there were the same stories, and that people even
brought fanditha-men from other islands to separate her and her husband — they survived by trusting each other. She cries,
then smiles and calls him for tea; Shamaan resolves to put his family's happiness first.

At Maghrib Zumra comes home anxious and upset ("someone is plotting to separate us; your own people give you bad advice";
Siththidaitha told her on the road that her work is useless and Shamaan is seeing another woman). Shamaan remembers his
friends' and relatives' warnings that Zumra lives too freely away from the island, but swears he trusts only her and holds
her hand; she cries, and they promise not to let any outside storm rock the boat of their marriage. Then Zumra rushes out
of the room in agitation and comes back a little later with a strange faint smile; he doesn't ask. Before Fajr she wakes
him ("I made food, I'm going").

In the morning Yameen climbs onto the bed; Shamaan realises he has neglected his son and takes him outside, where Sakeena
and the neighbour Shafeena tell him someone has badly hurt Alibe. They go to Alibe's crowded yard; Alibe has just died —
the sight breaks Shamaan's heart, he weeps and leads the funeral work. At sunset he finds Zumra home early and smiling; she
says Alibe's death was "good, even too late — he was a wicked man who harmed people with sorcery". Shamaan is angry.

Fear grips the island: frightening sights, doors shut at sunset, empty roads after Isha, nobody goes to the beach —
everyone says it is a jinn. Alibe's family decide to bring the atoll's most famous fanditha-man; he can only come a week
later; meanwhile Mudhimbe recites and sprinkles recited water. On the great day the whole island waits on the jetty as a
big dhoni crosses the golden lagoon: Hassanfulhu, all in white, calm and firm, is welcomed and lodged on Mudhimbe's
veranda. On the second day, at dusk, forty strong men wait at the thundi (no women allowed); after Maghrib he sits where
the waves break and recites — a sudden storm, wind, rain, thunder; some step back, he shouts "Be brave!". At Isha he stops:
"some jinn girl lives on this island". Sattar, afraid, says Shamaan is married to a very strange girl whom some call a
jinn; Hassanfulhu: "I'll go to that house — tomorrow". Walking home the youths are caught by a second storm; the island
sinks into darkness; Sattar shelters alone under a house's eaves to wait out the rain.

## Tone

dread (Alibe's death, the island in fear) → hope (the golden arrival) → a supernatural storm and a chilling accusation;
ends on a cliffhanger (Sattar alone in the rain).
