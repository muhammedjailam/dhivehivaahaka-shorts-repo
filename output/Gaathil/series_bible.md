# Gaathil (ޤާތިލް, "The Killer") — series bible

Dhivehi audio mystery-thriller. Story order (site episode ids): **606 → 607 → 608**
(one continuous arc; 607 opens by repeating the end of 606, 608 opens by repeating the end of 607; 608 ends on a
cliffhanger, more episodes likely).

Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is identical: a man wrapped in black — long black robe, black turban-style
head wrap and a black cloth over nose and mouth, grime-smeared skin around wary eyes — glancing back over his shoulder
beside a crumbling, mould-stained whitewashed coral-stone building with arched wooden windows, sandy ground, coconut
palms silhouetted against a grey-amber dusk sky over the sea. Desaturated, gritty, low-key cinematic. That palette
(slate grey, ash white, sepia sand, cold dusk, deep shadow, a little warm amber) is the series' signature.

SETTING: an unnamed Maldivian island. At its **east tip an old, crumbling white coral-stone lighthouse** (abandoned,
mould-streaked, leaning trees around it). Near the **east-side harbour a row of old abandoned warehouses (bā gudhan)**
with weathered, broken coral-stone walls — the wall in Shahid's photo is one of these. The **island club's warehouse
(kulabu gudhan)** where a powerful businessman was found murdered 20 years ago. The island **police station** (a modest
whitewashed building; a cold, bare interview room with a table, chairs, a window; Amjad's office with a laptop).
**Shahid's house**: an old coral-stone island house; his room = desk with a computer and a big monitor, camera gear,
a window with wooden shutters, a ceiling bulb and a desk lamp. Sandy lanes, small shops.
608 flashback: a **remote, sparsely populated island in another atoll** (fishing dhonis, a small house, a quiet
harbour), a modest wedding, a **small launch crossing the sea on a pitch-black night**, and on the home island a
**dense, frightening jungle** (big screw-pines, magoo shrubs) with an **old rusted iron hatch in the ground** leading
to an **underground bunker**: one small room with a dim electric bulb, a table, a narrow cot, sealed food boxes and
stacks of old papers.

Times: 606 = stormy night with heavy rain and lightning (lighthouse), Shahid's room at night (rain on the window), the
golden next morning, the police station mid-morning. 607 = same morning (police station), daytime at home, afternoon
at the harbour warehouses, evening at home (desk lamp). 608 = night at home (desk lamp, phone glow); then flashbacks:
"20 years ago" (Ibrahim flees, desaturated sepia memory), the other atoll's island (bright sea days, warm), the
night call, the black-night launch, the jungle and the bunker (dim yellow bulb, deep shadows).

## Episode synopses
- **606**: Pitch-dark stormy night, heavy rain. At the island's east tip the old crumbling lighthouse looks like a
  giant spirit rising from the black sea. Photographer Ali Shahid, who loves unusual scenes, photographs the lighthouse
  in the rain; with a thunderclap and lightning flash the shutter clicks. A strange cold feeling; goose-bumps. He hurries
  home through the rain, locks his room, connects the camera to his computer. The last photo: his eyes widen, the coffee
  mug slips onto the desk. In the dark background, among the trees' shadow, a figure — not an ordinary person: in a
  heavy old-fashioned thick coat of long ago, standing boldly in the downpour. Zooming 50, 100, 300%: it is **Ibrahim
  Naseem**, who vanished 20 years ago and whom the whole island buried as dead. Deep pain and fear on his face; his eyes
  look straight into the lens. The bulb flickers; a slow knock at the door; the window bangs in the wind; he clutches
  his chest, walks to the door, unlocks it: his old mother, worried — she heard something fall. He forces a smile,
  says the coffee mug fell, sends her off, locks the door again. Memory: 20 years ago (he was a small child) a powerful
  businessman was found murdered near the island club's warehouse; the last person seen there was Ibrahim Naseem, who
  then vanished; police and islanders searched the island, the uninhabited islands and the reefs; everyone concluded he
  had drowned or died. Suspicion fell on him. "But he's alive!" Zooming more: at Ibrahim's feet an unusually shaped
  piece of iron, behind him part of an old decaying wall — not the lighthouse, somewhere else on the island. He decides
  to take the photo to the police in the morning (narrator: if he knew who was at the bottom of this he'd delete it).
  Falls asleep. Golden morning, yet darkness in his heart; memory card in his pocket; he suspects every face in the lane.
  Police station: cold room, waiting. Chief **Officer Amjad** enters (respected, sharp eyes), sits: "What big matter?"
  Shahid silently puts the memory card in Amjad's laptop and opens the photo: "taken last night near the lighthouse."
  As the face enlarges Amjad's colour changes — he grips a pen hard, breath short — a secret fear, not surprise; he
  quickly controls himself, narrows his eyes. "This… this is Ibrahim Naseem, who went missing twenty years ago." Shahid:
  "Yes officer! He's not dead — he's living on the island; a danger to everyone." Amjad gets up and stares out of the
  window.
- **607**: Amjad at the window, a storm in his head. He puts a hand on Shahid's shoulder: "This is no ordinary matter —
  it's connected to national security. From today I handle it personally; no islander and no other officer may know.
  It's a secret between you and me." Shahid nods, but a seed of suspicion is planted. When Shahid leaves, Amjad locks
  the door and from the bottom of his drawer takes a secret phone: "Where are you? You've been seen! Your face got into
  a camera lens… Leave that place now!" Shahid walks out uneasy, decides to dig on his own. At home he studies the photo
  again: the broken iron piece, the old ruined wall — he recognises the old warehouses near the east harbour. There:
  fresh footprints and a small note hinting at a meeting. As he leaves, a strange figure behind him gives a warning and
  runs into the warehouses; Shahid chases but loses him; finds a disguise mask he dropped. Amjad arrives with a police
  team, questions Shahid sharply, orders "Seal the area! Nobody leaves!", then steps close and whispers: "I told you
  this is none of your business. Today you got away. Next time I see you here I'll have to arrest you." Shahid is now
  sure Amjad is hiding Ibrahim, maybe silencing him — and Shahid too. Walking away afraid, he feels the weight of his
  bag: inside, the silicone mask Ibrahim dropped. At home, door locked, under the bright desk lamp he examines it: inside
  near the edge a tiny maker's mark — the kind used only in films or by foreign spy agencies for special missions.
  "How did Ibrahim Naseem get such a thing?"
- **608**: (Recap of the mark.) Shahid grips the mouse, looks at other night photos: in a corner a fishing dhoni far
  out at sea; the man at the helm has the same body shape as the old man who ran from him today. Ibrahim lives in
  disguises — he imagines him as an old man with a long beard buying goods at shops, a young fisherman, a stranger
  labourer at the harbour — that is why nobody recognises him. Why does he meet the people who use the warehouses?
  A message from an unknown number (hands shaking): "That mask in your hand could become your death sentence. Don't try
  to find its secret! If Amjad sees it, that night will be the last night of your life!" He replays the words; questions
  without answers. To understand, we go back into Ibrahim's memories: years ago, desperate, Amjad laid all the blame for
  the murder on him; he fled for his life — Amjad's secret advice: "If you want to be safe, never even set foot on this
  island again." Amjad sent him away; Ibrahim landed on a lonely, sparsely populated island in another atoll with a new
  face from the silicone mask Amjad secretly gave him, introduced as **Adam**, a quiet, hard-working fisherman who loves
  solitude. Every day at sea, fighting himself, the dark past gnawing at him. Hope came from an innocent young woman,
  **Mariyam**; her kindness ended his loneliness; she knows nothing of his past. After a time he asked her family for her
  hand; the marriage was tied without obstacle. He started a new life — but every night, when lying down to sleep, he had
  to take off the fake mask secretly in the pitch dark. The pain of a husband who cannot show his own face to his wife.
  About two years later, one night, a call on his secret phone: Amjad, ordering him to leave immediately and come back
  to the old island, or Amjad would expose his mask in front of Mariyam. Forced, he takes Mariyam and flees: one night he
  tells her they must leave at once — "part of a very secret, dangerous government mission; to save our lives we must
  run." She, loving and trusting, follows without complaint. They arrive on a small launch on a pitch-black night at the
  island of his dark past (she still knows him only as Adam). He leads her not to the inhabited part but into the most
  isolated, dense, frightening jungle. "Adam… where are we going? Nobody lives here!" She grips his arm. "Mari… be quiet!
  Our safety is here." In the thick of big screw-pines and magoo shrubs, an old iron hatch raised from the ground; he
  opens it: a dark underground bunker. Her eyes widen. Inside a small room with electricity, secret papers, sealed food
  boxes; she has to start her new life in that narrow, frightening bunker room. On the second night she wakes: in dim
  light Adam sits at the table with a hand on his face, then pulls something like a skin from near his ear. She covers
  her mouth to stop a scream: he lifts the whole mask off and lays it on the table — a completely different, strange,
  ageing face. Not the Adam she knows. Who did she marry? She lies frozen, breath held; her eyes on the table where her
  husband's face lies — the silicone mask he just peeled off. Looking at the real face of the man at the table, all
  strength drains from her body.

## BINDING RULES (override the literal narration)
1. **Hijab always**: every woman (Shahid's mother, Mariyam) wears a hijab fully covering hair and neck in every image,
   also at home, at night and in the bunker. No other women needed.
2. **The 20-year-old murder is never shown**: no body, no blood, no weapon, no victim. Show the old club warehouse at
   night in a sepia memory haze with islanders holding lanterns gathered at a distance, police boats/torches searching
   reefs and uninhabited islands, a dark sea.
3. **No weapons anywhere**: police have no guns, no holsters, no batons; no handcuffs (arrest is only a spoken threat);
   no fighting (the stranger only warns and runs; Shahid only chases at a distance).
4. **The silicone mask is a prop, never body horror**: a soft, flesh-toned theatrical silicone face mask like a film-
   studio prop, lying flat or held in hands; no skin peeling, no blood, no gore, no flayed look. The removal (608
   seg 54–56) is shown with Adam seen from the side/behind in dim light, his hand at his jaw near the ear (seg 55), then
   the mask lying on the table (seg 56/59) and his real face (`ibrahim` card) in dim yellow light (seg 57/60) —
   ordinary, weathered, sad, not monstrous. Mariyam's reaction: wide eyes, hand over her mouth.
5. **Married couple Adam–Mariyam**: side by side; her hand gripping his arm is fine; no embrace, no lying down together,
   no kissing. The wedding = a modest nikah: families seated on a mat, henna-decorated hands, two cups, nothing
   intimate. "Every night when lying down to sleep he removes the mask in the dark" → Adam alone in a dark room seen
   from behind by a window, a hand raised to his face (no bed with the couple). Mariyam "lying asleep" in the bunker →
   she half-sits on a narrow cot under a shawl, fully dressed with hijab, waking with wide eyes; Adam far across the
   room at the table.
6. **Screens and text**: the computer monitor may show the photo (rainy dark trees, a figure in a heavy coat) but no
   interface text; phones are never readable — only a soft glow on the face; the "small note" at the warehouse is a
   folded scrap of paper with no visible writing; the maker's mark inside the mask = a tiny engraved abstract emblem
   (a small geometric symbol), never letters or numbers; the "secret papers" in the bunker are blank-looking stacks and
   folders. No signs, no boat registration, no police lettering, no badges with text.
7. **Ibrahim in the photo** is a real man (not a ghost): standing in the downpour among the shadows of trees, heavy
   old dark-brown wool coat, staring at the camera; at his feet an oddly shaped broken iron piece, behind him a broken
   old coral-stone wall. Keep it human and unsettling, never supernatural.
8. **The lighthouse** is an old abandoned white coral-stone lighthouse, crumbling and mould-stained, at the island's
   east tip; its "giant spirit" description is atmosphere only — no faces in the clouds.
9. **Children**: Shahid "was a small child" 20 years ago — do not show a child; use the memory imagery of rule 2.
10. Men may touch men: Amjad's hand on Shahid's shoulder (607), Amjad leaning close to whisper.
11. **Amjad** must look calm, respected and controlled in public; his fear shows only in small signs (gripping a pen,
    narrowed eyes, a turned back at the window). His secret phone = a plain old black phone held to his ear, office
    door shut.
12. No text/numbers anywhere, no music; no adhan needed.
