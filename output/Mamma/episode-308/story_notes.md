## Synopsis
PRESENT. A pitch-black storm night: no moon, no stars, the wind rising and the waves roaring. A passenger dhoni fights its
way into a small island's harbour and comes alongside the jetty; passengers hurry off before the downpour. Shahula (black
abaya, black hijab) steps off with her baby son Zidhaan asleep on her shoulder and a bag in her hand, and stands lost,
looking up the island's main road with nowhere to go. Rain begins. She walks up the road; lightning and thunder make her
flinch and the rain soaks her. Seeing the island mosque ahead, she hurries into the shelter of its porch, sets her bag
down and shifts the baby to her other, tired arm: "What a stormy night... when will this rain stop? How will I take
Zidhaan anywhere in this?" Thunder fills her with fear; she holds Zidhaan tighter ("Subhanallah!"). Whenever loneliness
takes over, the pages of the past open by themselves. She was one of the island's prettiest girls, raised without a
father by her brave single mother Khadheeja, who worked in other people's houses to pay for her schooling — but that love
lasted only a short while: Khadheeja died on just such a stormy night. Shahula's gaze falls on the cemetery beside the
mosque, now walled; she closes her tearful eyes and only one word escapes her: "Mamma".

MEMORY (age 12). Shahula takes a last look at her mother's face and breaks into sobs; Faathanikey draws her aside and
tells her to be patient; the shroud is drawn over the face and she is moved away. The bier is carried out of the house;
the eldest aunt (Bodudhaitha) stops her from following ("Let me go to my Mamma!"); nobody listens. Shut in a room, she
slips out and runs through the pouring rain to the cemetery, where she watches her mother laid to rest. When everyone has
gone she sits by the fresh grave and lays her head on the sand, and hears her mother's voice: "My child, light of my eyes —
don't let your education go dark; be a person of knowledge; stay firm on good character, be obedient; you are my whole
life." That night she burns with fever, murmuring "Mamma"; Faathanikey keeps changing wet cloths on her forehead, afraid
of what Zubair will do if he learns Shahula went to the cemetery. Zubair, Khadheeja's full brother, a fisherman, was far
from the atoll when she died; engine trouble delayed his dhoni, so on the imam's advice the burial was done without him.
NEXT MORNING his dhoni returns; he comes home with his share of fish, not a trace of grief on his face, lays the fish on a
tray to cut. Faathanikey brings a water pot and asks him to look at Shahula — her fever won't come down. He ignores her,
then snaps: "Go away, don't pester me! ... Is this the first time someone has died?" — "She was your own sister! Have you
forgotten all she did for you?" — "Didn't she make us pay for it? We can't manage our own lives and now Shahula too...
how good it would have been if she had taken her along!" He rinses his hands, takes the towel and goes inside.
Faathanikey, shocked and in tears, sits down to finish the fish — and sees Shahula in the doorway, wrapped in a blanket,
shivering with fever, tears streaming: she heard everything. (End of episode.)
