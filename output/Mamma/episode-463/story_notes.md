# Mamma — episode 463 — story notes

## Synopsis
The morning after Aamir's slap (end of 460). Shahula wakes alone in the dim bedroom, aching all over; she whispers
Aamir's name and, eyes closed, sees his cruelty again; she has always prayed for a loving family and now realises that
Allah's decree can differ from human plans. The baby Zidhaan cries; she lifts him from the cot and feeds him (shown only
as holding him wrapped under a shawl). Her split lip stings. Aamir comes in with a travel bag: he travels tomorrow —
iron clothes for two days, don't forget his shaving kit, toothbrush, toothpaste. She nods, crying. He sighs: "Why do you
make me angry, Shahoo? You know what happens when I'm angry." He sits on the edge of the bed, tries to stroke her cheek;
she shakes her head and pushes his hand away; he grips her arm hard. She looks at him with tear-filled but fearless
eyes; for once it is Aamir who hesitates: "Please... don't make me angry." She ignores him. The baby smiles at her, and
she counsels him pointedly in Aamir's hearing: be strong and kind, never hurt a helpless woman, be like your grandparents;
don't be educated-but-ignorant; women are mothers and sisters, jewels raised on a mother's love; give love, get love;
plant hatred, reap hatred. She looks Aamir straight in the eye; he is surprised; his phone rings and he walks out.
Alone, she is amazed at her own courage; her split lip and swollen forehead witness last night. She decides to leave
the house — because of what she endured the night her child was born, and Aamir's infidelity and cruelty.
FLASHBACK: her first labour — Aamir doesn't answer the phone; she takes a taxi to hospital alone, sobbing, wishing
Azeeza were alive; she calls her office friend Fiyaza. Eight hours later, after 2 a.m., the emergency caesarean is
delayed because no family member is there to consent; she signs the form herself, more afraid that Aamir will blame her
for a caesarean than of the pain. Next morning the little cot is empty; Fiyaza holds her hand, her tearful face says it
all; Shahula's silent sobs turn into loud weeping. Aamir blames her for the loss. Within five months she is pregnant
again; he ignores the doctors. The second birth is hard (blood needed, another caesarean). Next morning Aamir sits by
her bed, strokes her head, asks if she wants a drink — she thinks the baby has changed him; he goes for a king coconut,
but a canteen boy brings it; an hour later Aamir returns smelling of a woman's perfume.
PRESENT (the following stormy afternoon, Aamir away): she hurriedly takes the baby's clothes off the line under
gathering clouds, stuffs them into a bag, packs the bottle, milk tin and the little money from the savings tin, eyes on
the front door; every gust of wind makes her heart jump. The baby cries; she picks him up, opens the wardrobe, takes off
Azeeza's two gold bangles and leaves them in the drawer; with the birth certificate and papers she walks out of the
room — and stops dead, startled (cliffhanger).
