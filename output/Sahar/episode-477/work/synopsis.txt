SAHAR - EPISODE 477 - READER SYNOPSIS
(transcript: 65 segments, ~0:00-10:20)

======================================================================
1. SETTING
======================================================================
- Period: Palestine, 1948. This episode takes place about two days after the
  attack on Deir Yassin ([40] "these two days"; [8], [61] name Deir Yassin).
  Historically Deir Yassin was 9 April 1948, so this would be about 11 April 1948,
  in spring. The narration never names a season, but it does mention desert heat
  and hunger ([33-34]) and heat inside the metal box ([2]).
- Places:
  a) Haifa Port ("ހައިފާ ޕޯރޓު", [1]). Yazan arrives at 11 a.m. after about
     five hours in a pickup. A large, elegant ship is docked there.
  b) The ship: a luxurious cabin on the third (top) deck ([15-16]). The ship
     sails out to sea, and Yazan does not know the direction or the country
     ([30]).
  c) Hashim's house. The town is not named, but it is a refuge somewhere in
     Palestine outside the Deir Yassin area. It has a bedroom, a living hall
     (ފެންޑާ) with a sofa, a big modern kitchen, a well (ވަޅު) and a household
     truck ([29]).
- Time of day:
  * [1-18] Late morning, 11 a.m., at Haifa Port.
  * [19-29] Around midday. Sahar wakes at the Dhuhr adhan (މެންދުރު ނަމާދުގެ ބަންގި).
  * [30-41] Ship at sea, around midday or early afternoon. Yazan prays.
  * [42-54] Lunch preparation at Hashim's house (midday).
  * [54-57] Yazan's dream (a flashback or fantasy of Sahar's graduation).
  * [57-65] Afternoon on the ship. Yazan wakes up, and the crew woman arrives.
- Intercutting: the episode cuts back and forth between Yazan (prisoner,
  Haifa and the ship) and Sahar (Hashim's house). Yazan believes Sahar and his
  mother were abandoned in the desert and are probably dead. In fact they are
  alive and safe with Sahar's family.

======================================================================
2. SYNOPSIS (scene by scene)
======================================================================
[1-2] HAIFA PORT, 11 A.M. After a drive of about five hours, the pickup
  carrying Yazan reaches Haifa Port. Yazan has been shut inside an iron box
  (ދަގަނޑު ކޮށި) and can feel the heat on his body. His fear keeps growing.

[3-12] Yazan wonders why he has been brought there. A soldier (ހަނގުރާމަވެރިޔާ)
  takes him out of the box, handcuffs both his hands and leads him toward a big
  ship. Yazan, close to tears, asks where he is being taken and begs to be sent
  back to his family. He speaks in English, and the soldier answers in English.
  The soldier tells him to be grateful that he was let go without being killed:
  "We don't want you anywhere within smelling distance of this region." Yazan,
  trembling, asks why he wasn't killed. The soldier says: "You people are
  wealthy and generous. We wanted to loot the wealth of the rich in Deir Yassin
  and make the poor there poorer. That is how our power lasts. We hate you.
  Your whole family is dead. When you were arrested, your mother and your woman
  [wife] were left in the desert because we knew they would die there. We kept
  you alive so you would feel the grief and pain. We want you to suffer."
  He says this with arrogance. The narration notes that the takeover of
  Yazan's area had been carefully planned.

[13-19a] Yazan holds himself together. The soldier takes him aboard and up a
  staircase. The ship is beautifully designed, the kind that "civilised" or
  wealthy people travel on. Yazan had lived comfortably but had never seen such a
  ship. He is taken to the third deck, which he senses is the top. He asks
  pleadingly, "Where are you taking me?" The soldier says nothing, opens a
  cabin door, pushes Yazan in and knocks him to the floor. He warns him in a
  heavy voice: "Go to where you'll live. Don't even think of coming back to
  this region." He slams the door and leaves.

[19b-23] HASHIM'S HOUSE, DHUHR. Sahar wakes to the midday adhan. She gets up
  and looks for "the mothers" but sees no one, so she goes to the bathroom,
  makes wudu and prays. She comes out to the living hall. Her father Hamza and
  the owner of the house, Hashim, are sitting on a sofa. Hamza tells her kindly
  that the mothers are in the kitchen, worried because she slept so long, and
  that she should go to them. She asks where her younger brother is, since Ameen
  is not around. Hashim explains that Ameen is drawing water from the well for
  the mothers, which is not easy work.

[24-29] The adults' talk continues (Sahar may or may not still be present).
  Hamza's family's ordeal has been told to Hashim, who listens closely. Hashim
  advises leaving the area quickly. "They" will soon try to take over this area
  too. He calls the present moment a lull, like wind passing between two
  clouds, and warns that if they do not use it they will be trapped again.
  Hashim is well travelled and aware, and he is already thinking about the next
  danger. Hamza agrees: they should leave as soon as possible, because God will
  not abandon a servant who emigrates (hijra) seeking His face. Hashim, letting
  out a deep breath, says: "[I] and Safoora had two children; both are
  studying in Jordan. Let's set out in this house's truck; everyone will fit."
  (See Open Questions: the narration attributes this line to Hashim but names
  Safoora, who is otherwise Sahar's mother.) The plan is to flee to Jordan
  (އުރުދުން).

[30-41] THE SHIP, AT SEA. The soldier has locked Yazan in the cabin. The ship
  sails, and he does not know the direction or the country. The cabin has a big
  bed, a wardrobe, a small fridge beside the bed, a bathroom with a shower and
  a balcony with a sea view. It is lovely, but Yazan's life is in pieces. His
  beloved wife and his mother are "lost in the desert". If wild dogs don't kill
  them, hunger and heat might. He blames himself: "They suffered because of me.
  What a coward I am. Mother and Sahar were left to people worse than wild
  beasts because of me." He weeps. With no other option but to rely on God and
  pray for mercy, he makes wudu and prays two rak'ahs. The wounds the soldiers
  gave him make wudu painful on all four limbs (hands and feet). The scraped,
  torn places are still raw, and any drop of water makes his body shake from the
  sting. He is still a strong-hearted young man. He has seen many despairing
  things in these two days, and he keeps reminding himself that there is a
  Power greater than all and that He will fully repay this hardship.

[42-54] HASHIM'S HOUSE, KITCHEN. Safoora and all the women are busy cooking
  lunch. The kitchen is spacious and modern ("suited to the times"), with every
  utensil anyone could need. Sahar first stops at the well to chat with Ameen,
  then heads to the kitchen. Before she enters, Laila (Yazan's mother) speaks,
  her voice full of grief: "I keep thinking about Sahar. Even if she doesn't say
  it, the poor girl's heart must ache so much. Their marriage lasted only a few
  days, but they lived so lovingly. At night they would stay up late on the
  terrace talking about their hopes for the future and the work they would do
  to make their dreams real. What a beautiful, happy life we were living.
  Today everything is shattered. I'm weary of life." Another woman (most likely
  Safoora, possibly Fatima) answers: "Be patient, Laila! I know I can't truly
  feel the pain in your heart. Your husband and one child were martyred
  (shaheed). You don't even know whether Yazan is alive. But my heart says that,
  God willing, Yazan is alive. That boy will come to us, and we will meet again.
  Yazan is a brave boy with strong faith." Fatima starts crying too. The
  conversation stops when they see Sahar coming in. Safoora says warmly: "Sahar,
  habeeba, tell the fathers [Hamza and Hashim] to come and eat." Sahar
  pretends she did not hear them, and pretends not to see the mothers wiping
  their eyes, and leaves the kitchen.

[54-57] YAZAN'S DREAM (flashback or fantasy). Yazan sees Sahar at her
  graduation from Al-Azhar University (އަޒްހަރު ޔުނިވަރސިޓީ). She came first
  among the graduating doctors. In her ceremonial graduation robes she looks
  "like a houri/fairy (ހޫރު ޕަރީ) ready for the first night of marriage". Holding
  her medical degree, she comes down from the stage and runs to Yazan. When she
  reaches him they hold each other tightly. Then Yazan is woken by someone
  knocking on the door.

[58] He realises he fell asleep while making dua after his prayer. Weeping
  hard, he prays: "O Allah, decree a way for this servant and his woman [wife]
  to live together."

[59-65] THE SHIP, CABIN DOOR. The knocking comes again. Yazan straightens up,
  wipes his eyes and opens the door, afraid. A tall, young, beautiful woman stands
  outside. Her outfit shows she is ship crew. Yazan asks fearfully, "What
  happened?" She stands facing him with her arms folded (ހަތަރުކަޅި ހަމަކޮށްގެން)
  and says: "I've come to explain how the journey will go. You're the prisoner
  brought from Deir Yassin in Palestine, right?" Yazan, head bowed: "Yes. But I
  don't know what my crime is, or why I had to be put on this ship as a
  prisoner." She replies: "I didn't ask why you were brought. I know how things
  are going. Let's sit in the chair out on the balcony. I'll tell you what has
  happened and what will happen next." She walks into the cabin. (End of
  episode, cliffhanger.)

======================================================================
YAZAN (ޔަޒަން) - male, young man (ޒުވާނެއް [40]), estimated 25-30. Sahar's
  husband of only a few days [45]. Laila's son [49-50]. Comes from a wealthy
  Deir Yassin family ("lived a comfortable life" [15]). Speaks English [5].
  Physical state: injured by the soldiers; raw, torn, scraped wounds on his hands
  and feet [38-39]; handcuffed [4]; weeping [5, 36, 58]. Pious: prays and makes
  dua. Arc: delivered as a prisoner to Haifa Port, locked in a luxurious ship
  cabin, believes his wife and mother are dead in the desert, dreams of Sahar,
  and meets a crew woman who is about to explain his fate. No clothing described.

SAHAR (ސަހަރް) - female, title character. Estimated 24-27. A doctor who
  graduated first in her class at Al-Azhar University [55]. Yazan's new wife.
  Daughter of Hamza and Safoora, older sister of Ameen. Clothing: only in
  Yazan's dream, ceremonial graduation robes (ރަސްމިއްޔާތަށް ލައިފައި ހުރި ހެދުން

  her and Yazan, hides her pain and pretends not to notice.

HAMZA (ހަމްޒާ) - male, estimated 50s. Sahar's father ("ސަހަރްގެ ބައްޕަ ހަމްޒާ"
  [21]). Husband of Safoora, father of Ameen. Gentle and pious. Agrees to emigrate

HASHIM (ހާޝިމް) - male, estimated 50s-60s. Owner of the house where the family
  is sheltering [21]. A well-travelled, worldly, aware man [26]. Advises leaving

SAFOORA (ޞަފޫރާ) - female, estimated late 40s-50s. Most likely Sahar's mother:

FATIMA (ފާތިމާ) - female, adult (estimated 40s-50s). One of the women in the

AMEEN (އަމީން) - male. Sahar's younger brother (ކޮއްކޮ [22-23]). Son of Hamza

6. OPEN QUESTIONS / AMBIGUITIES
======================================================================
- [28] "އަހަންނާ ޞަފޫރާއަށް ލިބުނީ ދެ ކުދިން... އުރުދުންގައި" is attributed to
  Hashim [29], but Safoora otherwise reads as Sahar's mother (Hamza's wife). The
  options are: (a) Hamza actually says it (his two children, Sahar and Ameen;
  "studying in Jordan" doesn't fit them, though), (b) Hashim's wife is also
  named Safoora (unlikely), or (c) a narration slip for Fatima. The most likely
  reading is that Hashim's two children study in Jordan, which is why Jordan is
  the destination.
- Speaker of [48-51] ("Be patient, Laila..."): unattributed. Probably Safoora
  (she speaks next, at [52-53]). The narration then says "Fatima also cried".
- Fatima's identity is not stated. She may be Hashim's wife.
- The soldier says "your mother and the woman (އަންހެނުން)", meaning Yazan's wife
  Sahar [10]. Yazan uses "this servant's woman" in his dua [58]. Both mean wife.
- Deir Yassin is spelled two ways: "ޑެއިރް ޔާސިން" [8] and "ޑެއިރް ޔާސީން" [61].
- Spelling variants: ސަހަރް / ސަހަރްއަށް / ސަހަރްގެ; ފާޚާނާ [32, 37] vs ފާހާނާ [20];
  ޔަޒަންއަށް / ޔަޒަންނަށް.
- Ameen's age is not stated.
- Ships from Haifa: the destination country is unknown so far.
- How Sahar and Laila got from the desert to Hashim's house was evidently
  covered in earlier episodes. This episode only implies they were rescued or
  escaped.
