# Sahar (ސަހަރް) — series bible

Dhivehi audio drama, a historical war drama set in **Palestine, spring 1948**. Story order of the five episodes we have
(site episode ids; they form one continuous arc in this order): **359 → 367 → 421 → 477 → 574**.
Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is the same: a young man (Yazan, curly black hair, keffiyeh) and a young woman
(Sahar, black hijab) before a burning stone city under an ember sky, with the red Thaana title. Match its palette (see
`style.txt`): ember orange, smoky amber, charcoal; but our images NEVER show the embrace, fire engulfing people,
wounds or grime on faces.

SETTING: 1948 Palestinian hill villages west of Jerusalem: **Deir Yassin** (golden limestone two-storey houses with
arched windows, flat roofs, courtyards with flowering gardens, olive and almond trees, terraced hillsides, dirt lanes),
the wooded hills and caves around it, **Ein Karem** (a greener valley village of stone houses), the road to
**Al-Quds / Jerusalem** (old stone city, narrow lanes, domes in the distance — never readable signs), **Haifa port** (an
old steamship), the sea voyage to France, and the night road to the **Jordanian border**. Period details: oil lamps,
clay jars, copper pots, woven rugs, wooden chests, a stone well, 1940s trucks with wooden cargo beds, a crank telephone
(574). No modern objects.

## Episodes (one continuous arc)
- **359** — 9 March 1948, Deir Yassin: the lavish wedding of Sahar (18) and Yazan (20); their fathers Hamza and
  Mahmood are well-off best friends. Sahar moves into Mahmood's two-storey stone house with its flowering garden; the
  couple plan to study at Al-Azhar in Cairo (Sahar: medicine). The narrator explains the Zionist movement (historical
  context → symbolic landscapes, maps of hills, no flags/insignia). A month passes. An evening on the terrace: Yazan
  naps, Sahar and Noor tease him, Noor throws a rose at him and runs. In the yard Mahmood says: sleep early, the truck
  leaves at dawn (for Egypt). Dawn: the couple pack, Laila and Noor make a farewell breakfast — gunfire, Laila screams.
  Militiamen shoot Mahmood; he dies saying the Shahada in Laila's arms. In the kitchen five men attack Noor (sexual
  assault, then her throat is cut) while Yazan is held. He breaks free, strikes one with a stone at the door, and runs
  with his mother and wife.
- **367** — Same morning: Sahar, Yazan and Laila flee through the burning village (narration describes families lined up
  and hanged, bodies). Yazan is shot in the leg, beaten; Sahar's left arm is broken; Yazan is dragged to a truck
  ("tell Sahar I love her"); Laila is dragged, spat on, thrown down; Sahar faints. She wakes: Yazan gone; the women
  weep. Safiyya (forehead wound) arrives with her daughter Sama and talks Laila out of despair through faith; Laila
  tears cloth to bandage Sahar's arm and Safiyya's head. The four climb toward the wooded hill past the dead; Sahar's
  parents' house goes up in flames; from the trees she watches her childhood home burn. Sahar suggests hiding in a
  cave she played in as a child. Near noon they reach the cave and see "a scene hard to believe" (cliffhanger). Cut to
  Yazan: paraded on the truck through Al-Quds nailed by his hands and feet to an iron pole for an hour; at the soldiers'
  camp his wounds are bound with burning rags; he faints.
- **421** — The cave: the "unbelievable scene" is Sahar's parents Hamza and Fathimaa and brother Ameen, alive, hiding
  there — overjoyed reunion. The bad news: Mahmood and Noor martyred, Yazan missing, Sahar's arm deliberately broken.
  Hamza and Fathimaa tend wounds; Safiyya tells how Sama's father was killed and they escaped over a wall; Ameen comforts
  Sahar to sleep. Hamza had foreseen this and stocked the cave with food. They must leave: midnight, by hand-lamp, over
  rocks and thorny paths through the dark forest toward Ein Karem. Parallel: Yazan chained by the neck in the soldiers'
  camp; a soldier urinates on his face (humiliation); he prays Fajr sitting; is given two olives and a capful of water;
  is caged and loaded onto a lorry. The family reach Ein Karem at dawn; Hashim and Safoora take them in. Sahar lies awake
  thinking of Yazan, prays Fajr and dhikr until sunrise, removes her face veil in the women's room, sleeps beside Laila.
- **477** — ~Two days after Deir Yassin. 11 a.m.: Yazan reaches Haifa port after five hours locked in a hot iron box on
  a pickup; a soldier handcuffs him and taunts him in English (his family is dead, his mother and wife left in the
  desert); pushes him into a surprisingly luxurious cabin on the ship's top deck, slams the door. At Hashim's house
  Sahar wakes at the Dhuhr adhan and prays; Hamza and Hashim on the sofa, Ameen at the well. Hashim urges flight during
  the lull; Hamza agrees (hijra) — the household truck to Jordan. The ship sails; Yazan blames himself, weeps, makes
  painful wudu and prays two rak'ahs. In the kitchen Laila grieves; Safoora consoles her (Yazan is alive), Fathimaa
  cries; Sahar pretends not to hear. Yazan dreams Sahar graduates first in her class as a doctor at Al-Azhar and runs
  from the stage to him (show at arm's length, both hands held — no embrace). He wakes during his dua and weeps. A tall
  young crew woman (Claire) knocks: she will explain "what happened and what will happen" on the balcony (cliffhanger).
- **574** — Evening at Hashim's house: the family decide to flee to Jordan tonight; Sahar fears Yazan will be killed.
  Hashim and Hamza check the truck; goods loaded by 8 pm; passports — Hashim's son Abdullah has a friend in Jordanian
  immigration. On the French ship (about 30 prisoners to France), Claire tells Yazan he was spared only because Saudi
  Arabia warned against setting up the new state. Rain and a storm; they move inside his cabin (door left open in our
  images); France is five days away. Yazan wonders why she is kind, vows to find Sahar; seasick (at a rail / cabin
  porthole, hand over mouth), prays. 3 a.m.: Hashim locks his house "forever" and holds Safoora's hand; they leave while
  the checkpoint fighters on the Ein Karem–Jerusalem road are drunk. In the truck bed Sahar weeps for Yazan; Ameen puts an
  arm round her. Checkpoints let them pass. At Ramadha (Ramallah) the dawn adhan; they pray in the truck. 6 a.m. the
  Jordanian border; Hashim telephones Abdullah, the entry papers come, everyone weeps with joy; they plan to accept
  Jordanian citizenship for Palestinian refugees.

## BINDING content rules (override the literal narration)
1. **No violence on screen, ever.** No guns, rifles, knives, daggers, stones in hand mid-strike, punches, dragging by the
   hair, blood, wounds, bruises, grime/cuts on faces, burning people, bodies, hanged people, people lying on the ground.
   Gunfire = birds bursting from an olive tree, smoke rising over rooftops, a dropped clay cup, a face turning in shock.
   The burning village = columns of smoke and an ember sky over distant stone houses seen from the hill, with no people
   in the fire. "Bodies on the path" = an abandoned bundle, a lost sandal, a fallen basket of bread, an empty doorway.
2. **Mahmood's death (359):** never show him hit or dying. Show the moment as: Laila kneeling from behind in the dawn-lit
   courtyard, her white headscarf, head bowed, hands raised in dua; or Mahmood's white keffiyeh and prayer beads fallen
   on the stone steps. Shahada = a raised index finger silhouetted against the dawn sky (only his hand, no wound).
3. **Noor's assault and murder (359): NOTHING of it is shown or implied visually** — no Noor near men, no attackers, no
   torn clothes, no dagger. Show only: Yazan's horrified face in the kitchen doorway lit by dawn; the kitchen from the
   doorway empty with an overturned copper pot and a fallen breakfast tray; then the single red rose (from the terrace
   scene) lying on the stone floor; a door shut. Hum, no SFX for the attack except a muffled offscreen soft_thud when the
   narration says Yazan knocks a man down.
4. **Yazan's capture and torture (367, 421, 477):** never show the shooting, beating, nailing, pole, burning rags,
   chain on the neck, urination, cage bars on his body, handcuffs. Show: an old army truck driving away down a dusty
   road in smoke; Yazan seen from behind sitting in the truck bed, head bowed; Jerusalem's old stone lanes with a silent
   crowd of onlookers seen from behind looking toward a passing truck; Yazan's face in shadow, eyes closed, lips moving
   in dua; his hands resting palms-up loosely wrapped in clean white cloth; a dark tent camp at night with a fire far
   off; a dark iron box/crate on a truck under harsh sun (closed, from outside); at Haifa a soldier only as a back-lit
   silhouette in a doorway.
5. **Hijab always:** every woman and girl wears her headscarf fully covering hair and neck in every shot, also indoors,
   in the cave, while sleeping (show sleeping women SITTING against a wall or seen as a still covered shape under a
   blanket from a distance — never lying in a bed), and in the women's room in 421.
6. **Married couple Sahar & Yazan:** may sit or stand side by side, exchange glances, hold hands (also at the wedding and
   in the dream). NO embrace, kiss, head on chest, lying together (the cover's embrace is NOT reproduced). Sahar's
   memory of "his caresses" (421) = her looking at her own empty hand in the lamplight, or a memory of them walking side
   by side in the garden at golden hour.
7. **No other man touches Sahar.** Father Hamza may hold her hand or put a hand on her head; brother Ameen may hold her
   hand / an arm round her shoulders. Laila, Fathimaa, Safiyya, Safoora may hug her.
8. **Claire & Yazan:** never close, never touching, never in a romantic framing; she stands by an open door or on the open
   deck, arms folded, he stands or sits far from her. No cabin "alone with the door closed" image.
9. **Soldiers / militias / politics:** soldiers only as faceless distant silhouettes, never weapons, uniforms with
   insignia, flags, Stars of David or any symbols. The historical explanation (Zionist movement, the planned state,
   Saudi Arabia's warning) = maps-free symbolic landscapes: the hills at dusk, storm clouds over the Jerusalem skyline,
   an old ship at sea, a wax-sealed letter on a desk (no text). No national flags at all.
10. **Prayer & sacred:** prayer is shown respectfully (a person on a simple mat facing a dawn window, or sitting in the
    truck bed with hands raised). Mosques/domes only as distant silhouettes. No Quranic text, no Arabic script. The adhan
    is NOT synthesised (no voice SFX).
11. **Drinking:** the "drunk" checkpoint fighters = dark silhouettes around a small fire far off, swaying; no bottles.
    Seasickness = Yazan gripping a rail at a porthole, eyes shut — no vomit.
12. No readable text anywhere (passports = a small closed dark booklet, documents = folded papers with no visible writing,
    the telephone = an old wall crank phone).
