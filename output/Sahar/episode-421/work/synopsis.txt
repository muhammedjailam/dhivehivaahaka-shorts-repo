1. SETTING
======================================================================
Place: Mandatory Palestine, the Jerusalem hills, right after the attack on Deir Yassin.
  - A hidden hillside cave near Deir Yassin. It was Hamza's family's hiding place and Sahar's childhood haunt. [1-52]
  - An open field / soldiers' camp: Yazan held captive by a fire. [53-65]
  - A night walk over the mountain and through dense forest/scrub. [50-52, 66-68]
  - Ein Karem (written "އެއިން ކަރަމް" [53] / "އެއިން ކަރެމް" [68, 70]), a village very close to Deir Yassin [70]: the house of a middle-aged couple. [69-83]
Year/period: April 1948, the day of the Deir Yassin attack (9 April 1948) and the night and dawn after it. The narration names "Deir Yassin" [70] and "Jewish soldiers" (ޔަހޫދީ ހަނގުރާމަވެރިން) [33]. One soldier speaks English [58].
Season: spring (April). The narration never mentions weather.
Time-of-day progression:
  - Attack day, daytime: the reunion in the cave, then Sahar sleeps. [1-28]
  - Later the same day (afternoon/evening): Sahar wakes and eats. Fathimaa tells what happened that morning: Hamza came back from the Fajr prayer, saw armed men, and gunfire started. [29-45]
  - Night: Hamza waits until midnight (މެންދަމު). They leave by hand-lamp. [46-52]
  - Parallel thread, night to sunrise: Yazan wakes at night by a fire, prays Fajr, the sun rises, he gets "breakfast", and he is loaded onto a lorry in a cage. [53-65]
  - Night, near dawn: the family walks through dark forest and reaches Ein Karem close to Fajr time, with the whole village asleep. [66-72]
  - Dawn to sunrise: the Fajr adhan sounds. Sahar prays, does dhikr until sunrise, then sleeps. [79-83]
  - Note: the narration says Hamza wants them gone "before sunrise" [41], but they actually leave at midnight [46].

2. SYNOPSIS (scene by scene)
======================================================================
[1-8] THE CAVE: REUNION. Sahar, Laila, Safiyya and Samaa arrive at the mouth of a cave. Fathimaa cries out "Subhanallah! Hamza... there is my Sahar, safe!", runs to her and hugs her. Hamza hugs Sahar too, greets Laila and the others, and asks everyone to sit. Inside, Sahar sees her mother (Fathimaa), her father (Hamza) and her younger brother Ameen safe, and feels some relief. The cave is large and roomy, and trees and big rocks hide it from anyone walking below. It holds many of Sahar's childhood memories. Hamza and Fathimaa had not expected to see Sahar alive, because they had heard the fighters first attacked the houses in Sahar's area.

[9-16] BAD NEWS. Hamza, pale, asks: "Did my child's arm break?" Laila, voice trembling: "It didn't break... it was broken (by them)." Fathimaa, hand on her chest: "Where are the others? Mahmood, Noor... where is my child Yazan?" Sahar can only say "Mother..." before her voice breaks. Laila's face is drawn with grief for her husband and two children. Sobbing, she tells the story. Fathimaa, hand over her face: "If this is a nightmare..." Hamza learns that his closest friend Mahmood and Mahmood's son have been martyred and that his son-in-law Yazan is missing. He resolves to stay strong for the family.

[17-21] TENDING WOUNDS; SAFIYYA'S STORY. Hamza takes a piece of cloth from a bag and tries to treat Sahar's arm. Fathimaa wipes her tears and helps treat Sahar's and Safiyya's injuries. Safiyya and Samaa tell their story, Safiyya sobbing: soldiers killed Samaa's father, broke into their house and committed "crimes" (ޖަރީމާތައް, not specified). Mother and daughter got a small chance, jumped over the wall and escaped. Meanwhile Ameen tries to calm Sahar.

[21-28] AMEEN COMFORTS SAHAR. Ameen massages Sahar's head: "God willing, Yazan bebe will come... I'm here with you, dhaththa... I'll look after you." He cries too, and tells her to sleep because she is exhausted. Sahar is lying down with a hand over her face: "How can I sleep... when I close my eyes I see them torturing Yazan... I don't know what state that poor man is in." She goes on: "Kokko, how will I live without Yazan? We grew up together since childhood... every memory of mine is tied to him... such a loving, kind, good husband... I will miss him so much, kokko!" (the last phrase is in English). Ameen: "Bebe will come, very soon, he is strong, inshaAllah, don't lose hope." He hugs her. Worn out, Sahar falls asleep.

[29-39] WAKING; FATHIMAA EXPLAINS. Time passes. Fathimaa wakes Sahar while Ameen is eating a piece of bread. Fathimaa offers food. Laila is not there. Fathimaa says "Mother" (Laila) went outside to relieve herself, and "Safiyya dhaththa" went with her. Fathimaa hands Sahar bread and a cup of water. Sahar asks whether they had time to prepare, since there is food and water in the cave. Fathimaa, head bowed, explains: Hamza had been following the Jewish fighters' moves closely, listening to the radio and reading books, newspapers and magazines. At the market he bought lots of food, saying they might have to leave the area and must always be careful. He was always sad that Mahmood would not believe him. Mahmood called it a misjudgement and said "they won't come and overpower us". Hamza is grief-stricken now that Mahmood is martyred. This morning Hamza came back from the Fajr prayer and said things were bad: he had seen men coming with guns. He told them to get ready and go fetch Sahar's household. The car was ready, but as they were about to leave, gunfire broke out from the direction of Sahar's house. As they climbed the mountain to flee, Hamza said: "If my Sahar is safe, she'll come this way." Laila, Safiyya and Samaa come back into the cave as Fathimaa finishes.

[40-45] HAMZA: WE MUST LEAVE. Hamza follows them in. In a low voice he says: "Everyone get ready! There's no shelter left for us here. The whole area is burning to ashes. We'll escape over the mountain, we must be gone before sunrise... there's no safety here." He cries. Sahar, hugging Laila: "We'll never see Yazan again, will we? Every time I think of the humiliation and torture those evil men will put him through, my heart trembles." Laila: "Be strong, my child. This is what was decreed for today. We must be patient. You are my hope, my greatest strength to live, a mercy from Allah. Whatever happens, I'll be with you."

[46-52] NIGHT ESCAPE. Hamza waits until midnight, when he knows the fighters will be less active. He knows the paths from the mountain into the dense forest. The route is dangerous. He tells everyone to be patient and draw strength from dua and dhikr. They make fire by rubbing/heating two sticks of wood and light the hand-lamps (އަތްބައްތި) Hamza brought in a bag. Hamza leads everyone out of the cave. They are all afraid of the long walk on foot. They move slowly along a path of big rocks and thorny trees. Hamza's goal is to reach Ein Karem.

[53-65] PARALLEL: YAZAN IN CAPTIVITY (night to sunrise). Voices wake Yazan. His captors speak a language he does not know. Exhausted, he begs for a drop of water. He lies beside a fire in the middle of an open field with a chain around his neck, tied up "like a wild dog". He prays without stopping for rescue from these cruel men and for his beloved wife and mother to be kept safe. After he asks many times for water, one soldier comes over and urinates on his face (ކުޑަކަމު ދިޔައެވެ), says in English that he should "be full of water now", and walks off. Even so, Yazan does not miss his prayer. His hands and feet are not tied, so with great difficulty he does tayammum and prays Fajr sitting down. Soldiers keep coming over to harass him during the prayer. The sun rises. For "breakfast" they bring him two olives and a drop of water in a bottle cap. They then bring an iron cage of the kind used to carry animals such as lions, put Yazan inside, and load it onto a big lorry. The neck chain comes off, which is a small relief. He is anxious about what lies ahead but trusts that Allah decrees no harm for His servants.

[66-72] THE FOREST; ARRIVAL AT EIN KAREM. Animal sounds come from the dense trees and the darkness unsettles them. The group stays together, picks out and avoids the thorny trees, and walks very slowly. They reach Ein Karem near Fajr time. The village is asleep and silent. Nervously they knock at the first house they see, and after a while the door opens. Ein Karem is very close to Deir Yassin and news of the attack has already reached it, so the household sees at once that these are war refugees. They help immediately without asking questions, giving water and dates and telling them to calm down and rest. The house belongs to a very kind couple.

[73-83] THE COUPLE'S HOUSE; SAHAR'S DAWN. Only the middle-aged couple live there. Hamza and Ameen get one room and all the women (Sahar, Fathimaa, Laila, Safiyya, Samaa) get another. The women's room is beautiful, spacious and modern, with religious emblems/symbols (ދީނީ ޝިޢާރުތައް) hung in several places. Everyone lies down. Sahar cannot sleep: whenever she closes her eyes she sees yesterday's scenes. The memories of Yazan hurt most: his passionate caresses and kind words, his manly body, thin handsome beard and intense eyes. She turns to dua, strengthening her faith and renewing her hope of meeting her husband again. Tossing and turning, she hears the Fajr adhan. Laila had been sobbing earlier but is now asleep. Sahar quietly gets up, makes wudu in the bathroom, prays Fajr, and spends the time until sunrise in dua and dhikr. Everyone else sleeps. Her eyes are swollen from crying. Feeling a great heaviness, she takes the burqa (head covering) off her head, lies down beside Laila and soon falls asleep. END.
