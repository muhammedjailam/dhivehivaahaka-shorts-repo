SAHAR (ސަހަރް) - EPISODE 574 - READER SYNOPSIS
Transcript: 71 segments, approx. 0:00-10:39 (639 s).

=====================================================================
1. SETTING
=====================================================================
Period: Spring 1948, British Mandate Palestine, in the weeks right after the
Deir Yassin massacre (9 April 1948) and BEFORE the State of Israel is declared
(seg 22 speaks of "the state they are trying to establish"). So roughly
mid-April to early May 1948. Season: spring.

Two parallel storylines that cut back and forth:

A) LAND / FAMILY THREAD (segs 1-14, 36-71)
   - Hashim's family home in or near Ein Karem (seg 40 names the road "from
     Ein Karem into Jerusalem City" as the route out), a village west of
     Jerusalem near Deir Yassin.
   - Night-time escape by truck through the Jerusalem area, past checkpoints,
     to a place called "Ramadha" (ރަމާޟާ) at dawn, and on to the Jordanian
     border.
   - Time of day: evening after dinner (1-5); preparation over several hours,
     ready by 8 pm (7); house locked, departure near 3 am (36-39); dark night
     drive (43-52); Fajr call to prayer at dawn (53-55); reach Jordan border
     at 6 am, early morning, before/around sunrise (56-60); morning, about
     half an hour later, permission granted (61-64); morning drive into
     Jordan (65-71).

B) SEA / YAZAN THREAD (segs 15-35)
   - A French cruise-type ship ("boat") that sails round the world every year,
     diverted at England's request to carry about 30 Palestinian prisoners to
     France. Yazan is in a cabin with a balcony (two chairs on the balcony).
   - Time of day not stated. Weather changes: it starts to rain and the sea
     becomes rough (23), a storm seems to be coming (24), the ship rocks side
     to side (31-32). France is about five days away (25).

2. SYNOPSIS (scene by scene)
=====================================================================
[1-5] Hashim's house, evening, after dinner. Hashim says they cannot stay;
the enemy will target them next and would not hesitate to kill them all if
they fall into their hands. Sahar, near tears, asks whether they will then kill
her husband (Yazan). Hashim comforts her: "No, my child (ދަރިފުޅާ)! God
willing your husband will be safe." Safoora, collecting the empty plates,
says there is no reason to delay: they will leave tonight together with "our
guests" (Sahar's party). Everyone agrees and hurries to pack what they can
into boxes. Present: Hashim, Sahar, Safoora, (Hamza, Ameen implied).

[6] Hashim and Hamza go to check the truck arranged for the journey and pump
air into the tyres.

[7-8] Hours later, 8 pm: everything movable from Hashim's house is loaded on
the truck; only a small space is left for people. Many others in the area
have already begun leaving.

[9-10] Narration: after Deir Yassin, everyone fears their area will be
overrun. People leave their homes under compulsion, with no destination; some
are leaving on foot.

[11-14] Hamza asks what people without passports will do; will Jordan let
them in? Hashim reassures him: a friend of his son Abdullah works in Jordanian
immigration and told them to go into a phone booth near the border and call.
Hashim knew people fleeing suddenly would have no passports, so he asked "that
boy" what to do. He tells it all without letting anyone ask more questions.

[15-19] CUT TO THE SHIP. On the balcony of Yazan's cabin, a young woman sits
in one of two chairs and asks Yazan to sit in the other. She introduces
herself as Claire, a crew member. The ship leaves France every year to sail
around the world; on its way back this time it was brought to this region at
England's request. Only after arriving were they told it would take prisoners
to France; after talking with French authorities the crew learned what was
going on. There are about thirty prisoners aboard; only Yazan was brought from
Deir Yassin, the rest are from the Jerusalem area.

[20-22] Claire tells all this looking at Yazan's face. Despairing, Yazan asks,
"Why me? Why didn't they kill me?" Claire: "Don't talk like that, Yazan! As far
as I know they tried to kill all the prisoners, but because Saudi Arabia warned
it would not let them establish the state they are trying to establish, we
believe you too were released without being killed."

[23-26] It starts to rain and the sea grows rough. Yazan and Claire go inside
the cabin and close the balcony door. Claire, smiling, says she must go, a
storm seems to be coming; the restaurants are on the first deck, he should not
stay hungry. Yazan asks how long to France: about five days. She tells him to
go out and look around the ship, and that she will come to see him when the sea
calms; she has to leave without telling all the things she came to tell. She
closes the cabin door as she leaves.

[27-31] Yazan alone. Many questions: why does this girl pity him, why did she
come to his room and tell him all this? He resolves to proceed wisely; the
days ahead will be safe only if he acts carefully. Like a man possessed he
talks to himself: "With God's mercy I will find my womenfolk (family)... I
will make any sacrifice for it... I will still get my Sahar back."

[31-35] The sea gets rougher; the ship rocks side to side. Yazan gets a
headache, feels nauseous and vomits (seasick). He tries to sleep until the sea
calms. He has never travelled by sea and is anxious and frightened, but as a
brave young man he trusts in God and spends the time making du'a for safe days
ahead and for reaching a shore of safety.

[36-38] BACK TO LAND, near 3 am. Hashim locks the house door "forever" and
freezes. Holding Safoora's hand he says he thought he would die here, but
because of evil people they must leave their own homeland. Safoora, sad
herself, encourages him: this too is God's mercy; they must be patient; this
is far better than falling into the hands of "these Jews" (ޔަހޫދީން).

[39-42] Everyone boards the truck; they set off for Jordan near 3 am. Reason:
on the road from Ein Karem into Jerusalem City the fighters/soldiers
(ހަނގުރާމަވެރިން) are celebrating, and Hashim is sure that by this hour of the
night they will have wound down: "They drink alcohol and whatever else; drunk,
they'll be finished by now. If we drive past, they won't notice."

[43-48] Everyone is frightened on the trip, expected to take about three
hours. Sahar, sitting at one edge of the back of the truck, thinks of Yazan:
if he were here he would keep her spirits up and comfort her with stories;
what a great trial she faces. She wipes her tears. Ameen, sitting next to her,
asks, "Sister (ދައްތަ), are you crying?" She says she is remembering Yazan and
doesn't know what state the poor man is in. Not knowing what to say, Ameen
hugs her and sits holding her; though heartbroken himself, he stays strong for
his sister.

[49-52] They drive through the Jerusalem area. Hashim was right: the fighters
are so drunk they don't know what is happening. Checkpoints are set up but the
celebrating men can't even stand upright. No checkpoint tries to stop them.
Potholed roads; a feeling of insecurity. Lights in some houses, but no human
movement. No one can sleep.

[53-55] Frightened, Sahar does dhikr. Entering the "Ramadha" area, the Fajr
adhan is heard. The truck pulls over at the roadside and everyone prays inside
the truck; they are too afraid to get off. They had all left home with wudu
for this reason.

[56-58] After Fajr they push on quickly, hoping to cross the Jordanian border
area before sunrise. Everyone is amazed at Hashim's driving skill, keeping the
truck balanced on rough, potholed, rocky roads he knows from experience. Driving
fast, they reach the Jordanian border at 6 o'clock.

[59-62] Not a big queue, but they must wait a while. Hashim, exhaling deeply:
in daytime the fighters would be here, and crossing would be hard. He stops the
truck and gets out, calls his son Abdullah, completes the immigration
formalities, and returns about half an hour later with documents from
immigration.

[62-64] Hamza, worried, asks if all is settled. Hashim: "Hamza, how anxious you
are! We've been given permission. Everyone rejoice; praise and thank God."
Tears of joy from everyone.

[65-71] Driving on, Hashim explains next steps: Abdullah says Jordan grants
citizenship to refugees from Palestine. Their land, houses and belongings are
being looted and burned to ashes by the oppressors; better to live under the
protection of a Muslim nation. They should apply quickly, before the enemy
pressures Jordan to hand over all the refugees; if they fall into enemy hands
"they will end all our days." Everyone agrees and accepts deep down that there
is no other way. (Episode ends.)
