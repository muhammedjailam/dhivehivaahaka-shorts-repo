# Sahar (ސަހަރް) — Episode 574 story notes

## Synopsis
Spring 1948, a few weeks after Deir Yassin. Evening at Hashim's house in Ein Karem: Hashim says they cannot stay;
Sahar fears her husband Yazan will be killed. Safoora urges leaving tonight with their guests (Sahar's family). Hashim
and Hamza check the truck; by 8 p.m. it is loaded. Families are leaving the area, some on foot. Hamza worries about
passports; Hashim's son Abdullah has a friend in Jordanian immigration who told them to phone from the border.

Cut to the French ship: on Yazan's cabin balcony the crew member Claire explains that the ship, returning to France
from its yearly world voyage, was diverted at England's request to carry about 30 prisoners; only Yazan is from Deir
Yassin. "Why didn't they kill me?" — she says they meant to kill all the prisoners but Saudi Arabia's warning against
the planned state saved them. Rain and a storm drive them indoors; France is about five days away; she leaves. Yazan
wonders why she is kind, vows to find his family and Sahar, gets seasick, and spends the night in dua.

3 a.m.: Hashim locks his house forever and, holding Safoora's hand, grieves leaving his homeland; she consoles him.
They leave while the checkpoint fighters on the Ein Karem–Jerusalem road are drunk. In the truck bed Sahar weeps for
Yazan; her brother Ameen puts his arm round her. The checkpoints let them pass; nobody sleeps. At Ramadha the Fajr
adhan; they pray in the truck. Hashim races the rough roads and reaches the Jordanian border at 6 a.m., phones Abdullah,
returns with the entry papers; tears of joy. Driving on, he explains Jordan grants citizenship to Palestinian refugees;
their homes are being looted and burned; they accept there is no other way.
