# Taubaa — Episode 299: story notes

## Synopsis (English, internal only)
The narrator introduces a young man who fell prey to bad friends and spent years in a world of intoxicants, until an
incident woke him and he returned to his Lord. They met in a mosque in Riyadh, where he told his story.

He grew up in a very religious home in a Riyadh district; his father (may Allah have mercy on him) allowed no device of
entertainment into the house. At fourteen a group of bad friends waited for their chance and found it at exam time:
"white pills" to stay awake. He studied night after night without sleep, passed with very high marks, and kept taking
them until he was exhausted; then the same friends brought "red pills" to sleep. Too young to see the devils' plan, he
took about ten a day for three years or more, failed school and moved from school to school without a certificate.

As a last attempt he decided to move to his eldest brother's town. On a cold winter night he took his father's new car
without asking, red pills in his pocket, stopped with friends on the way and took a huge dose; a little before dawn he
drove off. Within minutes the world went dark: he woke in hospital, right leg broken, many wounds, 48 hours in the ICU —
his car had gone under a big lorry. Allah's mercy gave him a new chance, but it did not wake him. Moved to his father's
house in Riyadh, he kept buying pills from friends who came to his door. Recovering a little, one day at Asr, on a
crutch, he tried to stop passing cars, then hired a taxi to his brother's town. With his brother's help he entered a
middle school and got his certificate — but switched to alcohol, sold the red pills at double price for money, then
became hooked on hashish, going to school "like a fool", a timid coward, for about two years.

He lived alone in an isolated house at the edge of town. One afternoon two friends drove him around the streets for
hours; dropped back at his car he was so intoxicated he searched over two hours for his own house. Getting out of the car
he felt a violent pain in his heart; for the first time in years he remembered death — it seemed to stand before him.
He made wudu twice, prayed two rakats (al-Fatiha and al-Ikhlas), lay on his left side awaiting death, remembered that the
dying are laid on the right side and turned over, his whole body shaking while the pages of his sinful life flashed by.
Suddenly his foot moved — a light of hope. He drove to his brother's house, where the family had gathered for dinner, and
collapsed among them: "What happened?!" — "My heart hurts terribly." The nephew drove him to a private clinic; alcohol at
94%, the doctor refused and wanted the police, but agreed after pleading and money; ECG and treatment. His father, in
town, came, stood at his bedside, his chest tightened at the smell, and left without a word. The doctor warned him; he
left feeling he had a new life.

Afterwards every whiff of hashish reminded him of death and he put the cigarette out; at night he felt someone calling
"Get up!", woke trembling, remembered death, paradise, hell, the grave and two bad friends who had died. He began night
prayer and guarding the obligatory prayers for four months or more, until Allah sent a righteous young man who rescued
him from the bad people and took him to Makkah for umrah; he wept and begged forgiveness at the holy House. His advice to
Muslim youth: beware of bad friends. A closing dua for all sinners — Allah is Oft-Returning, Most Merciful. (The end.)

## Tone
Confessional and reflective: steel-blue, lonely night images for the years of addiction; cold fog and the ICU for the
crash; trembling lamplit darkness for the near-death night; warm amber/dawn-gold light as repentance takes hold, ending
in Makkah and a dawn dua.
