# Isq (ޢިޝްގު, "Love") — series bible

Dhivehi audio romance drama. This batch has six episodes that form ONE continuous arc, each picking up exactly where the
previous one stopped: **341 → 358 → 366 → 393 → 428 → 484**. The whole batch covers only about four days. Every
episode's cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is the same series poster (a man in a black suit between a woman in a
black dress and a young woman in a white dress with a flower, a dark city night window behind). The poster uses
non-Maldivian models with uncovered hair, so it is only an outfit/mood cue; the cards below are binding.

Look: warm romantic drama, rich golds and amber, soft lamplight and velvety shadows at night, luminous tropical daylight
on the island (see `style.txt`). Jaleel's Malé home is dark, modern and wealthy with gold lamps and city lights, like the poster.

## Places (keep them consistent within and across episodes)
- **Maura's family house** on an unnamed Maldivian local island: a two-storey house with pale onion-pink walls, a white
  gate (dhoraashi) in a coral-stone wall with magenta bougainvillea spilling over it, a sandy front yard with potted
  plants, a garden hose and a wooden joali seat. Upstairs, **Maura's bedroom**: pale-green and pale-grey walls, a white
  queen bed, a white four-door wardrobe, a white dressing table, a side table, and a glass **balcony door with a white
  curtain** opening onto a small balcony with a white railing that faces the street.
- **The guest house** — the island's best: a modern two-storey white building with dark-wood balconies directly
  ACROSS the sandy street from Maura's house; Jaleel's room has a balcony with a dark railing and a cream curtain at the
  open door. The two balconies face each other over a sandy street lit by streetlamps at night.
- **Nafeesa's house** (two doors down): an open sandy courtyard with a long wooden table and plastic chairs, a simple
  kitchen with a sink, a small fridge and a little door looking to the gate, a main room with a joali.
- **The island**: white sandy lanes, coral-stone walls, palms, breadfruit and fithuroanu trees, a small white mosque,
  a harbour/jetty, a small hospital with an outpatient pharmacy and a bench outside, a newly enlarged small airport,
  an empty white beach with joali seats hung under trees and a small open beach pavilion (holhuashi) with a tube light.
- **Jaleel's home in Malé**: a dark, luxurious modern home high above the city — floor-to-ceiling windows on Malé's
  night lights, gold lamps, black marble, a master bedroom with a balcony over a road, a living room with a cream sofa.

## Episodes
- **341** (pilot). NIGHT, Malé: Jaleel can't sleep beside his sleeping wife; he goes out to the balcony under moon and
  stars (narration: 28, rich, powerful, arranged marriage to a "perfect" wife, yet empty; he smokes — NOT shown).
  NIGHT, the island: Maura lands at the enlarged airport; pregnant Reema hugs her; Assad loads the boxes; drive home
  in the dark, teasing about boyfriends; family and neighbours at the gate, Zulfa's hug, old Nafeesa's jokes,
  bougainvillea, Maura greets her father Salaam ("Daddy's princess"); a huge dinner (bondibai, breadfruit rice,
  garudhiya, lonumirus) with laughter; her redecorated white room; she sleeps; wakes at 3 a.m., fetches water from the
  fridge, sleeps again.
- **358**. 3 a.m. water again → dawn birds, she checks the time on her phone, hurries to pray (prayer itself not
  shown). Malé: Jaleel leaves with a suitcase; Shaaliya says goodbye lovingly (the hug is NOT shown — see rules); a man
  takes the suitcase. A speedboat arrives at the island jetty; council officials and businessmen welcome him; black
  sunglasses, two huge bodyguards; car to the guest house; he scolds staff on the phone (Farhaadh is fired). Maura at
  her gate with a coffee mug sees only his tall back. Zulfa watering plants explains: a Malé businessman for a project.
  Maura on the joali; Reema (due soon) walks with Maura to the hospital. Outside the pharmacy Maura collides with him
  (her head bumps his shoulder — NOT shown as contact), medicine bag drops; "s-sorry"; his POV: time stops, her white
  lace dress, he forgets his wife; he walks on. SUNSET: Maura in an orange dress on her balcony photographing red
  clouds, frangipani on her hijab, playful selfies; across the street Jaleel (just out of the shower — shown dressed,
  see rules) half-hidden by his curtain secretly photographs her: "Do fairies really live in this world?"
- **366**. Same sunset moment from both sides: Jaleel staring from his balcony; Maura turns, sees him across the
  street, flees inside, shuts her door, hand on her racing heart, smiles. NIGHT after Isha: Zulfa on the phone with
  Nafeesa (helper cancelled, ten Malé guests) — Maura comes along (orange dress); Nafeesa's courtyard: long table, girls
  setting chairs, Maura carries dish after dish (chicken curry, mung-bean curry, salad, rice, roshi, fried fish and
  chicken, desserts, dhufaa tray) and photographs the table; the guests eat; Jaleel keeps looking at her; Lamha teases
  "He's the famous businessman Yoosuf Jaleel"; Zulfa asks Maura to show "this gentleman" the sink; tissues; he asks her
  name, "Maura… a very beautiful name… you're even more beautiful… shyness is lovelier still… I like it very much";
  he leaves. Closing narration about her heart.
- **393**. Narration recap. 2 a.m., Maura sleepless, draws back her white curtain; Jaleel on the guest-house balcony
  frowning at his phone (cigarette — NOT shown); she hides, peeks, smiles; he notices the curtain move, goes in and
  switches off his light to watch secretly; she steps out disappointed; he watches her inside her room (laptop, chips)
  and feels peace for the first time. NOON, scorching sun: Maura carries a 5-litre jug of watermelon juice to Nafeesa's;
  pours into crystal glasses; Jaleel arrives with a group (white shirt, black jeans, sunglasses, hair slicked back);
  her hand shakes and juice spills on his shirt; everyone expects anger, he says "It's nothing"; she shows him the
  kitchen sink, tissues from the top of the fridge; "You're Maura, right?"; he confesses he photographed her at
  sunset without permission; hands in pockets: "I have something very big to tell you — will you let me?" (cliffhanger).
- **428**. Opens in that kitchen: someone approaches, he steps away, gives her a long look and leaves. NIGHT, Malé:
  Shaaliya finds Fauziyya on the sofa with a betel tray; "Has Jaleel called?" — Shaaliya lies "yes" (he hasn't called in
  three days); narration on the loveless arranged marriage. NIGHT, island, full moon: after Isha, Maura bored at the
  gate; walks to the empty beach, sits on a joali under a tree by the tube-lit holhuashi; Jaleel, leaving the mosque,
  recognises her and sits on another joali a little apart (black shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbows); small talk
  (business studies, back two days); his phone rings: Shaaliya — he sweats, watches the wind lift the end of Maura's
  hijab, cuts the call short ("I'm busy"); Shaaliya alone, heartbroken. He stands, "I'm going"… then turns: "Aren't you
  coming? The house is close." She walks over, smiling.
- **484**. The walk home in silence under the moon, the eve of Eid al-Adha; at her gate (his POV: why does his hard
  heart break its principles?); "Good night"; "Maura… Eid Mubarak" — the first Eid wish she gets. EID MORNING: golden
  sunrise, busy streets, young people's water-and-colour play at the corners; Maura and Zulfa go to Nafeesa's kitchen
  (three or four women cooking the delegation's Eid lunch: fish balls, curries, roshi, bondibai); Lamha bursts in
  splattered with colour and pulls Maura out to play (Nafeesa persuades Zulfa); under a fithuroanu tree a bag of
  colour-filled juice-pack bags; Ibbe's boys approach, Maura ducks into a quiet lane and aims at a young man's back —
  but at the junction the bag hits Jaleel (on the phone with a client): his white shirt and face splashed with colour;
  he turns furious — and melts at the sight of her (cliffhanger).

## Binding content rules for this series (override the literal narration)
1. **Jaleel is never shirtless, never in a towel or bathrobe.** The shower/towel moments (358 sunset, 366 opening) are
   shown with him in a plain white long-sleeved shirt and long trousers, hair damp, half-hidden by the curtain.
2. **No smoking, no cigarettes, no smoke, no lighter** (341 balcony, 393 balcony): his hands rest on the railing or
   hold his phone.
3. **Jaleel and Maura never touch** and always keep a clear arm's-length gap (write it into every two-shot). Kitchen /
   sink scenes (366, 393, 428 opening): he stands a respectful step away, hands in his pockets; she looks down holding
   tissues out at arm's length. The hospital collision (358): show the moment AFTER — she steps back holding her
   forehead, the paper medicine bag on the floor, him a step away with the bodyguards behind. The night walks (428/484):
   side by side with a clear gap on a moonlit sandy lane. Never framed as a couple in an intimate pose.
4. **Jaleel and Shaaliya are married**: no bed scene, no embrace. 341: Jaleel alone sitting on an armchair / standing
   at the dark bedroom's balcony door; Shaaliya asleep is NOT shown — leave her out of that beat (mention only). 358 farewell: they stand at arm's length at the door; she looks up at him
   lovingly with hands clasped, he holds the suitcase handle, stone-faced.
5. **Secret watching and photographing** (358, 366, 393): shown with dignity — his face of wonder half behind the
   curtain, the back of his phone; never framed on her body. Maura's room is seen only through her balcony doorway at
   a distance, fully dressed in hijab, SITTING (never lying) on the bed or at the dressing table with her laptop.
6. **Hijab always** (rule above). The narration's "long curly hair in the wind" (428) = the loose end of her hijab
   fluttering; "flower in her hair" (358) = frangipani pinned on her hijab. Orange dress = ankle-length. Jaleel's knee
   shorts (393) = long dark trousers.
7. **Family affection**: Maura may hug her sister Reema and her mother Zulfa; with her father Salaam she holds his
   hands, beaming. Old Nafeesa may pat her shoulder. Reema's pregnancy: a modest rounded bump under a loose dress.
8. **Juice spill** (393): the juice splashes on his shirt sleeve and front; nothing at his lap.
9. **Eid colour play** (484) is the Maldivian Eid water-and-colour game in island lanes: young people and children
   with buckets of water and small colour-filled juice-pack bags, girls with girls and boys with boys; no Holi imagery,
   no temples, no other religious symbols, no animal sacrifice. Jaleel's shirt stays fully opaque, splashed with bright
   pink and green colour; a few colour drops on his cheek — nothing like an injury.
10. **Prayer** is never depicted; a small white mosque exterior with men leaving after Isha is fine.
11. **No readable text anywhere**: phone screens, the photo on his phone (show only a soft glow / back of the phone),
    the airport, the plane (no airline logos), signs, the hospital, papers. No numbers.
12. **No alcohol, nightclubs, dogs, other faiths' symbols.** Betel/dhufaa tray = a small traditional lacquered tray.
13. **Bodyguards**: two very large men in black uniforms with empty hands; no weapons, no earpieces needed.
14. No music, no instruments.
