# Isq — Episode 358: story notes

## Synopsis
At 3 a.m. Maura wakes up thirsty, fetches a water bottle from the kitchen fridge and goes back to sleep. At dawn the
birds and koels wake her; she sits up, sees on her phone it is nearly 5:30 and hurries off to pray before sunrise
(prayer implied only). In Malé, Jaleel leaves his room pulling a suitcase; Shaaliya says a loving goodbye ("I'll miss
you... call me when you get there"); it is their first separation since the wedding. He nods, hands the suitcase to a
man at the door and makes a phone call. A speedboat cuts through the waves to the island harbour, where council leaders
and notable businessmen welcome him; he greets them without the slightest smile, in black sunglasses, flanked by two
huge men in black uniforms. The car races off and stops at the island's best guest house; he steps out furious on the
phone, scolding his staff for losing a project, and has Farhaadh fired. Maura, at her gate opposite with a coffee mug,
hears it all but sees only the tall man's back as he strides inside; something stirs in her heart. In the yard Zulfa,
watering the plants, explains: a famous Malé businessman here for a project. Light banter on the joali (coffee vs tea);
pregnant Reema comes out and the sisters walk to the hospital. Outside the pharmacy Maura, hurrying out, bumps into a
man; the medicine bag falls, she winces "ouch" with her hand on her forehead — and freezes: it is the angry man,
flanked by the bodyguards and island officials. For Jaleel time stops: her innocent face and white lace dress strike
his ice-hard heart; for a moment he even forgets he has a wife. "S-sorry," she says and steps aside; he nods and walks
on, longing to look back but not giving the people around him cause to gossip. Reema's feet ache, so Maura calls a
taxi. At SUNSET Maura, now in an orange dress, photographs the red clouds from her balcony, pins a flower on her hijab
and takes playful selfies. Across the street Jaleel, just out of the shower, watches half hidden behind his curtain,
secretly photographs her and gazes at the photo: "What beauty... Do fairies really live in this world?" (To be
continued.)
