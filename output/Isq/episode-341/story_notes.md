# Isq — Episode 341 (pilot) — story notes

## Synopsis
Duration 663.9 s (11 min 4 s). Two storylines on the same night, joined by a dissolve.

**Malé, late night (0–138 s).** Yoosuf Jaleel lies awake beside his sleeping wife Shaaliya, sits up, sighs, closes his
eyes, then gets up, slides open the balcony door and steps out into the cool damp breeze. Neither the breeze nor the
smiling moon ("queen of the night") calms him. Hands on the railing, he breathes deeply; the narration says he lights a
cigarette and stares at the road below. He is 28, married, holds money, wealth, power and influence — anything, any
girl, can be his with a snap of his fingers. Shaaliya is beautiful and good, his parents' perfect choice. So why does
something feel missing, why the emptiness, why a darkness ahead with no road visible? Stars glitter like pearls on a
black shawl; the moon lights the horizon.

**The island, night (138–664 s).** A plane lands on the island's enlarged runway. Maura puts her headset in her
handbag, pockets her phone and gets off, amazed at how the airport and runway have grown since she left years ago.
Pulling her luggage she sees her elder sister Reema, eight months pregnant, walking heavily towards her. "Dhontha!" —
they hug as well as the bump allows; Reema: "Maoo, how pretty and grown up you are!"; Maura: "Look at you, all puffed
up!" Reema's husband Assad appears behind. Mum (Zulfa) didn't come — she's making tea and has gathered the neighbours;
"your diet is going to be ruined". Assad has already loaded the boxes. In the car Reema explains the new member of
parliament has developed the island and points out places in the dark; the baby is due in a month and always kicking
(Assad laughs — Reema can't sleep). Reema teases: "Where's your boy?" — Maura: no one she likes; the earlier boy turned
out to be talking to other girls, lucky she found out before getting close.
At the white gate the household and neighbours are waiting. Zulfa hugs her first: "So happy you're moving back for
good — I've cooked bondibai, breadfruit rice, garudhiya, lonumirus!" Old Nafeesa pats her shoulder: "Grown up, fair,
Malé style… we old ones are withered sticks, nobody looks at us now" — everyone roars with laughter. Maura loves the
bougainvillea over the wall and the pale onion-pink walls. "Where's Dad?" — Salaam is right behind her: "Daddy!" —
"Daddy's princess," Reema teases; "Daddy's still my favourite," Maura sticks out her tongue. The neighbours leave,
promising to come tomorrow.
Dinner: Zulfa lays out dish after dish; Maura: "How can I eat all this?" Zulfa: "You'll eat — I picked breadfruit for
this rice!" Salaam: she's been at it since the afternoon. "I'll get fatter" — "Better plump than a skeleton." Laughter.
Over-full, Maura stops at her bedroom door; Reema reminds her they go to the hospital tomorrow. Her room has been
completely redone by her father (pale-green and pale-grey walls, white queen bed, four-door wardrobe, dressing table).
After a shower she sleeps quickly, wakes thirsty at 3 a.m., fetches a water bottle from the fridge, drinking as she
walks back, puts it on the side table and falls asleep again. "To be continued."
