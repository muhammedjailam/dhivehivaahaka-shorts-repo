# Isq — episode 366 — story notes

Duration: 611.0 s (10 min 11 s). 64 shots (segments), 26 beats, 24 new images + 2 in-episode reuses.

## Synopsis
Picks up at the exact sunset moment that ended 358, now seen from both balconies across the sandy street. Jaleel,
fresh from the shower, has stepped onto the guest-house balcony without noticing himself, staring at Maura: "Do fairies
really live in this world?" He even forgets he has a wife. Maura, turning with her phone, sees him across the street;
the narration describes him in a towel with water dripping (shown dressed, see below). Her throat goes dry, an
indescribable melody stirs in her; she can't hold his piercing gaze, hurries inside, shuts the balcony door, hand on her
racing heart, and smiles without knowing why.

Night, after Isha (prayer only mentioned): coming downstairs, Maura hears Zulfa on the phone in the kitchen with Nafeesa
— Nafeesa has to serve dinner to the delegation from Malé (ten people), her helper Vaafiraththa can't come, and she
wants Zulfa's help. Zulfa insists Maura come too ("you'll make friends"); Maura worries about making a mistake in front
of respected people. Zulfa switches off the kitchen light, they lock up and walk two doors down. In Nafeesa's sandy
courtyard two girls are setting chairs around a long wooden table; in the kitchen Nafeesa and a girl are dishing up
(Vadheefa also cancelled, the dhufaa tray not ready). Maura carries dish after dish to the table — chicken curry,
mung-bean curry, two-leaf salad, rice, roshi, fried fish and chicken, three desserts, cold water, the dhufaa tray — and
photographs it. The guests arrive; Maura stands apart watching for her mother's signals, her eyes on the young man from
the balcony, whose gaze keeps returning to her. The girl beside her (Lamha) teases: "Handsome, isn't he… he's the famous
businessman Yoosuf Jaleel." Maura: "What!!!" — she knew the name, never the face; why would such a man look at an
ordinary girl like her? Lamha introduces herself. Zulfa calls: "Show this gentleman the sink." Maura freezes under his
gaze but obeys; he follows, looking at her orange dress. At the sink he washes his hands; she holds out tissues and
stands a little away with her head bowed. "What is your name?" — "Maura." — "Maura… a very beautiful name… you are even
more beautiful… shyness is an even lovelier quality… I like it very much" (double meaning). He leaves without another
word. Closing narration: she has known men in Malé and has male friends, but no one ever cast such a spell; without
knowing what love is, Jaleel's name is settling in her heart. To be continued.
