# Isq — Episode 393: story notes

## Synopsis
The narrator recaps: Maura has known men in Malé and has male friends, but no one ever cast a spell on her like Jaleel; his
name is being engraved in her inexperienced heart. **2 a.m.**: sleepless, Maura sits up on her bed, drinks water, checks
the time and, on an impulse, draws back the white curtain of her balcony door. The sandy street is bright with streetlamps
and the guest house's light. Suddenly Jaleel steps out on the guest-house balcony opposite, frowning at his phone, his hand
on the railing (narration: T-shirt, knee shorts, a cigarette — shown as a dull-grey T-shirt, long dark trousers and only his
phone). Maura hides behind the curtain, then peeks and smiles; her heart is drawn to his firm manliness. He suddenly looks
up; startled, she lets go of the curtain and presses herself against the wall, hand on her chest, praying he didn't notice.
He did: a fold of her dress shows beside the curtain, and he smiles — "so she was watching secretly". Knowing she won't come
out while he stands there, he goes in, draws his curtain, switches off his light and waits in the dark. Her curtain lifts;
her fair face appears like the full moon; she steps out and looks at his room, visibly disappointed — so he wasn't the only
one who wanted to see. He watches her inside her room (phone, a packet of chips, a film on her laptop, the laptop light on
her face) and, for the first time in his restless business life, feels peace; he gives her an attention he never gave any
girl, not even his wife; her name is carved into his heart like a line cut in stone.
**Scorching noon**: Maura carries a five-litre jug of watermelon juice (sent by her mother) to Nafeesa's kitchen. Nafeesa,
hurrying with crystal glasses, asks her to pour the juice — the guests are about to arrive and Lamha has vanished. As she
finishes, the gate opens and a crowd of men enters; among them Jaleel in a white shirt, black jeans, black sunglasses and
slicked-back hair. Throat dry, she carries the tray out and shyly serves; at Jaleel her hand shakes and a glass spills on
him. "Sorry, sorry!" Everyone is shocked and expects his anger — but he says "It's nothing", no displeasure on his face, only
a sharp meaningful look; the others exchange disbelieving glances. She offers the kitchen sink; he follows her in; she
fetches tissues from the top of the fridge. He looks deeply at her anxious, moon-bright face; wiping his hands he reassures
her ("these things happen"), confirms her name, and asks her forgiveness: he photographed her without permission yesterday
at sunset on her balcony — "but I took it because I really wanted to". A sweet unnamed feeling takes root in her; she cannot
speak. "Maybe the spill was luck — otherwise I'd never have had this chance to talk to you." She notices the gentleness in
the voice of a man who is harsh with everyone. Hands in his pockets: "Maura… I have something very big to tell you. Will you
give me the chance?" Her mind fills with unanswered questions. To be continued.
