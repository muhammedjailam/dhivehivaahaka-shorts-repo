# Isq — episode 484: story notes

## Synopsis
Picks up from 428's last line ("Aren't you coming? The house is close."). On the eve of Eid al-Adha, Maura accepts
Jaleel's invitation and they walk home from the beach together under the full moon without a word, though new feelings
grow between them. At her gate she stops and looks at him; he gives her a long, meaningful look and the narration asks
why his hard, principled heart, which lived only for wealth, wants to break its own rules for this short, fair girl.
"Good night." — "Good night." Maura, seeing his steadiness and his respect for women, is sure there is a soft heart
inside his hard shell. As she walks in he calls "Maura... Eid Mubarak" — no smile, but closeness in his voice; he is
the first to wish her a happy Eid (she had forgotten tomorrow was Eid). She wishes him back.
Eid morning: a golden sunrise, busy lanes, young people's water-and-colour play at every corner, women shopping for
lunch. Maura and Zulfa go to Nafeesa's house, where Nafeesa, Vadheefa and helpers (three or four women) are cooking the
Malé delegation's Eid lunch (chicken curry, fish curry, fish balls, rice, roshi, bondibai). Zulfa throws herself into
it; Maura laughs at her mother's enthusiasm (she is the island's famous cook). Lamha bursts in, covered in colour and
out of breath: "Come and play colours! Ibbe's lot are there too." Maura looks to her mother; Nafeesa pleads for her;
Zulfa smiles: "Go, but be careful — Lamha, look after her." They run out into the lanes; under a fithuroanu tree Lamha
has a hidden bag of colour-filled juice-pack bags; she tells Maura to hit someone still clean, then runs off as Ibbe's
three boys approach. Maura ducks into a quiet lane, sees a young man with his back turned and throws — but Jaleel,
walking fast and ending a stern business call, steps into the four-way junction and the bag bursts on him: his white
shirt is drenched in colour, drops on his face. Maura goes pale; the young man runs and hides. Jaleel turns furiously
towards the thrower — and his anger melts at the sight of "the beautiful fairy" before him. (To be continued.)
