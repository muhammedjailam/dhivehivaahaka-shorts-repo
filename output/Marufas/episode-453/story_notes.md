# Marufas — Episode 453: story notes

## Synopsis
The morning after the night of terror (ep 430). At sunrise Saahidha is shattered: today is the first day since Yamna
turned seven that she has missed an obligatory prayer, and Saahidha's trust in her husband Khalid — whom she married at
fifteen — has collapsed after the jinn pointed at him. At 7 am, weeping on the big swing on the veranda, she answers a
call from her son Faarish in Malé (heard only as a voice). When he says "Dad", her face hardens: "He is not someone even
you should call Dad!" Faarish explains he phoned Ghassan himself: Ghassan believes the jinn lied — before reciting,
Ghassan checked Yamna's name and age and both parents' full names with Khalid, and asked Faarish afterwards whether
Khalid's details were correct; a father who wanted his daughter dead would not give true details or pay so much for a
ruqyah. Splitting a family is a jinn's trick. Faarish and his friend Adheel will come tomorrow. Saahidha is torn.
She makes tea for Ghassan and Khalid, then goes to call Yamna: the door is locked from inside; on Ghassan's advice she
knocks only twice and goes back. In the living room Ghassan sits deep in thought, Khalid at the end of the long sofa,
chin in hand. Ghassan checks the two boys will arrive before tonight's recitation and asks Saahidha (holding a broom)
for one of Yamna's dresses and a lock of her hair; women may not enter the recitation. By noon the door is still shut;
Saahidha tells Ghassan (refusing to speak to Khalid) — Khalid answers that he has the spare key and opens the door.
Everyone freezes: the wardrobe's clothes torn and flung around, the white walls covered in frightening marker drawings,
desk drawers pulled out, books and pens everywhere, the window pane smashed — and Yamna sitting on the study desk, legs
hanging (the narration describes her hair hacked short, nail marks on her face, sleeves torn off and bitten, bleeding
arms). Saahidha nearly collapses; Yamna mocks them ("this drama has only just begun!") and a heavy laugh fills the room.
Khalid carries Saahidha to their bed, runs back; Yamna: "I am not your child, don't call me that!" He brings the
first-aid box and, unexpectedly, is allowed to treat her — he thanks Allah. She has eaten nothing since yesterday; no
response. Ghassan comes out of her room holding a dress and a lock of her hair. Khalid revives Saahidha with water; she
sobs and beats his chest: "Why are you doing this to my child?" Weeping, Khalid swears by Allah he never harmed his own
child. Her doubts dissolve; she rests against him and sighs. Exhausted, they lie down and fall asleep — until Yamna's
door slams with a huge BANG; they leap up and run out: Yamna, not herself, marches with heavy steps towards the kitchen;
Ghassan comes out of his room too. (to be continued)
