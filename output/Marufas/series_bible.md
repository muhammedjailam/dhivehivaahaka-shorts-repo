# Marufas (މަރުފަސް) — series bible

Dhivehi audio HORROR drama. Seven episodes in this batch form ONE continuous arc, in this order:
**430 → 453 → 461 → 510 → 514 → 544 → 545** (episodes in between are not supplied, but each one opens where the
previous ends). Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is the same series poster: a woman in a dark hijab and abaya
seen from behind in a misty dark lane, a huge smoky shadow creature with long clawed fingers looming above her, leaves
at the edge; title in white. Match that look: dark, misty, charcoal and smoke-grey, one soft light source (see
`style.txt`). Captions use a deep crimson pill (`caption_style.json`).

## Places (copy these descriptions into `LOC` so all episodes match)
- **yamna_room** — Yamna's spacious bedroom in the family's large modern island house: white walls, polished white
  marble floor, a big bed with white sheets and a pale headboard, a study desk with books and pens under the window, a
  tall dark-wood wardrobe, a glass sliding window beside the bed with a sheer white curtain, a round ceiling light.
- **parents_room** — the parents' bedroom: dark-wood bed with cream bedding, a bedside lamp, a framed abstract
  picture, heavy curtains. (Never show the couple lying in bed — see rules.)
- **living** — the large formal living room ("beyrugey"): a royal-style cream sofa set with big gold-trimmed cushions,
  an Italian marble centre table with crystal vases and ornaments, a soft velvet rug, a big wall-mounted TV (screen
  dark/black), a glass display showcase, marble floor.
- **kitchen** — a large modern kitchen: carved dark-wood cabinets, a long countertop with a coffee machine and a
  blender, a big double-door steel fridge, a sink, red clay and steel bowls.
- **veranda** — the front veranda ("fendaa") with a big wooden swing bench (undhoali), potted plants, a sandy yard,
  flowering trees by the boundary wall.
- **island_lane** — white sandy lanes between coral-stone walls, palms and breadfruit trees, a few dim street lamps.
- **beach_night** — the Rannikamagu beach at 3 am: dark lagoon, faint moonlight, a huge old beach tree whose shadow
  falls on the sand.
- **abandoned_house** — a long-abandoned coral-stone house in a lonely overgrown corner of the island: broken wooden
  shutters, peeling walls, dust, cobwebs, a dark back room with a single old wooden chair; reached through scrub.
- **faarish_room** — Faarish's room at home: a king-size bed with dark cushions, a large wall clock WITH NO NUMBERS, a
  dim bedside lamp.
- **hospital** — the island hospital: emergency room with curtained beds; ICU corridor with a large glass window into
  Yamna's room (monitors showing abstract green lines, NO numbers), plastic chairs along the corridor; Yamna's ward
  room later.
- **mosque** (exterior only) — a small white island mosque at dawn/dusk.

## Episodes
- **430** — Night. In Yamna's marble bedroom a terrible deep voice comes out of her ("Do you think what you're doing
  is so clever?"); her body arches on the bed, eyes blood-red, foam, a scream; the room turns ice-cold; the bulbs go
  out. Mother Saahidha cries to Khalid to fetch Saeed. Saeed comes, recites Quran and a short ruqyah; Yamna screams
  louder, convulses, then collapses asleep. Saeed: "This is not ordinary — show her to a proper raqi." Saahidha and
  Khalid sit by her bed; Saahidha strokes her forehead: maybe last week's fever? Khalid remembers Yamna's odd behaviour
  for two weeks: always ill, bringing flowers from the garden into the house at sunset. Fajr adhan; Khalid goes to pray.
  Morning: Yamna wakes aching, remembers nothing ("allergy from takeaway food"); she has an important test; in school
  uniform she drinks tea, leaves laughing with friends on the lane; Saahidha watches, relieved. Sunset: the sky
  reddens, Yamna's smile vanishes, she convulses worse than before, a heavy male voice screams. Khalid ("Saeed was
  right") phones a famous raqi and sends a launch. Near midnight **Ghassan** arrives (black trousers, white shirt, big
  black bag), whispers privately with Khalid, puts his palm on Yamna's forehead and recites. Yamna thrashes, pushes
  Saahidha away, rakes her own cheeks with her nails (blood), laughs in a man's voice: "Stop reciting!" Ghassan
  questions the jinn: who sent you? The jinn: "One of you — you handed her to us!" and points at **Khalid**. Saahidha,
  stunned, hugs Yamna and sobs. The jinn threatens worse; Ghassan, sensing he must prepare more, stops. Saahidha glares
  at Khalid with hatred: "Get out!" Khalid and Saeed leave. At fajr Saahidha wakes Yamna to pray; Yamna sits up
  furious: "What prayer? Go do your own thing — get out of here!" Saahidha leaves the room shaking. (to be continued)
- **453** — Sunrise. Saahidha is shattered: first day since age 7 that Yamna has skipped a prayer; she no longer trusts
  Khalid. 7 am, crying on the big swing on the veranda; phone rings: son **Faarish** in Malé. She says don't even call
  him "father". Faarish says he phoned Ghassan himself: Ghassan believes the jinn LIED (Khalid gave all the correct
  names and details — why would a man who wanted his sister dead do that and pay so much?); splitting families is a
  jinn's trick. Faarish and his friend Adheel will come tomorrow. Saahidha makes breakfast tea for Ghassan. Yamna's door
  is locked from inside; she knocks twice (Ghassan advised not to push her) and goes to the kitchen. In the living room
  Ghassan sits deep in thought on a single sofa; Khalid at the end of the long sofa, chin in hand. Ghassan: will the two
  boys arrive by tonight? He asks Saahidha (sweeping with a broom) for a dress of Yamna's and a lock of her hair; women
  may not enter during the recitation. By noon the door is still shut; Khalid fetches the spare key and opens it:
  the wardrobe clothes are torn and flung about, the white walls covered with frightening marker drawings, the desk
  drawers pulled out, books and pens everywhere, the glass window panel smashed — and Yamna sitting on the study desk
  with her legs hanging, her long hair cut short and ragged, deep scratch marks on her face, her sleeves torn off and
  her arms bitten and bleeding. Saahidha nearly collapses. Yamna mocks them; a heavy laugh. Khalid carries Saahidha to
  their room and lays her on the bed, runs back; Yamna: "I'm not your child, don't call me that!" Khalid brings the
  first-aid box and, unexpectedly, is allowed to treat her wounds — he thanks Allah. She hasn't eaten since yesterday;
  no response. Ghassan comes out of her room holding a dress and a lock of hair. Khalid sprinkles water on Saahidha's
  face and pats her cheek; she wakes, sobs, beats on his chest: "Why are you doing this to our daughter?" Khalid,
  weeping, swears by Allah he never harmed his own child. Her doubts dissolve; she rests her head against him. Exhausted,
  they lie down and fall asleep — but suddenly Yamna's door slams with a huge bang; they jump up and run out: Yamna,
  not herself, marches towards the kitchen; Ghassan comes out of his room too. (to be continued)
- **461** — In the kitchen Yamna yanks open the freezer, pulls out a whole frozen fish and starts eating it raw; the
  men try to hold her but she flings them off with inhuman strength; she finishes it, her eyes roll up, red-tinged foam,
  and she collapses on the floor. At that moment **Faarish** arrives from Malé, horrified: "Father, to the hospital
  now — food poisoning!" Before the ambulance she vomits everything. At hospital she gets first aid and is sent home,
  strangely calm, lifeless as a doll. Heavy, ominous sunset. Saahidha bathes her, makes her pretty, feeds her — the only
  calm moment in 24 hours. After isha the family prepares for Ghassan's midnight recitation: Khalid, Faarish and
  **Adheel** go in with him. Saahidha hugs Yamna tightly, praying, then leaves in tears, closes the door and slides
  down to sit on the floor outside, back against the door, knees up. Inside: lights dimmed, Ghassan sits by Yamna's
  head murmuring unintelligible commands; Yamna moans, then the heavy voice screams; Saahidha covers her ears; sounds of
  things breaking and the men holding Yamna down; icy cold seeps under the door. Saahidha, forehead on the door, chin
  raised, prays silently. Inside, Yamna (tied to the bed) convulses, eyes rolling; Ghassan demands: "Who are you? Who
  sent you?" The jinn: "I needn't answer you, wretch!" Ghassan threatens; she screams and her body rises off the bed;
  then the jinn begs "don't hurt me!" — and finally names **"the old man of this island, Aadhanbe"**. Khalid, Faarish and
  Adheel exchange looks of disgust — the jinn lied before. Faarish: "Liar!" Saahidha outside thinks the same: the jinn
  wants to sow suspicion and blame an innocent old man. Ghassan subdues it with another warning; Yamna shivers and
  faints; the cold fades. Khalid covers her with a blanket and asks Faarish to open the door; Saahidha rushes in,
  hugs her, weeps. Yamna opens her eyes, looks from face to face, expressionless — finally her gaze stops on Ghassan,
  and a wide smile spreads across her face. (to be continued)
- **510** — About a month later. Night after night Ghassan recites, gives Yamna white cloths drawn with strange
  letters and "blown-on" water; nothing improves — he is no ordinary fraud: he has won the family's total trust. He
  announces the final, most dangerous stage: he must go into the room ALONE (the jinn draws strength from the family).
  3 am, eerie cold: Ghassan takes his secret black bag into Yamna's room and locks the door. The family waits on the
  living-room sofas, hearts pounding. Silence, then Yamna's own innocent voice screaming: "Don't do this! Please...
  Mother! Father! Save me!" — they think it is her battle against the jinn. After an hour it goes quiet. Ghassan comes
  out drenched in sweat, panting, a red scratch on his cheek: "Alone it fought hard... it will get lighter each night."
  Khalid opens the door: Yamna sleeps peacefully on her back, dried tears on her cheeks, a white blanket to her neck,
  hair neat; Saahidha weeps with joy. Khalid tells Faarish to take Adheel to the mosque for fajr, puts his arm round
  Saahidha and goes to rest with a peace he hasn't felt in months. TRUTH (narrator): the "peace" is Ghassan's sorcery;
  on some days Yamna is silent, voiceless, a prisoner of Ghassan's ifreet — she wants to scream to her mother that every
  night Ghassan, through his ifreet, robs her honour, but her own body has been made a silent prison; silent tears. On
  the other days she is a different creature: foul words, self-harm (biting, scratching, banging her head), eating
  rubbish and live cockroaches, never bathing, a stench — those days the sea jinn (maarid) is in control, stronger than
  Ghassan's ifreet; Ghassan's nightly visits are to strengthen his ifreet to take full control. Noon: Khalid and Ghassan
  come home after the dhuhr prayer for lunch (fried fish on the table); Khalid met Saeed outside his shop: Saeed asked
  about "kamana" and wants to learn shar'i ruqyah — may he sit in on a recitation? Khalid looks at Ghassan for
  permission. (to be continued)
- **514** — Ghassan, displeased: nobody on the island must know about Yamna's progress (the sorcerer would make the
  magic stronger); sneers "Madinah University... they think he's so great!" — he wants Saeed, a real scholar, kept
  away. After lunch: "Tomorrow night is a big night — everyone in the room; and send another 20,000 rufiyaa to my
  account." The family keeps paying, sacrificing everything for "kamana". Next night after isha and dinner the session
  starts; Yamna convulses, eyes red; the maarid's heavy voice — but this time it laughs and EXPOSES Ghassan: "You're
  blind! This cheat handed this body to his ifreet; every night he goes in alone and his ifreet violates her!" Khalid
  and Faarish freeze; Ghassan recites faster: "A jinn's lie to divide us!" They believe him. The jinn mocks: "You didn't
  believe me about Aadhanbe either — go now to Rannikamagu beach: he is there with a bowl of sihr water ready to pour
  into the sea!" Silence; Ghassan looks startled and afraid. Faarish and Adheel run out into the 3 am darkness to the
  beach. Under a huge beach tree in faint moonlight a figure sits on the sand: **Aadhanbe** in a checked white sarong,
  white shirt, white cap, muttering over a strange bowl. "Stop!" He turns, startled; the two young men grab him; the
  bowl drops and the black water soaks into the sand. Faarish is now 100% sure. Adheel gags him with his own cap; they
  drag him to a long-abandoned house far from people, tie him to a chair with a rope. Faarish: "Why are you doing sihr
  on my sister? Who told you?" Aadhanbe, trembling, crying: "For Allah's sake let me go — I never did sihr on anyone's
  child!" Faarish: "You lie in the first row of the mosque five times a day..." Adheel, who saw Yamna's state, thinks no
  sin in anything done to such a sorcerer. Aadhanbe swears: "Jaleel asked me to do something so fish (bait) would come
  to the island — that's why I was on the beach!" Faarish, in rage, remembering his little sister, picks up a stick and
  strikes him hard; the old man screams; Adheel gags him again. "Bro, let's go — we must check on the others." They
  leave him there and slip home through the dark. Ghassan, just finishing, comes out looking worried: "Any proof?"
  Faarish: "Caught red-handed!" Khalid and Saahidha come out furious; Saahidha: "Killing such merciless people is no
  sin!" Khalid: "Where is Aadhanu now?" Faarish lies: "Exposed, he fled screaming." His heart pounds. (to be continued)
- **544** — Dim room: Faarish and Adheel lie on Faarish's king-size bed whispering about what to do with Aadhanbe; if
  it gets out, the island will explode. Adheel: can't keep him there forever; can't send him away (police); they will
  frighten him tomorrow at sunrise — claim they have proof of his sorcery — so he keeps quiet. Lights off; they try to
  sleep. Next morning: golden light but a heavy silence; Saahidha sits head bowed on the living-room sofa, eyes red and
  swollen, untouched tea steaming; Khalid comes out slowly, sleepless, sits beside her and lays a hand on her shoulder;
  their eyes meet. Suddenly Yamna bursts out of her room — wild hair, red swollen eyes, trembling with rage: "Get away!
  Everyone get away!" She hurls the crystal vases and ornaments from the Italian marble table at the wall, smashes the
  big TV, breaks the showcase glass with her hands (bleeding, feeling nothing), rips the sofa cushions — the beautiful
  room is wrecked. "Kamana! My child!" Saahidha tries to hug her; Yamna shoves her to the floor; Khalid can't hold her;
  she runs to the kitchen — on these days she craves raw meat; the family no longer keeps raw fish. She tears open the
  double-door fridge, smashes vegetables, fruit, bottles; throws the coffee machine and blender down, steel and red-clay
  bowls clatter; opens every cabinet; stops at the bin under the sink: the fish guts thrown there in the morning; a
  terrible smile — she eats the raw guts with blood running down her chin. Saahidha retches, weeps, sinks to the floor;
  the men try to pull her away; she shoves them all. Soon food poisoning: stomach pain, her face darkens, vomiting; the
  power leaves her; they rush her to hospital, emergency room, critical. In the corridor Faarish's grief turns to rage:
  Aadhanbe is the root of all this. Red-eyed, he grips Adheel's arm: "Let's go — I won't let that old man live today."
  They stride to the abandoned house, fling the door open; Aadhanbe, tied to the chair, is terrified. Faarish grabs his
  shirt collar and punches him; Adheel threatens; blood from his face and mouth; he begs: "Faarish! Adheel! Don't do
  this to my old body... let me go!" Faarish only sees his sister fighting for life. Starved and beaten, the old man is
  finished; weeping, he begs: "I'm dying... give me a bite of food... even a drop of water..." (to be continued)
- **545** — The word "food" makes Faarish see Yamna eating the raw guts. "Food? Wait — today I'll give you something
  tasty." He goes home, scoops the raw guts Yamna left on the kitchen floor into a bag, returns and throws them before
  the old man: "Eat! This is what my sister ate because of you!" He tries to force it into his mouth; Aadhanbe, horrified,
  thrashes his head, smears it on his face, retches and vomits; they leave him half-dead, slam the door and go. At the
  hospital (they have showered, but their faces betray them): the ICU corridor; Saahidha sobbing on a chair, head
  bowed; Khalid pacing; through the glass, Yamna fighting for her life, many machines, a ventilator, the heart monitor
  racing and changing; doctors and nurses working round her bed; Faarish weeps. By afternoon the machines quiet, the
  monitor steadies; the poison is fading (her body was already weak from not eating properly). She wins — her parents
  weep with joy — but she opens her eyes silent, empty-eyed; Saahidha holds her hand and calls her; Yamna opens her mouth
  but no sound comes: her tongue is "locked", she is mute; a tear runs down her cheek. Faarish and Adheel enter the room:
  she lies like lifeless flesh, wasted, skin scarred, eyes sunken and dark-ringed; she looks at Faarish with tear-filled
  eyes, lips moving, desperate to tell him something — no sound. Faarish, sure Aadhanbe did this, shaking with rage,
  leaves; Adheel follows. Back at the abandoned house Aadhanbe lies half-conscious in his own filth and blood; Faarish
  lifts his face by the hair: "You made my sister unable to speak — after today you will never speak again!" Adheel
  pulls his head back and forces his mouth open; Faarish takes a sharp knife from his pocket and cuts out his tongue;
  a terrible scream; blood; they throw the knife down and leave him writhing in the chair, without looking back. Two
  days later Aadhanbe's family and the islanders begin searching; the police join, checking empty plots and abandoned
  houses; a police team enters the lonely house with flashlights, opens the room: a terrible smell — Aadhanbe dead,
  still tied to the chair, slumped, in a pool of dried black blood, his tongue beside him, eyes wide in agony. The
  police cordon the area; the news spreads; in the hospital room Faarish's face shows nothing — he just watches his
  silent sister. "Though he silenced the man who silenced his sister, the darkness over their lives will never lift."
  (to be continued)

## Binding content rules (override the literal narration)
1. **Yamna is a minor.** NEVER show her hurt, scratched, bitten, bleeding, with cut/uncovered hair, bare arms or torn
   sleeves, contorted, arched, levitating, foaming, with red/rolled-back eyes, vomiting, eating raw fish/guts/insects,
   tied to the bed, or being held down. She is always fully clothed in her long dress (or uniform / hospital gown) with
   the white hijab fully covering hair and neck; in bed she lies or sits under a blanket up to her chest/shoulders.
   Possession is shown through the ENVIRONMENT and REACTIONS: bulbs flickering or dead, a cold blue haze, a crumpled
   bedsheet, the curtain lifting with no wind, a dark smoky shadow with long shadowy fingers on the wall (like the cover,
   never a face or body, never touching anyone), the parents' terrified faces, Yamna as a dim silhouette sitting up in
   the dark with her face in shadow, or a calm close-up of her blank, still face (no marks) with a cold stare. Haggard
   look = pale, dark circles, tired eyes — no wounds, no scars.
2. **Ghassan's abuse (510, 514)** is NEVER implied visually: never show Ghassan alone with Yamna, never in her room at
   night alone. Show the locked bedroom door from the corridor, the family waiting on the sofas, his black bag, his sly
   face in the corridor light, Yamna asleep peacefully WITH her parents in frame.
3. **Aadhanbe (514, 544, 545)**: never show grabbing, gagging, dragging, binding/ropes, hitting, a stick or knife,
   blood, vomit, guts, the tongue, the corpse. Safe visuals: Aadhanbe sitting alone on an old wooden chair in the dark
   room, frightened, hands in his lap, unhurt and clean; Faarish's furious face half in shadow (at a distance from him);
   the two young men walking fast down a dark lane; the weathered closed door of the abandoned house; the bowl dropped
   on the sand with dark water soaking in; an EMPTY overturned chair in the dark room; police flashlight beams on the
   outside of the house at dusk; officers standing at the doorway with grim faces (inside not shown); islanders
   searching with torches. The beach capture = two young men approaching an old man under the tree, at a distance.
4. **Kitchen/raw-food scenes (461, 544)**: show the open freezer/fridge light and the family recoiling in horror,
   Yamna seen from BEHIND at the fridge (hijab, dress), or the aftermath: smashed plates, scattered vegetables and fruit,
   open cabinets, a fallen blender — no fish in her hands, no blood, no guts.
5. **Wrecked bedroom (453)**: show the room (torn clothes on the floor, scribbled dark abstract scrawls — no letters,
   no faces — on the white walls, scattered books, cracked window glass) and Yamna sitting on the desk fully clothed in
   her lilac dress and hijab, face in shadow, cold stare. Her hair and arms are never shown.
6. **Living-room destruction (544)**: shards of crystal on the velvet rug, a cracked dark TV, broken showcase glass,
   ripped cushions — with Yamna seen from behind or as a blurred figure; Saahidha on the floor in shock (not hurt).
7. **Recitation/ruqyah**: Saeed or Ghassan sitting/standing beside the bed reciting with a raised palm or cupped
   hands — no visible book text, no Quran pages with writing; Ghassan's palm may hover above (not touch) her forehead.
   His sorcery items: a black leather bag, plain dark clay bowls, plain white cloths — never symbols or letters.
8. **The married couple** Khalid & Saahidha may sit side by side; his hand on her shoulder at most; never lying in bed
   together, never embracing. 453's chest-beating → Saahidha crying into her hands in front of a weeping Khalid.
   Mother-daughter hugs are allowed (fully clothed). Father may lay a blanket over Yamna or touch her forehead.
9. Every woman and girl wears a hijab fully covering hair and neck in every shot, including in bed and at home.
10. Hospital: no needles, no blood, no tubes in the mouth; Yamna in bed with an oxygen nasal line at most, monitors
   with abstract green lines only, seen through the ICU glass. Doctors/nurses in scrubs, women in hijab.
11. Police: dark-navy uniforms and caps, flashlights only, no weapons, no badges or text, no tape with writing (a
   plain cordon line).
12. No text anywhere: wall clock without numerals, phones blank, TV black, no marker words on the walls, no money
   notes, no bank screens.
13. No music in the sound design; the adhan may only be implied by ambience (no synthesized voice).
14. The jinn/maarid/ifreet are NEVER shown as a figure with a face — only smoke, shadow, cold haze, flickering light;
   use it at most twice per episode.
