# Suratul Faatihaa - Episode 424 - story notes

## Synopsis
Duration 674.8 s (11:15), 70 shots. Continues verse 4 "Maliki yawmid-din": in this world Allah's dominion is seen only
from behind the veil of natural laws and cause and effect; on that Day the veils are torn away and some will wish they
were dust. Fear, but not despair: "ar-Rahman ar-Rahim" come first, you are still alive, the door of repentance is open,
the scales are not yet set up - the verse comes to wake you, not to destroy you. Zainul Abidin turned pale at wudu
because he knew Whom he would stand before. Knowing the three attributes makes the heart tremble in prayer; every step
is on a narrow bridge between infinite mercy and perfect justice; verses 1-4 together give the strongest station:
grateful, secure, awake.
New section "Worship and divine help" (214 s): verse 5 "Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in" - the heart of the Fatiha;
speech turns from "He" to "You"; the fronted object = "You alone" (tawhid); servitude to money / approval / desire;
self-examination questions; "na'budu" = we (the ummah, never alone); worship + help together (needy creatures), seeking
help breaks arrogance; the inner prayer (help me against my self, Shaytan, the world's trials, to worship perfectly);
worship before help = choose a direction first; the covenant; the verse sits at the exact centre of the seven verses
(Hadith Qudsi of the divided prayer). Verse 6 "Ihdinas-siratal-mustaqim" (532 s): why do praying Muslims still ask for
guidance? Guidance is not a one-time certificate but a lifelong journey on a road that may be slippery or dark;
"Ihdina" is plural; "Sirat" = a wide clear road, and elsewhere the bridge over Hell - how you walk here decides how you
cross there; "Mustaqim" = no leaning; human nature leans to excess, heedlessness, fear, arrogance, so we ask in every
rak'ah. Ends mid-verse 6 (continuation, no cliffhanger).

## Tone
Reflective, reverent, awe moving into hope. Opening: blinding white-gold hereafter symbolism, then warm dawn mercy;
the wudu story hazy and desaturated; the verse-5 body in lamplit mosque nights and dawns; verse 6 as roads and journeys,
ending on the Sirat and a straight-backed ruku' at dawn.
