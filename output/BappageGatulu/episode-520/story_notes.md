# Bappage Gatulu — Episode 520: story notes

Duration 585.7 s (9:46). 63 shots, 23 beats, 22 new images + 1 in-episode reuse.

## Synopsis
**2011 (0:00–5:46).** A stormy November night in Malé: black clouds, waves pounding the reef, empty streets. Eight-year-old
Iyaan sits at the sitting-room window watching the rain run down the glass, waiting for his father, MP Ahmed Zahir, an
honest politician who speaks out against corruption and has been receiving death threats. His mother Aminath wrings her
hands on the sofa watching the clock: it is eleven, Zahir is late. Iyaan asks why; she forces a smile, strokes his hair and
tells him to sleep; he refuses — Bappa promised to bring him a storybook. A car stops: Zahir's black car. Zahir gets out
of the back seat with a big briefcase, waves to the driver and walks toward the door. A motorbike roars up with its
headlight off, two men in black with covered faces; it stops in front of Zahir; the driver tries to get out; the pillion
rider attacks Zahir with a long blade, again and again, while Iyaan watches with his face pressed to the glass. The
briefcase falls, Zahir cries out, Iyaan screams "Bappa!", Aminath runs to the door, Iyaan throws it open; the bike speeds
away. Iyaan finds his father on the road, holds his face, begs him to open his eyes. Zahir, struggling to breathe, tries to
hold his son's hand and whispers "son… Mamma… look after…" — his last words. Aminath collapses weeping over him, the
trembling driver phones for an ambulance, neighbours look out of their windows. Iyaan's tears dry from shock; he stares at
his hands. A fire of revenge ignites: "Bappa, I will find the people who did this and take revenge." His innocence drowned
that night; his heart turned to stone.
**— "Fifteen years of silence" —**
**PRESENT (5:46–9:46).** Iyaan is 23, tall and strong, the country's most skilled investigative journalist at its biggest
newspaper, his articles making officials tremble; abroad he trained in martial arts and self-defence, and he is a hacker
with his own tracking network — all for one purpose: revenge on his father's killers. He lives with his mother in a small
apartment in Henveiru; she is frail, eyes faded with grief, always with her prayer beads. She asks if he's home; he sits
beside her and kisses her hands; she has taken her medicine; she begs him to be careful writing about corruption ("your
father too…"). He reassures her, but inside he weeps, remembering the day the police shelved the case as "unsolved" when he
was twelve — leaving the police station he lost all faith in justice and promised himself he would find it. That night he
locks his room: a big board with photos of the murder night and the MPs of that time, linked with red string, a big
question mark in the middle. On his laptop, police files he pulled from their secret database: key evidence and his
father's phone recordings removed — only possible with very high influence. He has identified the motorbike rider, later
reported dead of a drug overdose. Who ordered it? To be continued.
