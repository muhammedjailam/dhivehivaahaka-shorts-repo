## Synopsis
**2011 (0:00–6:10).** A stormy November night in Malé: black clouds, waves pounding the reef, empty streets. Eight-year-old
Iyaan sits at the sitting-room window watching the rain run down the glass, waiting for his father, MP Ahmed Zahir, an
honest politician who speaks out against corruption and has been receiving threats. His mother Aminath wrings her hands
on the sofa watching the clock: it is eleven, Zahir is late. Iyaan asks why; she forces a smile, strokes his hair and
tells him to sleep; he refuses — Bappa promised to bring him a storybook. A car stops: Zahir's black car. Zahir gets out
of the back seat with a big briefcase, waves to the driver and walks toward the door. A motorbike roars up with its
headlight off, two men in black with covered faces; it stops in front of Zahir; the driver tries to get out; the pillion
rider attacks Zahir with a long blade, again and again, while Iyaan watches with his face pressed to the glass. The
briefcase falls, Zahir cries out, Iyaan screams "Bappa!", Aminath runs to the door, Iyaan throws it open; the bike speeds
away. Iyaan finds his father on the road, holds his face, begs him to open his eyes. Zahir, struggling to breathe, tries
to hold his son's hand and whispers "son… Mamma… look after…" — his last words. Aminath collapses weeping, the trembling
driver phones for an ambulance, neighbours look out of their windows. Iyaan's tears dry from shock; he stares at his
hands. A fire of revenge ignites: "Bappa, I will find the people who did this and take revenge." His innocence drowned
that night; his heart turned to stone.
**— "Fifteen years of silence" —**
**PRESENT (6:10–10:47).** Iyaan is 23, tall and strong, the most skilled investigative journalist at one of the country's
big newspapers; abroad he trained in martial arts and self-defence, and he is a hacker with his own tracking network —
all for one purpose: revenge on his father's killers. He lives with his mother in a small apartment in Henveiru; she is
frail, her eyes faded with grief, always with her prayer beads. She asks if he's home; he sits beside her and kisses her
hands, asks about her medicine; she begs him to be careful writing about corruption ("your father too…"). He reassures
her, but inside he weeps, remembering the day the police shelved the case as unsolved when he was twelve. That night he
locks his room: a big board with photos of the murder night and the MPs of that time, red string, a big question mark.
On his laptop, police files from their secret database: key evidence and his father's phone recordings removed — only
possible with very high influence. He has identified the motorbike rider, later reported dead of a drug overdose. Who
ordered it? To be continued.
