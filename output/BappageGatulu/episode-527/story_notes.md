# Bappage Gatulu — episode 527: story notes

Duration 659.4 s (10:59), 72 shots. PRESENT timeline only (no 2011 flashback in this episode).

## Synopsis
2 am, Malé asleep. In his locked room, lit by the monitors, Iyaan works among electronic parts, microchips and the
reader holding the data of the card he secretly copied from Raaya. He cracks its encrypted codes: it only opens the
gate in the outer wall of Asim's house; the inner doors have offline biometric locks that can only be breached through
the system's main server. He hacks the government CCTV network around the house (two bodyguards at the door, no other
movement), studies the angles of the wall cameras and maps their blind spots on his own digital map. A message from his
secret source inside a telecom company gives the IP addresses of the house's internet and security systems; he starts
writing software to make the cameras repeat old views so the security room sees only an empty street. The biggest
obstacle: Asim's secret office opens only with Asim's fingerprint. Iyaan will make a fake silicone fingerprint, but
first needs a clean print from something Asim touches — so he must win more of Raaya's trust and get inside the house.
At the board he looks at Asim's photo: "however strong your security, I'll find a gap." Near dawn he pockets a secret
plug-in device that will give him the whole system once it is connected to Asim's router; he lies down (shown sitting
on the edge of the bed).
Next afternoon Raaya phones: come see my paintings at home, for tea. Asim is out at a meeting. Iyaan packs the device,
a fingerprint-lifting spray and tools. The bodyguards stop him and look him over, but Raaya comes out to welcome him and
they step back; he enters without needing the copied card. Marble floors and expensive furniture — luxury built on
public money; hatred burns, but he smiles: "Wow, Raaya, your house is beautiful." She seats him on a sofa and asks a
staff member for tea for two. When she goes to her room he spots a small glass-walled room in the corner: main router,
network box, server racks. He slips in, plugs the device into a free port at the back of the router — a small green
light — and returns to the sofa. Raaya brings her paintings; tea and light snacks arrive. His eyes stop on an expensive
gold pen on a glass shelf: Asim's signing pen, used this morning. "Bappa's favourite pen — he hates anyone else
touching it," Raaya laughs. A call from her gallery staff takes her out to the balcony. Second chance: he sprays the
pen, Asim's thumbprint appears, he lifts it with tape, puts the pen back and sips his tea. "Sorry, Iyaan, I took long."
He leaves, the key to Asim's secret room in his pocket and the house's security data streaming to his home computer.
At home, heart pounding, he locks his room: all the CCTV feeds and door logs are his. The office has the most modern
fingerprint reader; with a 3D resin printer he makes the print as a thin silicone layer for his own finger. A total
blackout would alert the guards, so he plants a virus that replays five calm minutes of earlier footage on demand —
the guards will see an empty sitting room. A loud alert: Asim has come home. On the sitting-room feed Asim enters,
uneasy, followed by his secret secretary with a big briefcase. Through the router's microphone: "All the evidence is
now in my office... That journalist Iyaan is digging too much. He must be stopped." Iyaan freezes — does Asim know? No:
it is about his corruption articles; Asim doesn't know who he is, but clearly wouldn't hesitate to have him killed.
"Tomorrow night, when Asim goes to the big political rally, I go into that house." He looks at the silicone fingerprint
on his palm. The plan is complete. To be continued.
