## Synopsis
2 am, Malé asleep. In his locked room, lit by the monitors, Iyaan works among electronic parts, microchips and the
reader holding the data of the card he secretly copied from Raaya. He cracks its encrypted codes: it only opens the
gate in the outer wall of Asim's house; the inner doors have offline biometric locks — "they can only be breached
through the system's main server," he says to himself. He hacks the government CCTV network around the house (two
bodyguards at the door, no other movement), studies the wall cameras' angles and maps their blind spots on his own
digital map. A message from his secret source inside a telecom company gives the IP addresses of the house's internet
and security systems; he starts writing software to make the cameras repeat old views so the security room sees only
an empty street. The biggest obstacle: Asim's secret office opens only with Asim's fingerprint. Iyaan will make a fake
silicone fingerprint, but first needs a clean print from something Asim touches — so he must win more of Raaya's trust
and get inside the house. At the board he looks at Asim's photo: "however strong your security, I'll find a gap."
Near dawn he pockets a secret plug-in device that will give him the whole system once it is connected to Asim's
router; he lies down (shown sitting on the edge of the bed).
Next afternoon Raaya phones: come see my paintings at home, for tea. Asim is out at a meeting. Iyaan packs the device,
a fingerprint-lifting spray and tools. The bodyguards stop him and look him over, but Raaya comes out to welcome him and
they step back; he enters without needing the copied card. Marble floors and expensive furniture — luxury built on
public money; hatred burns, but he smiles: "Wow, Raaya, your house is so perfect." "Thank you, Iyaan, come this way."
She seats him and asks a staff member for tea for two. When she goes to her room he spots a small glass-walled room in
the corner: main router, network box, server racks. He slips in, plugs the device into a free port at the back of the
router — a small green light — and returns to the sofa. Raaya brings her paintings; tea and light snacks arrive. His
eyes stop on an expensive gold pen on a glass shelf: Asim's signing pen, used this morning. "Raaya, that's a beautiful
pen — is it your father's?" "Yes, Bappa's favourite — he can't stand anyone else touching it," she laughs. A call from
her gallery staff: "Iyaan, wait a moment," and she steps out to the balcony. Second chance: he sprays the pen, Asim's
thumbprint appears, he lifts it with tape, puts the pen back and sips his tea. "Sorry, Iyaan, I took so long." "No
problem at all. I have to go now — I'm so glad I saw your paintings." He leaves, the key to Asim's secret room in his
pocket and the house's security data streaming to his home computer.
At home, heart pounding, he locks his room: all the CCTV feeds and door logs are his. The office has the most modern
fingerprint reader; with a 3D resin printer he makes the print as a thin silicone layer for his own finger. "How do I
black out the security system?" A total blackout would alert the guards, so he plants a virus that replays five calm
minutes of earlier footage on demand. A loud alert: Asim has come home. On the enlarged sitting-room feed Asim enters,
uneasy, followed by his secret secretary with a big briefcase. Through the router's microphone: "All the evidence is
now in my office room... That journalist Iyaan is digging too much. He has to be stopped." Iyaan's blood freezes — does
Asim know? No: it is about his corruption articles; but Asim clearly wouldn't hesitate to have him killed. "Tomorrow
night, when Asim goes to the big political rally, I will go into that house." He looks at the silicone fingerprint on
his palm. The plan is complete. To be continued.
