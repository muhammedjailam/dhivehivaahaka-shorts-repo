## Synopsis
A clear new morning in busy Malé. Inside, Iyaan burns with a fire that will not go out. In front of the mirror he does
up his shirt and reminds himself that from today every smile, word and glance must be calculated — he is going to war
against one of the most powerful and evil men in the country. He goes to the Athena Art Gallery on Boduthakurufaanu
Magu, run by Raaya, Home Minister Asim's daughter. Behind the big glass door: the smell of canvas and paint, modern
paintings, few visitors. He stops before a large oil painting of a red sun rising from a black sea. Soft footsteps, a
young woman's gentle voice: "what this painting shows is not just a sunrise." It is Raaya, in a simple white dress, so
innocent it is hard to believe she is a killer's child. Iyaan: it looks like the victory after a great war, or hope
coming out of darkness. Her eyes widen; "I'm Raaya, I painted this." — "I'm Iyaan" (narration: he holds out his hand;
she greets him with a smile). Memories of his father's death come to him but his face shows nothing. They talk about
art, philosophy and famous artists — he spent nights studying everything she likes. She feels she has found an
unusually like-minded friend: the trap's first success.

Two cars stop at the door; security guards get out; Home Minister Asim walks in, white shirt, black trousers, proud and
powerful. "Oh, that's my father." Iyaan's heart pounds: the man who ordered his father's death is in front of him; his
fists tighten; it takes all his will to control his rage. A deep breath, a calm face. "My dear Raaya" — Asim takes her
by the shoulder, looks Iyaan over: "Who is this?" — "Bappa, this is Iyaan, he really loves my paintings." — "Iyaan...
what family are you from?" Iyaan looks straight into his eyes: "An ordinary family, Minister. My father used to run a
business. He's no longer in this world." Asim nods — he does not see Ahmed Zahir in the young face — tells Raaya he is
off to a meeting and, turning to go, pats Iyaan's shoulder the way a superior does to an inferior. Iyaan looks at the
shoulder: "Your end will come by this very hand of mine." "Raaya, I have to go — we'll meet again?" — "Of course, I'll
be waiting." Out on the street: the first trap laid perfectly.

The next afternoon, a busy breezy café by the sea near the Hulhumalé ferry terminal. Iyaan at a sea-facing table with a
black coffee and a notebook of names and plans; he shuts it and puts it away when Raaya approaches in a soft light-blue
dress, happier than yesterday: "Hello Iyaan, am I late?" — "No, I just got here. What would you like?" She talks of her
paintings and dreams while another part of his mind works on a different plan. He casually asks about her father's
security: she sighs — CCTV and bodyguards always around the house, and the house can only be entered through a
biometric lock system (Asim's or Raaya's fingerprint or face scan). He sympathises — hard for an artist to be shut in
like that; she decides he is the only one who understands her. Her father wants her in politics, she doesn't; his
office room is forbidden and always locked. Iyaan touches the coffee cup to his lips: that secret office must hold the
remaining evidence of his father's death and Asim's present dirty dealings. He drives her home to Asim's castle-like
mansion. "Iyaan, today I was really happy." — "I was very happy too." As she gets out, a small key ring falls from her
handbag: gallery keys and a small electronic card. He quickly picks it up; a tiny digital scanner hidden in his palm
copies the card. "Here are your keys." — "Thanks, Iyaan." She walks to the house; he drives off smiling: he now holds a
copy of the card for the first security door of Asim's house. To be continued.
