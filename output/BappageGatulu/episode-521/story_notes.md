# Bappage Gatulu — Episode 521: story notes

## Synopsis
Present day. Iyaan (23) stands at his window listening to the traffic, convinced his father, MP Ahmed Zahir, hid the
corruption files somewhere — find them and he finds the killer. On his desk lies his father's old watch, stopped at
11:15, the moment of his death; Iyaan grips it. His chance comes when his mother Aminath leaves for medical treatment on
an island: the apartment is empty, and he can search his father's things without hurting her. In the small dark
storeroom that smells of old paper stands Zahir's big wooden chest. He blows off the dust, opens the old lock — old
parliament files, books, personal letters, nothing. After an hour he empties the chest and notices the inside is
shallower than the outside; pressing the bottom lifts a false panel: a black metal-cased hard drive, top-end for 2011.
He runs to his room, locks the door and connects it: "military-grade encryption — enter code". His software fails three
times; the drive is close to locking. Birthday, mother's name, wedding date — nothing. Then he remembers his father
whispering, days before he died: "My son, after every storm the birds sing in calm weather." He translates it into
English, arranges letters and numbers his father's way, and on the last chance — the screen blinks twice, a green
"access granted". The files: bank accounts, black-money lists, state land and lagoons sold secretly for millions — a
network controlling the state. Only a small bedside lamp and the blue screens light the room; his fingers tremble. A
folder "Confidential Audio 2011" holds one audio file. With headphones on he hears a phone ring, then the voice he hears
on TV every day: Asim, then a minister, now the powerful Home Minister. A man reports Zahir will submit all the
documents to parliament tomorrow and threats have failed; Asim laughs cruelly and orders: shut his mouth for good, make
tonight his last night, find and destroy the hard drive, the rest of the money will go to the account. Iyaan flings the
headphones down. After fifteen years he has the answer: Asim ordered it, the man in white who now runs national
security. At his board he removes the question mark, pins Asim's photo and circles the face in red: "Did you think
fifteen years made you safe?" No tears — a dangerous fire. He does not want simply to end Asim; he wants to watch his
power, honour, influence and family crumble, and see him beg. Hours of research find the weak point: Asim's only child
Raaya, apolitical, free-spirited, running a big art gallery in Malé, adored by her father. Iyaan smiles: "This is the
door into Asim's world." To be continued.
