# Bappage Gatulu (ބައްޕަގެ ގާތިލުން — "Father's Killers") — series bible

Dhivehi audio drama, political revenge thriller. This batch has six episodes that form one arc, in this order:
**520 → 521 → 522 → 527 → 529 → 536** (episodes in between are not part of this batch; each one ends "ނުނިމޭ" = to be
continued). Every episode's cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is the same series poster: Iyaan in a black t-shirt and
dark cargo trousers, fists clenched, on a rain-wet Malé street at night between dark buildings with red-lit shop
fronts; a faceless hooded figure looms behind him. Match that look (see `style.txt`): dark noir, charcoal/teal with
blood-red accents, rain and reflections.

TWO TIMELINES:
- **2011 (flashback, only the first ~5½ minutes of 520 plus brief memories later)** — a stormy November night in Malé.
  Cold, slightly desaturated blue-grey haze. Cards: `iyaan_young` (8), `zahir`, `aminath_young`.
- **PRESENT (fifteen years later; Iyaan is 23)** — modern Malé/Hulhumalé, sharp noir light. Cards: `iyaan`,
  `aminath`, `asim`, `raaya`, `sameer`, `fareed`.
Use `transition="dissolve"` into and out of flashbacks/memories and `transition="black"` for time jumps.

## Episodes
- **520** — 2011, a November night; black clouds over Malé, rough sea, empty streets, eerie silence. Iyaan (8) sits by
  the sitting-room window watching heavy rain run down the glass, waiting for his father, MP Ahmed Zahir, who was
  getting death threats for speaking out against corruption. Aminath sits on the sofa wringing her hands, watching the
  clock: 11 pm, Zahir is late. Iyaan asks why; she smiles, strokes his hair, tells him to sleep; he refuses — Bappa
  promised to bring a storybook. A car stops outside: Zahir's black car; he gets out of the back seat with a big
  briefcase, waves to the driver, walks to the door. A motorbike roars up with headlights off, two men in black with
  covered faces; it stops before Zahir; the driver tries to get out; the pillion rider pulls out a long knife-like thing
  and stabs Zahir many times as Iyaan watches through the glass (NEVER shown — see rules). Briefcase falls, Zahir cries
  out, Iyaan screams "Bappa!", Aminath runs to the door; Iyaan opens the main door; the bike speeds away; under the
  street lights his father lies on the ground, rainwater turning red. Iyaan falls on his father, holds his face; Zahir
  opens his eyes, struggles to speak: "son… look after… mother…" — his last words; he dies. Aminath collapses
  weeping over him, the driver phones for an ambulance shaking, neighbours look out of their windows. Iyaan's tears dry
  from shock; he stares at his hands; a fire of revenge ignites; he vows to find and punish the killers. His innocence
  drowned that night; his heart turned to stone. — "Fifteen years of silence" — Iyaan is now 23, tall, strong,
  the country's most skilled investigative journalist; trained abroad in martial arts and self-defence, a hacker with
  his own tracking network. He lives with his mother in a small apartment; she is frail, always with prayer beads. She
  asks if he has come home; he sits beside her and kisses her hands (mother–son, allowed); she has taken her medicine;
  begs him to be careful writing about corruption ("your father too…"); he reassures her. He recalls the police closing
  the case as "unsolved" when he was 12 — leaving the police station, losing faith in justice. That night he locks his
  bedroom: a big board on the wall with photos of the murder night and MPs of the time, linked with red string, a big
  question mark in the middle. He opens his laptop: he has pulled the investigation files from the police database —
  key evidence and his father's phone recordings were removed, only possible with very high influence. He has identified
  the motorbike rider — later reported dead of a drug overdose (never show drugs). Who ordered it? To be continued.
- **521** — Iyaan at his window, Malé traffic noise. He believes his father hid the corruption files somewhere. On his
  desk, his father's old wristwatch stopped at 11:15, the time of death; he grips it. His mother leaves for treatment on
  an island; the apartment is empty — his chance. He goes into the small dark storeroom smelling of old paper; in a
  corner his father's big wooden chest. He blows off the dust, opens the old lock: old parliament files, books, letters
  — nothing. After an hour, disappointed, he empties the chest; notices the inside is shallower than the outside; presses
  the bottom — a secret compartment lifts: a black metal-cased hard drive. He runs to his room, locks the door, connects
  it: "military-grade encryption — enter code". Three failed attempts, near lockout. He remembers his father whispering
  his favourite saying ("after every storm, the birds sing in calm weather"), translates it into English and arranges it
  as a code like his father used to; last chance — "ACCESS GRANTED" in green (no readable text on screen; show a green
  glow). Files: bank accounts, black-money lists, state land and lagoons sold secretly, millions of dollars. Only a
  small bed-lamp and the blue screen light the room; his fingers tremble on the mouse. Folder "Confidential Audio 2011":
  he puts on headphones and plays it — a phone ringing, then a heavy male voice he hears daily on TV: Asim, then a
  minister, now the powerful Home Minister. A man reports Zahir has all the documents and will submit them to parliament
  tomorrow; threats failed. Asim laughs cruelly: "Close his mouth forever. Make tonight his last night. Find and destroy
  the hard drive. The rest of the money will go to the account." Iyaan throws off the headphones. Asim directly ordered
  it — the man who today wears white and is in charge of national security. Iyaan stands at the board, removes the
  question mark, pins Asim's photo and circles his face with a red marker: "Asim… did you think fifteen years made you
  safe?" No tears; a dangerous fire in his eyes. He won't simply kill Asim — he wants to watch his whole political
  empire, honour, influence and family crumble, and see him beg. Hours of research for Asim's weak point: his only
  child Raaya, apolitical, free-spirited, runs a big art gallery in Malé; Asim adores her. Iyaan smiles dangerously:
  "This is the door into Asim's world."
- **522** — A new day, clear sky, busy Malé. Iyaan buttons his shirt in front of the mirror (show him fully dressed,
  doing up the top button of a charcoal button-up shirt) and reminds himself every smile and word must be calculated. He
  goes to the "Athena Art Gallery" on Boduthakurufaanu Magu: big glass door, smell of canvas and paint, modern paintings,
  few people. He stops before a large oil painting of a red sun rising out of a black sea. Soft footsteps; a woman's
  voice: "this painting isn't just a sunrise." It is Raaya in a white dress, innocent face. Iyaan: victory after a great
  war, hope rising out of darkness. Her eyes widen; she introduces herself as the painter; "I'm Iyaan" (the narration
  has him offer his hand — show instead: he places a hand on his chest in greeting, she nods and smiles, arm's-length
  gap). They talk about art, philosophy, famous artists — he studied all her interests for nights. Two cars stop
  outside; security guards; Home Minister Asim walks in, white shirt, black trousers, proud and powerful. "Oh, that's my
  father." Iyaan's jaw tightens; he controls his rage. Asim puts his hand on Raaya's shoulder (father–daughter, allowed),
  looks sharply at Iyaan: "Which family are you from?" Iyaan: "an ordinary family, Minister; my father was a
  businessman, no longer alive." Asim doesn't recognise Zahir in his face; leaves for a meeting, patting Iyaan's shoulder
  like a superior. Iyaan, alone: "Your end will come by my hand." He tells Raaya he must go; she says she'll wait. Next
  afternoon: a café near the Hulhumalé ferry terminal by the sea, breezy and busy. Iyaan at a sea-facing table with black
  coffee and a notebook of names and plans — he shuts it and puts it in his bag when he sees Raaya coming in a light-blue
  dress. They order; she talks of her paintings and dreams; he steers to her father's security: CCTV cameras, bodyguards,
  a biometric lock on the house; her father's office room is forbidden and always locked; her father wants her in
  politics but she doesn't. He drives her home to Asim's castle-like mansion. As she gets out, a small key ring falls
  from her handbag: gallery keys and a small electronic card; Iyaan quickly picks it up — a tiny digital scanner hidden
  in his palm secretly copies the card — and hands it back ("thanks, Iyaan"). He drives off smiling: he now has a copy
  of the card for the first security door.
- **527** — 2 am, all Malé asleep. Iyaan in his locked room lit by the monitor: electronic parts, microchips, the card
  reader. He cracks the card's encrypted codes — it only opens the outer wall gate; inside are offline biometric locks.
  He hacks the government CCTV around Asim's house, studies live feeds (two bodyguards at the gate), maps the cameras'
  blind spots on a digital map. A message from his secret telecom-company source gives the IP addresses of the house
  network. He builds software to loop the cameras so guards will see only an empty street. The biggest challenge: the
  secret office door opens only with Asim's fingerprint — he plans a fake silicone fingerprint but first needs a clean
  print of Asim's from something he touches. He looks at Asim's photo on the board: "I'll find a gap." By near dawn he
  has finished; puts a secret plug-in device in his pocket; lies down on the bed (show: sitting on the edge of the bed in
  the dark, or the room empty with the device on the desk). Next afternoon Raaya calls: come and see my paintings at
  home, for tea. Asim is out. Iyaan brings the plug-in device, a fingerprint-lifting spray and tools. Bodyguards stop
  and eye him, but Raaya comes out to welcome him and they step back. Inside: marble floors, expensive furniture — luxury
  built on public money; hatred burns but he smiles: "Wow, Raaya, your house is beautiful." She seats him on a sofa and
  asks a staff member for tea. While she fetches paintings he spots a small glass-walled room in a corner of the sitting
  room: the main router, a network box and server racks; he slips in and plugs the device into a free port at the back of
  the router — a small green light; he slips back to the sofa. Raaya brings paintings; tea and light snacks. His eyes
  land on an expensive gold pen with Asim's name on a glass shelf (no text visible) — Asim's signing pen, used this
  morning. Raaya: "Bappa's favourite pen, he hates anyone touching it." Her phone rings (gallery staff) and she steps
  out to the balcony. Second chance: he sprays the pen lightly, Asim's thumbprint appears, he lifts it with a small piece
  of tape, puts the pen back, sips tea calmly. "Sorry, Iyaan, I took long." He leaves with the key to Asim's secret room
  in his pocket and the house's security data streaming to his computer. At home he locks his room: the live CCTV feeds
  and door logs are his. The secret office has the most modern fingerprint reader; he 3D-resin-prints the print as a
  thin silicone layer that fits on his own finger. To avoid a total blackout he plants a virus to loop five calm minutes
  of earlier footage on demand. An alert: Asim has come home. Live sitting-room feed: Asim, uneasy, followed by his secret
  secretary carrying a big briefcase. Via the router's microphone: "All the evidence is in my office now… That journalist
  Iyaan is digging too much. He must be stopped." Iyaan's blood freezes — does Asim know? No: it's about his corruption
  articles; Asim doesn't know who he really is, but wouldn't hesitate to kill him. "Tomorrow night, when Asim goes to a
  big political rally, I'll go into that house." He looks at the silicone fingerprint on his palm. The plan is complete.
- **529** — Pitch-dark sky over Malé, lightning flashes, thunder, cold damp wind, rain about to pour; it is just past
  2 am. Asim and Raaya are at an official banquet for a foreign delegation at Kurumba Maldives resort; only outer-wall
  guards at the house. Iyaan in a narrow lane behind the mansion, dressed in black, face covered by a mask, codes running
  on his phone. "3… 2… 1… run." The virus loops the CCTV. The copied card opens the outer gate with a soft beep. Like a
  shadow he moves through the sitting room; every footstep echoes in the silence. Up the stairs to the secret office: a
  blue-glowing fingerprint reader. He places the silicone print on his right thumb: red light… he holds his breath… beep,
  green; the heavy lock clicks open. Inside, silence; he switches on a small flashlight: Asim's secret office — a big
  desk, tall bookshelves; behind the desk a big steel safe. He attaches a code-breaking device to the safe's keypad;
  numbers race. Suddenly a car stops outside — voices coming in: Raaya "Bappa, I'm so tired today"; Asim "Go to sleep,
  dear." Footsteps up the stairs, straight to this room; the safe needs another minute; no way out. The door's lock beeps;
  he snatches the device and hides under the desk. The door opens, the big light comes on; from under the desk Iyaan sees
  Asim's expensive black shoes. Asim searches files on the desk; Iyaan's hand brushes a small drawer handle, a glass
  paperweight on the desk rattles; Asim freezes: "Who's there?" He slowly circles toward the back of the desk; as he
  bends to look under it — all the lights die, an alarm blares, a red-alert siren fills the house. Asim shouts for his
  guards: "Why did the power go? Turn on the backup!" Iyaan's software overloaded the main fuse system; the backup
  generator starts in a minute. The office door bursts open, a phone flashlight: "Bappa! Where are you?" — Raaya,
  terrified. "Raaya, don't come in!" Asim takes her hand (father–daughter) and leads her out. In that moment Iyaan slips
  behind a long curtain, through a glass door onto the balcony; the generator roars, lights return; Asim looks back into
  the office — empty, under the desk empty; thinks he imagined it, locks the door, goes downstairs with Raaya. On the
  balcony Iyaan breathes, drenched in sweat; he jumps from the balcony wall (show: a dark figure crouched on the balcony
  wall, ready — no fall), through the trees behind the house into the narrow lane, pulls off the mask and walks away:
  "Raaya… you saved my life." The device had already sent the safe's data to his phone over wifi. At home he locks his
  room, still trembling (the narration says he takes off his black t-shirt and lies on the bed — show him SITTING on the
  bed fully clothed in a dark t-shirt, holding a tablet). Folder "PZ 2011": a 15-year-old secret police log of Asim's
  orders to the lead investigator: the motorbike number plate and the fingerprint reports from the knife were destroyed by
  a letter with Asim's signature (show papers as blank/blurred). An Excel sheet "Partners' Share": three men — Asim, the
  current Speaker of Parliament Sameer and Fareed, MD of the largest private bank — stole millions from the state budget
  to secret offshore accounts; Fareed paid for the murder, Sameer covered it up politically, Asim had gangsters do it.
  "It's the whole system." He pins Sameer's and Fareed's photos either side of Asim's and links all three with red
  string. New plan: destroy the trust between them — send Fareed his own secret bank files as if from Asim, so Fareed
  thinks Asim is blackmailing him. 8 am: his phone vibrates — Raaya, crying: someone hacked the house last night and tried
  to open Bappa's safe, he ran away; police all over the house; Bappa is very scared, says it's a political enemy; "don't
  come here now, Bappa suspects everyone, police are checking phones." His heart stops but he acts shocked and
  concerned. A twinge of pity: she's innocent — but she is a killer's child. The virus wiped every frame of him; police
  see only an empty staircase. Asim's fear is Iyaan's first psychological victory. At his computer he opens a dark
  hacker network (show: a black screen with green code lines, an anonymous mask silhouette icon — no text) and sends the
  first warning to Fareed's private email, with the sender's IP pointing to Asim's office. The storm has begun.
- **536** — 9 am: Fareed at his office desk opens the secret email; his face changes: receipts of the money he sent to
  the killers' account in 2011 to murder MP Zahir; at the bottom: "These secrets won't stay hidden forever. Get ready to
  pay." His most trusted IT man traces it: sent from Home Minister Asim's house. Fareed, furious, grinding his teeth,
  believes the break-in was Asim's staged drama to extort him; he phones Speaker Sameer: "We must meet now. Asim's lost
  his mind — he wants to destroy us!" Two hours later, in a secret private lounge in Hulhumalé: Sameer seated (the
  narration says he is smoking — NEVER show a cigarette or smoke; show him seated with his hands folded, or turning a
  pen), Fareed standing, Asim seated, angry. Asim slams the table (palm on table only): "What nonsense, Fareed? Someone
  broke into my house last night and nearly opened my safe! Why would I email you?" Fareed throws his phone in front of
  Asim: "Then why does the IP match your router? You're trying to pin Zahir's murder on me!" "Stop!" shouts Sameer —
  experienced and certain: Asim's no fool, he wouldn't send an email that traces so easily; last night's intruder got into
  the system and used that IP. "Then who?" asks Asim, now afraid. "Who knows the three of us were involved?" Sameer opens
  a file on his phone: no camera caught the intruder, but police cyber-crime saw the virus — the work of a top digital
  forensics expert; and a young journalist has been writing detailed corruption stories with evidence: Iyaan — "and do you
  know what I found this morning? He is the only son of Ahmed Zahir, whom we destroyed fifteen years ago!" The ground
  falls from under Asim: he remembers the young man at Raaya's gallery, the deep look in his eyes when he patted his
  shoulder. "He used Raaya to get into my house!" Sameer stands (drops the cigarette — not shown): "Zahir's son has
  grown up and is ready to grab our throats. He must be eliminated." The three men's war turns against Iyaan. But Iyaan
  was listening live through Asim's phone the whole time; he puts down his headphones, no fear — a dangerous smile: "You
  found out too late. When you come to destroy me, you'll walk right into my net." Asim starts the hunt, but Iyaan is two
  steps ahead. First he calls Raaya, serious: come secretly to the art studio, unseen by her father's security and the
  police. She trusts him and goes. A quiet Malé lane, an old building, third floor: a silent studio; a laptop open on a
  table; Iyaan at the window looking out. "Why did you call me here? Bappa is furious, he'll do anything to find who
  breached the house." He turns, no smile today: "Do you know who got into your house? It was me." She steps back,
  eyes wide: "What kind of joke is this?" "I went in to find the truth of my father's murder. Your father, Home Minister
  Asim, wears white but his hands are stained with an innocent man's blood." "No! That's a lie! My father would never!"
  Tears; she turns to leave. "Listen to this." He touches the laptop: Asim's own voice from the safe audio: "Zahir has
  started talking too much… close his mouth forever… make tonight his last night…" Her hands go to her ears; she knows
  the voice. She sinks to the floor (show: kneeling/sitting on the floor against the wall, hands over mouth — not lying);
  her world stops, her heart breaks. Iyaan kneels in front of her (a respectful arm's-length gap): "The man murdered was
  my father. He died in my arms when I was eight, by your father's order. For fifteen years my only purpose was this
  truth." She sees the deep grief and hatred in his eyes; no words. "What should I do?" she sobs. "Asim plans to kill me
  tonight with Sameer and Fareed. I need your help; Asim can only be beaten from inside his house." (The narration has
  him take her hands — show instead: he holds out the laptop / a small black drive towards her, no touching.) "Before
  his next big rally I need the second backup drive with his data. Only you can do it." Long silence; then she decides
  to help, for justice and for her father's great sin. Wiping her tears: "I'll do it. I'll help expose my father's crimes
  to the whole nation." Iyaan, inwardly: "Asim, you took my father from me. Today your own child stands against you. Your
  end will be no less heartbreaking."

## Content rules (BINDING — they override the literal narration)
1. **The murder (520) is never depicted**: no knife or "long blade", no stabbing, no strike, no blood, no wound, no
   red water, no body on the ground. Show instead: rain on the window glass and the boy's face pressed to it in horror
   (lit by headlights); a motorbike headlight cutting through the rain / two dark helmeted silhouettes on a motorbike
   seen from far behind; the black briefcase lying alone on the wet road under a street light; the open front door with
   rain blowing in; the boy kneeling in the rain beside a dark shape that is OUT OF FRAME (frame on the boy from the
   chest up, looking down, rain); Zahir's last words = a close-up of the boy's face in the rain with tears, or a man's
   hand (sleeve of the navy jacket) loosening around a small boy's hand on wet tiles — no blood; death = rain on an
   empty street at night, the mother kneeling seen from behind, neighbours' lit windows, a distant ambulance glow; "his
   hands stained with blood" = the boy's small open hands, wet with rain, trembling, in cold blue light (NO red on them).
2. No weapons of any kind anywhere in the series (the knife fingerprint report = a blank sealed envelope). Bodyguards
   carry no visible weapons. No guns, no knives.
3. No smoking: Sameer's cigarette is never shown (no cigarette, no ashtray, no smoke). No alcohol at the resort banquet
   or the lounge — glasses only of water/juice, or none.
4. No drugs: the overdose is only a closed file folder / a faceless photo pinned to the board.
5. Raaya always wears a hijab fully covering her hair and neck (the narration's shoulder-length hair is ignored), loose

8. The break-in (529): black hooded top, gloves, plain black cloth mask over nose and mouth (eyes visible) — never a

   analog watch face with no numerals. Investigation-board photos are small faceless/blurred portraits; Asim's "photo" on

11. Faces in the upper two-thirds, calm lower third. Never use the words knife, stab, blood, kill, dead, body, corpse,
    murder, gun, cigarette in a `visual` — describe only the safe substitute.
