# Yaraa (ޔާރާ) — series bible

Dhivehi audio drama by Shail Abdurrazzaq, a **newlywed romance that turns into a horror-mystery**. Story order (site
episode ids): **573 → 582** (one continuous arc so far; 582 ends on a cliffhanger).

Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is identical: a young woman in a long-sleeved red dress, seen in profile
from behind, in a foggy night lane of tall buildings lit by warm amber street lamps, teal-blue haze. Her hair is
uncovered on the cover, so **all references are text-only** and she always wears a black hijab in our images. The
cover's palette (crimson red against teal-blue night, warm golden rim light) is the series' signature: use it for
night and dusk, and keep Yaraa's crimson dress as the warm accent in daylight scenes.

SETTING: a deserted (uninhabited for many years) jungle island an hour's dinghy ride from Male — a dazzling white sand
beach, dense overgrown jungle of coconut palms, screw-pines and magoo shrubs, a narrow sand spit at the island's tip
(thundi), shady trees along the beach ridge where they leave their bags, an inland **pond (kulhi)** with a **taro field
(kandoofa)** beside it and thick tall grass clumps full of spider webs; later a dark storm with rain and a small blue
tarpaulin shelter the men rig on the beach. Malé: the jetty at dusk/night, lamp-lit lanes, Naveen's motorbike. Time:
the Eid al-Adha holidays, a calm clear morning; 573 ends around noon–early afternoon; 582 has the black cloud, thunder,
storm, and arrival in Malé at about 7 pm.

## Episode synopses
- **573**: Eid holiday; three days after the wedding, Mifzaal takes Yaraa on a picnic to a deserted island near Malé
  with Naveen and Shifza (who lost their first child). Calm sea, dinghy: Yaraa sits by Mifzaal in the back seat holding
  his hands; Naveen teases ("you'll be pulling his fingers off"), Mifzaal says he gave his whole heart to his "queen";
  everyone laughs. They land on a beautiful island (white sand, jungle), put the bags in the shade. Naveen and Shifza
  play in the shallows; Mifzaal takes Yaraa to the island's tip; she runs ahead across the sand, spreads her arms in the
  wind and beckons him; joyful moment in the golden sun. They rest in the shade of a magoo bush. Mifzaal says he hopes
  next time they'll bring a little child; she smiles, speechless with joy. Around noon Yaraa leaves to fetch food from
  Shifza ("I'll be right back"). Faisal phones: teasing about Mifzaal forgetting his friends now that he has a wife;
  Mifzaal laughs but feels a pinch. Yaraa still hasn't come back. As he dials her, a woman's voice calls his name from
  behind — not Yaraa's, not Shifza's: a hoarse, scratchy voice. He turns slowly: only a huge black cloud and the
  silent jungle, nobody. Yaraa's phone is switched off. He hurries back towards the boat feeling someone following,
  glancing back. The dinghy is still there; Naveen, puzzled, says Yaraa and Shifza went to the pond inland; signal is
  very weak here. Mifzaal asks if anyone lives on this island — Naveen laughs: it has been deserted for ages. Mifzaal
  keeps the voice to himself; both walk towards the pond. (Last shot: author credit "Shail Abdurrazzaq".)
- **582**: The black cloud arrives with a thunderclap. At the pond: they shout the women's names — no reply, not even
  footprints; no phone range. Mifzaal points at the taro field by the pond — maybe they went in; Yaraa loves learning
  how Maldivians lived in the old days. They search: only rustling leaves, spider webs in the tall grass clumps; neither
  dares enter the deep jungle alone. Despair, questions: where did they go? did someone do something to them? are they
  hiding to scare us? No help on this cut-off island. The voice spins in Mifzaal's head; his hands shake; Naveen stands
  frozen, hand on his head, then breaks: "my baby left this world without warning — has my Shifza left me the same
  way?" Mifzaal comforts him (hand on his shoulder): "I know what a father feels when he loses a child." Naveen: "But
  you only just married — how would you know a father's feelings?" Mifzaal freezes (as if hit by 32 bolts of lightning)
  and dodges: "I've got signal!", goes to the shoreline and calls the coast guard, who promise to come. Relief.
  Strong wind and heavy rain; they huddle under a small tarpaulin shelter. A message tone: a message from Yaraa's
  phone — a loving farewell: "I love you endlessly… a little while ago we were together on the beach… I left hoping to
  return… longing for your gentle hands… now what will be has happened; don't erase my memory, nor our resolve to make
  room for a little child; we've been married only three days; there's little hope I can come back; don't stay on this
  island, go to Malé; tell Naveen Shifu is fine; I will always love you." Mifzaal weeps, yet is relieved they are
  alive. They cancel the coast guard request to search themselves — without thinking it through. Across big waves they
  reach Malé around 7 pm. The hoarse voice again in his ear: "Ey Mifzaal, this is a new beginning. Remember, you must
  still come out under Yaraa's protection." Was the voice behind the disappearance, or is he imagining it? Naveen pats
  his shoulder: "Let's go home tonight; we'll check for news tomorrow." Naveen gets on his motorbike; Mifzaal decides to
  tell him about the voice; Naveen's eyes widen — and at that moment someone else behind them calls Mifzaal's name. He
  turns to see who. (Cliffhanger.)

## BINDING RULES (override the literal narration)
1. **Hijab always**: every woman's hijab fully covers hair and neck in every image (boat, beach, wind, memories). The
   cover's loose hair is never reproduced. "Her shining hair blew across his face" → the loose end of her black hijab
   fluttering in the sea wind near his shoulder.
2. **No swimwear, no body emphasis**: 573 seg 21–22 describe "the red swimming dress" and "her figure" → Yaraa in her
   normal loose crimson long dress, running barefoot on the sand seen from behind/medium-wide; never describe body
   shape. Naveen and Shifza "playing in the sea" → wading ankle/knee-deep fully clothed, splashing water, seen from a
   distance.
3. **Married couples (Mifzaal–Yaraa, Naveen–Shifza)**: may sit or walk side by side and hold hands; her hand on his
   arm is fine. **No embrace, no carrying, no lying down together, no kissing, no blown kiss.** 573 seg 24–25 (she
   jumps into his arms) → both standing close side by side at the tip of the sand spit, laughing, her arms spread wide
   in the wind, golden sun on her face. Seg 26 (lie down under the magoo) → both sitting side by side on a woven mat in
   the shade, leaning back against the trunk. Seg 32 (blows a kiss) → she looks back over her shoulder with a warm
   smile and a little wave.
4. **The voice is never a person**: no ghost, no woman in the jungle, no silhouette with a face, no glowing eyes, no
   hands. Show only: Mifzaal's frightened face turning; the huge black cloud over the jungle; an empty jungle path;
   swaying leaves; palm shadows; the empty beach. In 582's Malé scenes the "someone behind" is not shown either —
   end on Mifzaal turning around in the lamp-lit lane (cover mood), the lane behind him empty or out of focus.
5. **Yaraa and Shifza after they leave (573 seg 30) are never shown in the present**; no hint of harm. While the
   message is read (582 seg 30–42) use Mifzaal's face lit by the phone glow, the storm, the tarpaulin shelter, and soft
   dissolve **memories** of earlier happy moments (Yaraa smiling on the beach / in the boat — new images in 582's own
   folder, with a warm hazy memory glow).
6. **No child or baby anywhere** (Naveen's lost baby, Mifzaal's hoped-for child, his "father's feelings"): use the
   sky, the sea, the horizon, faces.
7. **Phones**: never a readable screen; screen faces away from camera or shows only a soft glow.
8. Men may touch men (Mifzaal's hand on Naveen's shoulder, Naveen patting Mifzaal).
9. Faisal (573 seg 33–40): a shot / reverse-shot pair — Mifzaal on the phone under the magoo tree; Faisal in his Malé
   office (or a café) on the phone, glasses, smiling.
10. Coast guard: only Mifzaal calling from the waterline; at most a distant grey sea, never officers.
11. The storm in 582 is dark slate-grey with silver rain, not night (afternoon); arrival in Malé = blue dusk with warm
    amber lamps; the final lane scene matches the cover's foggy teal night with amber lamps.
12. No text/numbers anywhere (no boat registration, no signs in Malé), no music; no adhan needed.
