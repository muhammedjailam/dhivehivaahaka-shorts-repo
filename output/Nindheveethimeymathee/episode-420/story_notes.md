## Synopsis
Night, days after Sana's death. Lail opens the door of his late mother's room and finds his father Haizum weeping. Lail joins him; Haizum consoles him (everyone goes when their time comes), tells him to return to school next week. Laira comes in and begs her father never to bring them another mother; he promises and holds both children close. Narration: Sana's later bitterness was the result of Haizum's "one mistake"; once she was gentle and their marriage happy until "someone's eye" fell on it. All three weep silently.
Near midnight Lail sits sleepless in his own room imagining his mother coming in with food and tucking his blanket. His phone lights up: six messages from Saba. They chat, then he calls her: shy silences, his verses, teasing ("Saba loves me, right?" — "No…"), goodnight; then texts, compliments ("especially your eyes"), red hearts.
Two days later at school, classmates offer condolences; Asil, Shahid, Saba and Sadhee arrive; at free period Lail and Saba slip out separately, meet on the street, both phones ring (Asil and Sadhee), they go to a cafe, then return to class separately; friends tease, they deny everything.
Days pass: Haizum returns to the office, Laira looks at universities, Maama Shafeeqa moves in to look after the children. Calls and secret meetings become routine. One afternoon Lail is dropped by his driver outside Asil's building; Saba comes down; they walk to the seawall. He buys one king coconut for both. "Are we really just friends?" He recites his own poems; she blushes, looks at the sea. Cliffhanger: she says his eyes look just like her little brother's — "Ali Laith Haizum".
