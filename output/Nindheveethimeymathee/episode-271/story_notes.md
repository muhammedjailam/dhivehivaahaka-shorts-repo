## Synopsis
Sunrise over Hulhumalé: joggers, cafés, parents walking children to school. On the balcony of his west-side
beachfront penthouse, writer Lail (29, back in the Maldives since yesterday after nine years of silence) types on
his laptop with a coffee. A Facebook notification: BREAKING NEWS — TV announcer Saba has jumped from a balcony. He
drops phone and cup, stunned, in tears. His elder sister calls (why didn't you say you were coming to Malé?); he
cuts her short, asks about her little Saha's fever, hangs up, changes into a T-shirt, grabs his wallet and drives to
the hospital. At the entrance he nearly collides with a woman; in the deserted ICU corridor she calls his name — it
is Sadhee, Saba's closest friend. Saba is in surgery; the police called Sadhee last night. Outside the theatre his
best friend Asil (Saba's cousin) clings to him weeping ("where were you? even Haizumbe told us nothing"); Miya
watches, surprised; school friend Shahid: Saba survived because it was the second floor; Zuhuruf (her husband) is
unreachable. The surgery succeeds. In the waiting area Sadhee confronts Lail about nine years of unanswered
messages; he refuses to talk. He slips away to the recovery-room door and sees Saba through the glass. Miya asks
how he knows Saba — "my classmate, Asil's cousin" — and why Saba didn't recognise him; he only says it's better
she doesn't, and leaves unseen. Night: pacing at home; flashback to yesterday at the airport, when Saba's message
invited him to "Tharinnaa Eku" — keeping an old promise that she would take his first ever interview. On the pool
terrace his friend Jahaadh calls ("watching TV?"); Saba's smiling face on TV. Sadhee texts: she is staying with
Saba tonight, come if you want. Driving there, a woman calls: "I'm still waiting for you." He sighs.
