## Episode synopses
- **591**: Rainy September night in Manhattan. On the 44th floor of Vance & Hart Investments, billionaire Victor Vance
  (50) is found dead at his desk, apparently by his own hand (the narration describes the body, the wound, blood and a
  pistol in his hand — none of it is shown). FBI analyst Alex Brand arrives, followed by NYPD Detective Rogers, who
  says it is an obvious suicide: cameras show Victor entering alone at 8:30, the door locked from inside, Wall Street
  troubles. Alex walks the room silently, loosening his tie, mapping the geometry. Kneeling by the desk, he notes: a
  suicide locks the door so nobody stops him — but would not arrange everything so neatly; the wound is said to be on
  the right, yet phone, wallet and papers are lined up on the left — the desk was staged. The left hand is clenched
  rigid, nails dug into the palm; he gently opens the cold fingers: an ancient, heavy, bronze-gold **Roman coin** with a
  laurel-crowned emperor and the Latin "Mors omnia solvit" (death settles everything) — a coin placed with the dead
  as an honourable death-gift. Skin under Victor's nails: he struggled; someone held him from behind and forced his own
  hand. Cameras? "Hackers loop cameras like a children's game." Alex orders the servers seized and forensics in: it is
  murder. At the window, the city below like a black sea: this is the start of a long-planned revenge. His phone
  vibrates: "Father". Judge Richard Brand, his trembling voice: he saw the breaking news. Alex says it is murder.
  Silence; fast breathing; Richard warns him some things should not be dug into, Vance dealt with dangerous people,
  better to accept the police verdict of suicide, "think of your career". Alex, stunned that the man who taught him
  truth above all now says stop, mentions the Roman coin — and on the line a crystal glass shatters on stone, a
  frightened gasp, the call cuts. "Father? Father!" — only the dead tone. Cold fear. Does Father know about the coin?
  Then the antique **fax machine** in the corner starts by itself (Rogers startles, hand going to his hip — not shown).
  A white sheet slowly comes out, no sender: "The first step has been taken. Six remain. The sins of 1998 cannot be
  hidden by buried bones. Justice comes in the rain, Alex." The killer knew Alex would come, knew his name. Thunder;
  the lights flicker. A dangerous game to reveal a dark secret of ~20 years ago, with Alex and his family in the middle.
- **615**: Dawn. On the Amtrak from New York to Boston Alex sits sleepless, the coin in a plastic evidence bag and the
  fax on the little table; its words repeat like the wheels. His father's phone has been off all night. The FBI Boston
  field office called him: Professor Edward Harris (~60), Harvard expert on ancient civilisations, found dead near the
  campus. South Station; he heads to Cambridge's old red buildings, wet golden leaves. A big Victorian house with
  police tape; an officer stops him, he shows his FBI badge (no lettering visible); Detective **Sarah Allison**, Boston
  homicide, tall, sharp-eyed, grey overcoat, hands in pockets, Boston accent, comes out; firm handshake (→ shown as a
  respectful nod, rule 4). "I've read your record — best pattern analyst" but she doubts a NY banker and a Cambridge
  professor are linked. Harris is dead in his own library, by cyanide it seems (body never shown; the "empty whisky
  glass" becomes an overturned teacup, rule 7). His hand is open. Sarah: suicide, a lonely man. Alex: lonely men don't
  write historical phrases at the end — a white sheet beside the professor with shaky Latin letters (shown as an
  unreadable faint scrawl). In the left pocket of the professor's tweed coat: an identical Roman coin, "Mors omnia
  solvit". Sarah turns serious: the second death. Vance died in NY at 9 pm, Harris the same night at 2 am: one killer.
  Harris taught at Harvard since the 1990s, an expert on ancient secret societies. Sarah searches the drawers and pulls
  out a dusty **1998 Harvard yearbook**; in the secret clubs section: a black-and-white photo of seven young men in
  suits with Roman-coin pins — "The Seventh Senate 1998". Young Victor Vance far right, young Harris smiling beside
  him; one face scratched out. Six members in 1998, two dead, five left. Sarah goes pale: in the middle, Senator
  **William Hastings**; and a little behind them a young woman — **Emily Ward**, Sarah's childhood neighbour, who
  vanished from Harvard in late 1998; police called it a runaway and closed the file; she never came back. Alex
  connects it: the club, the missing girl, a killer taking revenge — not legal justice but blood for blood. At that
  moment a small black metal object smashes through the library window: "Down!" Alex pulls Sarah down (→ both drop to
  the floor, no touching shown); thick black **smoke**; a figure jumps in through the window; Sarah shouts "Stop!" and
  fires into the smoke, hitting a bookshelf; Alex draws and aims at the shadow (→ no guns shown, rule 3). The figure in
  black with a mask has come for the yearbook, snatches it; Alex grabs his coat; a fast, trained fight — the figure
  twists Alex's arm and elbows his chest, Alex falls back against the desk (→ never shown as contact, rule 3). At the
  window the masked figure turns, dark furious eyes above the mask: "Alex! The third will die tonight! Give my regards
  to your father!" — and jumps out, gone into the rain. Smoke clears; the yearbook is gone, but one torn page is left
  in Alex's hand: part of the members' list; the third name is Senator William Hastings, due tonight for a live
  interview at a Boston TV station. Alex straightens his coat; they run out into the rain to save the third target.
  And the question remains: what was scratched out of that photo?

## BINDING content rules (override the literal narration)
1. **Women**: every woman (Sarah, Emily in the photo, any background woman) in loose long-sleeved ankle-length clothing
   or an ankle-length coat with a **hijab fully covering hair and neck**, in every shot. Sarah's "jet-black hair" is
   never shown.
2. **Dead men are never shown**: no body, no slumped figure, no hand of a corpse, no wound, no blood, no foam. Show the
   aftermath instead: the empty black leather chair turned away under a desk lamp, the desk with neatly lined-up phone,
   wallet and papers on one side and scattered reports on the other, the rain-streaked glass wall, a forensic marker
   with no numbers, police tape, an empty library desk with scattered papers and an overturned teacup, a closed door.
   Opening the dead hand = **Alex's own latex-gloved hand holding the coin up to the light** (or the coin in his gloved
   palm). Skin under nails / the forced trigger = Alex's narrowed eyes, his gloved fingers pointing at the desk,
   Rogers' shocked face.
3. **No weapons, no fighting shown**: no pistol, holster, gunshot, muzzle flash or gun-pointing anywhere (Victor's
   pistol, Rogers' hand to his hip, Alex drawing, Sarah firing). Shots fired = splintered bookshelf wood in the smoke /
   a startled face / hands empty. The smoke bomb = shattered window glass and billowing black smoke (no device in
   anyone's hand). The fight = the masked figure and Alex as tense silhouettes in the smoke at a distance; then Alex
   alone half-fallen back against the desk catching his breath, hand on his chest. No grabbing, twisting, elbowing.
4. **No touching between men and women**: the handshake = a firm respectful nod, Sarah's hands in her coat pockets.
   "Alex pulls Sarah down" = both crouched low on the floor a clear distance apart as glass flies.
5. **The coin**: an ancient bronze Roman coin, laurel-crowned emperor in profile (it is a coin, not an idol); its Latin
   inscription is NEVER legible — describe "a worn, illegible rim inscription". No blood on it; red is reflected light.
6. **No readable text**: the fax = a white sheet with a few blurred dark unreadable lines; the professor's Latin scrawl
   = faint shaky illegible pen marks; the yearbook page and member list = blurred lines, no letters; phone screens
   glow with no characters ("Father" on the screen is narration only); police tape plain; badges plain.
7. **No alcohol**: Harris's "empty scotch whisky glass" = an overturned porcelain teacup and saucer. Richard's breaking
   glass = a crystal water glass shattering on a stone floor.
8. **Richard's call**: show Richard alive and frightened in his study, the phone at his ear; the cut-off = the crystal
   water glass shattering on the dark floor beside the desk, the phone lying on the desk, an empty chair — Richard is
   never shown hurt, falling or attacked.
9. **The 1998 photo**: a faded black-and-white / sepia group photo of seven young men in dark suits with small round
   coin pins, smiling stiffly; one face replaced by a rough pale scraped patch; a young woman in a long dress and
   headscarf standing a little apart behind them. No caption text.
10. **The masked killer**: hooded black coat, black mask below the eyes, burning eyes; never with a weapon, never a
    monster. His voice is "wind-like" — just a cold stare.
11. **Lightning/thunder & flicker**: welcome as atmosphere (the 44th-floor lights dimming, rain on glass).
12. No music; no other faiths' symbols (no church crosses on the Boston/Cambridge skyline).
