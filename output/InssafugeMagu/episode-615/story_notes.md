# Inssafuge Magu — Episode 615 story notes

## Synopsis (English)
Dawn on the Amtrak from New York to Boston. Alex Brand sits sleepless, the Roman coin in a plastic evidence bag and the
killer's fax on the little table; its words ("The first step has been taken. Six remain. The sins of 1998 will not be
hidden by buried bones.") repeat in his head like the wheels on the rails. His father's phone has been switched off
since the call broke off last night — why was the judge so afraid, and what happened in 1998? An urgent message from
the FBI Boston field office has pulled him off the New York scene: Professor Edward Harris, a Harvard historian of
ancient civilisations, has been found dead near the campus.

From South Station he heads into Cambridge's old red-brick streets, wet golden leaves in the puddles, to a big
Victorian house ringed with police tape. An officer stops him at the door; Alex shows his badge and Detective Sarah
Allison (Boston Police Homicide) comes out — tall, sharp-eyed, hands in the pockets of her grey overcoat, a Boston
accent. She has touched nothing; she has read his record ("the FBI's best pattern analyst") but doubts a New York
banker and an old professor are linked. Harris lies dead in his own library, apparently from cyanide (body, foam and
the empty whisky glass are never shown). His hand is open, unlike Vance's. Sarah calls it a lonely man's suicide;
Alex answers that lonely men don't write historical phrases at the end — a sheet near the professor bears shaky Latin
letters. In the left pocket of the professor's tweed coat Alex finds an identical Roman coin, "Mors omnia solvit".
Sarah's hardness breaks: the mark of the second death. Vance died at 9 pm in New York, Harris at 2 am the same night —
one killer. Harris, a Harvard teacher since the 1990s and an expert on ancient secret societies: why would anyone take
revenge on him? Their past, their youth... Sarah pulls a dusty 1998 Harvard yearbook out of a drawer. In the
secret-clubs section: a black-and-white photo of seven young men in suits with Roman-coin pins, "The Seventh Senate
1998" — young Vance at the far right, young Harris smiling beside him, one face scratched out. Six members in 1998,
two dead, five left. Sarah turns pale: in the middle stands Senator William Hastings, and a little behind the men is
Emily Ward — Sarah's childhood neighbour, who vanished from Harvard in late 1998; the police called her a runaway and
closed the file. Alex connects it: a secret club, a missing girl, a killer taking blood-for-blood revenge.

At that moment a small black object smashes through the library window. "Down!" — both drop to the floor; it is a smoke
bomb and the library fills with black smoke. A figure jumps in through the window; Sarah shouts "Stop!" and fires, the
bullet hitting a bookshelf; Alex aims at the shadow. The masked figure in black has come for the yearbook and snatches
it; Alex grabs his coat, a fast trained fight, and Alex is thrown back against the desk. At the window the figure turns,
dark fury in his eyes above the mask: "Alex! The third will die tonight! Give my regards to your father!" — and leaps
out into the rain. The smoke clears; the yearbook is gone, but a torn page stayed in Alex's hand: part of the members'
list, the third name Senator William Hastings, due tonight for a live interview at a Boston TV station. Alex
straightens his coat and the two run out into the rain to save the third target. And the question remains: what was
scratched out of that photo? (To be continued.)

## Tone
Cold neo-noir suspense: sleepless dread on the train, procedural tension in the library, grief in Sarah's reveal,
then a sudden violent intrusion (shown only as glass, smoke, silhouettes and faces) and an urgent cliffhanger.
