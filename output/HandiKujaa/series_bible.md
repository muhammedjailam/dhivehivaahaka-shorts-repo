# Handi Kujaa (ހަންޑި ކުޖާ, "The Spirit Girl") — series bible

Dhivehi audio horror story. Story order (site episode ids): **580 → 583 → 594 → 605 → 629** — one continuous arc;
every episode opens by repeating the last lines of the previous one; 629 ends on a cliffhanger.

Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is identical: a beautiful spirit woman in a flowing white gown walking
barefoot over dark wet coral rocks at a stormy shore, huge wild indigo storm clouds, silver rain, a teal-green glow
on the breaking waves, a narrow strip of orange sunset behind a dark rocky headland, palms on the right. On the cover
her hair is uncovered and her dress sheer — **we never copy that**: our spirit is redesigned modestly (rule 2). The
palette (deep indigo/storm-blue, silver rain, sea-green spectral glow, a little warm amber) is the series' signature.

SETTING: **Beenaafushi (ބީނާފުށި)**, a famous small uninhabited picnic island of the atoll where everyone goes on
holidays: powder-white sand, a clear turquoise lagoon, coconut palms, dark coral rocks at the shore, and a **dense
dark jungle** inside (screw-pines, magoo shrubs, huge trees). On the shore near the beach: **an enormous centuries-old
nika (banyan) tree** with a vast canopy and huge twisting roots that form hollows big enough to shelter in (old island
travellers used to sit and recite Quran beneath it; protective verses were buried under it long ago). In the island's
centre, in a treeless clearing of old bare ground: **a small, very old whitewashed coral-stone ziyaarath (shrine
building)**, pale, with a dark low doorway (no text, no symbols). The family's **wooden Maldivian dhoni** (a cabin
"gegandu" on deck, an engine-room hatch, a wooden tiller) anchored in the lagoon. Their **camping tent (feyli-ge)**:
a large canvas family tent on the beach, lit inside by a single oil lantern (faanoos), mats on the sand floor, bags.

Times: 580 = bright sunny holiday morning on the sea and the island (turquoise, white), early afternoon heat; ~4 pm
sudden storm (black clouds, bruised violet light); sunset "like a dreadful curtain"; then storm night. 583–629 = one
long stormy night: near-black indigo, silver rain, white lightning flashes, sea-green spectral glow around the spirit,
torchlight/lantern light, warm ember glow.

picnic groups' dhonis in 580 = distant boats leaving.

## Episode synopses
- **580**: A dhoni crosses a blue-silk sea carrying Aadhambe's family of ten to Beenaafushi for a holiday. Aslam at
  the prow: "Bappa, the weather is lovely today!" Zaahid smiles at the sky. Hot early afternoon; they land; Raaidhu
  helps grandfather down; Aslam helps his mother. Grandfather and mother set up cooking and tea; children swim and
  play on the sand. Two other groups leave by midday — the family has the island alone. After 4 pm a black cloud from
  the north swallows the sky, wind rises, palms thrash. Aadhambe: "Zaahid! Call the skipper! A big storm is coming!"
  As the dhoni tries to leave the lagoon, something tangles in the propeller; the engine dies. Stranded. Sunset
  spreads like a dreadful curtain. Shareefa calls "Aslam, don't go far into the jungle!" — the wind swallows it.
  Aslam is on the west shore watching the waves; thunder, rain. Running back through the trees he sees, in a small
  clearing, a woman in white, her long dark hair (→ veil/ribbons) flowing in the wind, eyes glowing like a cat's,
  a bewitching smile. "A human? Or a handi?" She beckons; her eyes turn red. He is frozen; a soft voice calls
  "Aslam..."; she steps back into the jungle and he feels pulled after her. He tries to run — a terrible deep growl
  shakes the jungle. In the tent (wind nearly tearing it) Aadhambe recites Quran; Shareefa near tears: "Aslam still
  hasn't come!" Zaahid shouts his name into the black rain. Naazim takes a torch to search; Raaidhu wants to come.
  Grandfather: "No one goes into the jungle alone! This is no ordinary storm." They go together — Zaahid, Naazim,
  Aadhambe, Raaidhu with torches; the women pray in the tent. Tree shadows like giant ghosts. A crash on the right:
  under a huge tree, tangled in the roots, a big seabird freshly dead, torn (→ never shown bloody), a stench of rot
  and burning. Aadhambe pokes it with his stick; tiny black worms fall (→ not shown). "This was not done by humans.
  Hurry — Aslam's life is in danger." Deep in the jungle Aslam is lost in her spell; she glides towards him, her feet
  making no sound on the leaves.
- **583**: She closes in, raises her long cold hand, long black nails; her voice in his head: "Come to me..." He
  reaches for her hand — Zaahid's distant shout "Aslam!" breaks the spell. He sees clearly: her beauty melts into a
  ghastly face, black tears, a terrifying smile. He tries to scream, no sound; trips on a root and falls. Raaidhu sees
  a pale sea-green light to the west; Zaahid runs; they find Aslam unconscious, ice-cold, strange black marks drawn in
  the sand around him and a wet black handprint on his shirt. Zaahid lifts him into his arms. A huge shadow flies off
  over the treetops with a dreadful shriek; the wind redoubles; every torch dies. Pitch dark; rain like needles.
  Raaidhu panics; Zaahid: "I'm here!" Aadhambe recites Aayathul Kursi — the sounds around them quieten. Naazim's
  charged phone is dead. "This is that thing's doing. Take Aslam to the beach." Naazim's torch flickers back on; the
  brothers carry Aslam out to the tent. Shareefa and Aisha run to him crying; his face white and swollen, lips black,
  a stench of rot and burning. Aisha takes his hand — it's burning hot, though Zaahid said ice-cold. Aslam opens his
  eyes — completely white — sits up, stares out into the rain and laughs; a deep woman's voice comes from his mouth:
  "She has come... she has come for me..." Shareefa hides the children behind her; Aisha is stunned. Aadhambe kneels
  before him; Aslam grips his wrist like iron: "Old man, you think you can break my power?" Eyes veined red.
  Aadhambe: "By Allah's leave your power will leave this body!" and recites Surat al-Jinn loudly. Aslam convulses,
  screams, the tent cloth whips, the lantern flares and dims. Zaahid and Naazim hold his arms and legs — he has the
  strength of four men; Raaidhu grabs his feet. Black liquid from his mouth (→ not shown); he collapses unconscious.
  Silence but the rain. "Is he saved?" "Only for now. This is the beginning. The handi is in this jungle; she wants
  Aslam's soul. We must survive tonight." "But the engine is broken." A slithering sound on the sand outside. Naazim
  lifts the tent flap: by the waves, in white, the handi stands facing the tent; the surf roars louder where she
  stands; lightning; red eyes glowing like lamps.
- **594**: Zaahid sees her too: "Ya Allah!" A scream from the dhoni in the lagoon — Sameer, who stayed aboard to fix
  the engine. Aadhambe: "Don't go out!" but the brothers run through the rain and surf, climb aboard; a lantern swings
  in the cabin; Sameer lies at the engine-room door, dead (→ never shown: horrified faces, lantern, open hatch, a
  fallen spanner, a sandal). Soft laughter from the stern: by the tiller sits the handi, soaked in sea water (→ no
  blood on her face) — "Now it's your turn..." She rises; the planks creak with each step. A huge wave tilts the dhoni;
  the men fall; she flies up onto the cabin roof, red eyes blazing. "Jump!" They dive into the sea (cold, oily, foul),
  swim ashore. She stands on the cabin roof pointing toward the island, as if signalling. In the tent: women and
  children terrified, Aslam lying in the middle twitching. Aadhambe burns certain leaves in a small clay pot for
  smoke; the stench fades. "Shareefa, Aisha, bring the children to the middle, away from the tent walls." A long
  stretched inhuman shadow slides across the canvas; long fingers scrape outside — "khrees... khrees". Aadhambe recites
  over a bowl of water, blows three times, sprinkles it around the tent; a pained shriek outside. The lantern dies.
  In the dark Aslam sits up and seizes Aisha by the throat (→ never shown: darkness, a sliver of ember light on
  terrified faces). Raaidhu blows the clay pot's embers alight. Shareefa pulls her son's arm: "Let go, my son!" The
  handi's voice from Aslam: "He is mine! None of you can save him!" Aadhambe recites the strongest verses and strikes
  Aslam's back with his stick (→ no contact: stick raised, reciting); Aslam lets go and falls aside; Aisha coughs on
  the floor. The brothers burst in, stinking of the sea: "Sameer has been killed!" "Our only way out is sunrise; her
  power lives in the dark. We stay shut in this tent tonight." A heavy thud on the tent roof; the poles shake. The
  voice outside, now like a hundred voices: "Aslam... come to me... or your whole family will be buried here tonight."
  Aslam's unconscious body slowly rises into the air, limbs hanging, head tipped back toward the roof; the stench
  worsens. Aisha reaches for his foot — "Don't touch him! Get back!"
- **605**: Aadhambe recites over the water bowl and throws it at Aslam — like an electric shock, tiny blue sparks fly
  off him, he drops to the floor. A shriek on the roof; the canvas roof tears open; rain pours in. Through the rip:
  the handi standing on the tent pole in the rain, her beauty gone — rotten blackened face, red tears (→ ashen grey
  cracked face, glowing red eyes, no blood). "None of you will leave this island alive tonight!" Aadhambe: "Take Aslam
  and run to the big nika tree on the shore! I'm right behind you. Nobody look back!" Everyone runs into the rain; the
  sand looks red as blood (→ dull red storm-light on the sand). Lightning — Naazim looks back: grandfather fallen, his
  stick snapped in two, the handi flying after them. "Don't look back! Run!" Black roots burst from the sand trying to
  grab their feet; Shareefa recites Aayathul Kursi with the children. They reach the ancient nika tree and shelter in
  its giant roots; the handi stops at the edge of its shade, cannot enter, claws the air: "Before sunrise I will rip
  out this tree too!" Aslam twitches; his eyes darken; he grips Aisha's foot; then another voice: "Brother... grandpa
  ... grandpa is coming..." From the dark rain a figure walks up — grandfather, smiling, holding the broken stick.
  Zaahid starts toward him; Naazim holds him back — no footprints in the wet sand, and his feet are turned backwards.
  "That's not Bappa!" The handi laughs; the two figures circle the tree. "Your grandfather is ours now!" Aslam stiffens,
  claws at his own throat (→ not shown: he lies rigid, Aisha holding his hands). Naazim remembers grandfather's old
  tale: spirits fear iron. "Our cooking knives!" — from Zaahid's wet bag a big old iron kitchen knife; Naazim, lips
  moving in prayer, drives it into the sand beside Aslam's head. Aslam relaxes; two shrieks outside. Then rustling
  above: hundreds of black crows with red eyes in the canopy; they swoop down like a black cloud, pecking (→ no
  wounds). Zaahid covers his head; Naazim whirls a cloth (never shirtless); the women shelter the children in the roots.
- **629**: The crows and screams mingle in the rain; the handi can't enter but has turned the island's spirits on the
  family. Aslam suddenly stands, eyes normal but full of terror: "STOP!" — every crow falls silent and returns to the
  treetop. "Aslam, are you awake?" He turns to his mother in tears: "Mamma... uncle... we can't stay here. She opened
  my tongue; she wants me to tell you: a hundred years ago travellers killed her child in this jungle; her vengeful
  spirit rises once every hundred years; tonight is that night. She wants the life of the family's youngest male —
  Raaidhu!" Raaidhu backs against a root. The fake grandfather outside the shade stretches out his arms with the
  grandfather's loving voice: "Raaidhu, come to grandpa, I'm safe, I came looking for you" — his backward feet hidden
  by the roots. Raaidhu walks forward, spellbound; Zaahid grabs his shoulders: "Bappa, it's grandpa calling!" Naazim:
  "It's a trick!" — hand on the iron knife in the sand, reciting Aayathul Kursi. The handi descends and lays her long
  black nails on the fake grandfather's shoulder: his face rots, eyes turn red; the mask of deceit falls; Raaidhu
  falls back, awake. "You won't last till sunrise. The sea is coming." The waves turn red as blood (→ wine-red glowing
  sea, no gore) and creep up the shore; where they touch the outer roots, the roots blacken. Aslam: "Uncle, we must go
  to the other side — to the old ziyaarath in the middle of the island; grandpa said it's the oldest, safest building;
  that sea can't reach it." Zaahid: "Entering the jungle is walking into death's mouth." No choice: Naazim pulls the
  knife from the sand; the eight of them leave the shade. The handi dives at them in a gust; Naazim slashes the knife
  through the air (→ held up like a talisman); she recoils shrieking into the treetops. Aslam leads through the black
  jungle; black puddles, sharp thorns; the women carry the children. "Is it far?" A crash on the right — the fake
  grandfather bursts out running on his backward feet and rakes Naazim's shoulder with his long nails (→ no contact
  shown: Naazim fallen, clutching his shoulder, no blood); he drags Naazim toward the dark; Naazim strikes the figure's
  arm with the iron knife (→ the knife raised between them, the figure recoiling); deep inhuman roar; it lets go;
  Zaahid and Aslam lift Naazim. The handi descends again, intent on taking Raaidhu. They burst into a treeless clearing:
  a small pale whitewashed ancient ziyaarath of old stones, a strange silence. "Lord, save us!" Raaidhu prays — a
  flicker of hope — then it dies: standing in the shrine's doorway, blocking the way, is skipper Sameer, whom they saw
  dead in the dhoni: upright, face death-white, eyes completely black (→ no wounds; collar and neck in shadow), arm
  stretched out toward them. (cliffhanger)

## BINDING content rules (override the literal narration)
1. **Hijab always** for every woman and girl (Shareefa, Aisha, the small girls): fully covering hair and neck, also
   at night, in the rain, in the tent. Wet clothing stays opaque and loose.
2. **The handi is always modest**: opaque, loose, long-sleeved, ankle-length white gown and an opaque white veil fully
   covering her hair and neck — never sheer, never clinging, never uncovered hair, no jewellery. Her famous "long black
   hair reaching the ground / spreading like a net" is drawn as **long dark smoky ribbons/tendrils trailing from under
   her veil** on the wind. Beautiful form: pale-olive luminous face, sea-green glowing eyes. Angry: red-glowing eyes.
   Horror form: ashen grey, cracked, porcelain-like face with dark hollow eyes glowing red — never rotting flesh,
   blood, black tears, fangs or a monster face. Long dark fingernails are fine. She floats/flies with her gown streaming.
3. **No blood, no gore, no bodies anywhere.** 580's dead seabird: a large dark seabird lying still among huge roots,
   half in shadow, wings spread (no wound, no blood, no worms) — or just the torchlit faces staring down. Sameer's
   death (594): never his body — the brothers' horrified faces in swinging lantern light at the open engine-room hatch,
   a fallen spanner, a single rubber sandal. The red sea and red sand: an eerie wine-red glow/tint of storm-lit water
   and sand, no visible blood. Sameer's revenant (629): standing upright, ashen-white face, black eyes, collar and neck
   in deep shadow, arm outstretched — no wounds.
4. **Possession without body horror**: Aslam possessed = pale milky-white eyes or red-veined glare, ashen skin, a
   rigid sitting posture, an unnatural laugh. No black liquid, no foaming, no contortions, no bent-back head. His
   levitation (594/605): his body floating horizontally a little above the tent floor, limbs hanging, seen from the
   side in lantern light, the family recoiling — calm, eerie, not grotesque.
5. **No violence as contact**: the throat-grip on Aisha (594) is never shown — the dark tent, ember light on terrified
   faces, Shareefa pulling Aslam's arm while Aisha is out of frame or a blurred shape; Aadhambe's stick strike =
   Aadhambe raising the stick while reciting, then Aslam collapsed; Aslam's grip on Aadhambe's wrist (583) is fine
   (hands only); the crows never wound anyone (people covering heads, crows swirling); Naazim's shoulder (629) = him
   fallen on the ground clutching his shoulder, no blood; restraining Aslam (583) = the men holding his arms/legs down
   on the mat, no struggle gore.
6. **The iron knife** is a talisman: an old plain iron kitchen knife only held up high glinting toward the spirits, or
   standing upright stuck in the sand beside Aslam — never pointed into or touching anyone. No other weapons.
7. **Naazim is never shirtless** — in 605 he whirls a cloth (a sarong/towel from the bags) over his head.
8. **Quran and ruqyah respectfully**: Aadhambe recites from memory (eyes closed or raised cupped hands), blows over a
   small bowl of water and sprinkles it, burns leaves in a small clay pot for smoke. No visible Quran pages/text.
9. **Children** (three small kids) only small, in the background, behind/under Shareefa and Aisha; never close-up
   frightened, never touched by the spirit or crows.
10. **The fake Aadhambe** = Aadhambe's look with a sly unnatural smile and backward-turned bare feet (heels in front)
    on smooth unmarked wet sand; later ashen face with red-glowing eyes. Not gory.
11. **No faces in clouds/shadows** except the handi's own stretched shadow on the tent canvas (594) — a tall thin
    silhouette with long fingers, fine.
12. **No readable text**: no lettering on boats, phones, bags, the shrine; the dead phone (583) is a dark blank screen.
