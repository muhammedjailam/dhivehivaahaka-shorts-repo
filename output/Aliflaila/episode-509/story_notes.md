## Synopsis
- FRAME (shots 1–5): Night 5 in Shahryar's chamber. Shahrazad bows and asks leave to continue; the impatient king wants
  the third old man's secret and to know whether the jinni will kill the merchant.
- TALE (6–8): in the desert the jinni has pardoned two-thirds of the merchant's life; the third old man steps forward
  leading a grey mule: "this mule is my own wife".
- TALE-WITHIN-THE-TALE (9–54): his beautiful wife was secretly a sorceress. After a year of marriage he sailed away on
  a year-long trading voyage. He came home secretly at midnight through the back door, found the bedroom door ajar and
  saw her with another man (narration only — never shown). The man fled through the window; the wife, furious, threw
  spelled water on him and turned him into a big black dog, beat him and threw him into the street. A night of fear
  among fierce dogs and rubbish heaps. Next morning a kind old fish-seller fed him and took him home; the fish-seller's
  veiled daughter hid behind a curtain, recognised an enchanted man, and with water and "by the will of God" restored
  him. She gave him a flask of water; at midnight he threw it on his sleeping wife and turned her into a mule.
- TALE (55–63): the old man presents the mule; the jinni throws down his sword, claps with joy, frees the merchant
  completely, turns to black smoke and sinks into the earth. The merchant weeps, embraces and thanks the old men, who
  leave with their animals; he goes home to his wife and children.
- FRAME (64–76): the king sighs with relief; Shahrazad teases "The Fisherman and the Jinni" (a golden flask in the net,
  a terrifying thing inside); the king's curiosity is lit; dawn breaks with the Fajr call; Shahrazad falls silent; the
  king spares her one more day and goes to court. Night 5 ends; cliffhanger into Night 6.
