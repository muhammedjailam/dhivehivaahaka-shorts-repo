ALIFLAILA (އަލިފުލައިލާ) — EPISODE 498 — SYNOPSIS
Title card (shot 1): "Night Four: The Tale of the Second Old Man and the Two Black Dogs"
(ރޭ ހަތަރެއް: ދެވަނަ މުސްކުޅިޔާއާއި ކަޅު ދެ ކުއްތާގެ ވާހަކަ)
72 shots, 0–659 s (~11 min). Narration is third-person; the inner tale is told by Shahrazad, and inside it the
Second Old Man tells his own story in first person (tale-within-a-tale-within-a-tale).

======================================================================
1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
======================================================================

--- FRAME STORY (palace, night) ---

[Shot 1–4 | 0–31 s] Title of Night Four. Black night falls over the world; deep silence over the whole kingdom;
not a sound in King Shahryar's palace. But the king lies awake, restless and impatient to know how things end in
the "terrifying desert" between the jinni and the old men (continuing the previous night's tale).

[Shot 5–9 | 31–73 s] As the last part of the night nears, Dunyazad, as usual, softly calls her elder sister:
"Dear sister, if you are not asleep, finish the beautiful tale you broke off yesterday — tell us the story of the
second old man who had the two black dogs." Shahrazad looks toward the king and asks permission to continue.
Shahryar: "Tell it! I very much want to know the secret of those two dogs." Shahrazad begins.

--- INNER TALE (Shahrazad narrating): the Merchant and the Jinni, in the desert ---

[Shot 10–13a | 73–~105 s] "O mighty, wise king! When the First Old Man finished his tale, the giant jinni forgave
one-third of the merchant's blood (his forfeited life). Then the Second Old Man, leading two black dogs, stepped
forward and addressed the fearsome jinni: 'These two black dogs behind and beside me are not ordinary animals —
they are in truth my own two elder brothers, and I am their youngest brother.'"

--- INNER-INNER TALE (Second Old Man's own story, flashback, 1st person) ---

[Shot 13b–15 | ~105–131 s] When their father died he left the three brothers 3,000 dinars; they split it evenly,
1,000 each. The two elder brothers opened shops in the bazaar; the youngest (narrator) opened a small shop too.

[Shot 15b–23a | 131–~200 s] The eldest brother sold all his stock, bought 1,000 dinars of goods and sailed away on a
big ship to trade abroad. A year later, as the narrator sat in his shop, a ragged beggar in torn clothes stopped at
his shop door — it was his eldest brother, hungry and destitute, bankrupted. The narrator ran and embraced him,
took him home, had him bathed, dressed him in fine new clothes. Checking his accounts he had 2,000 dinars profit;
he gave his brother 1,000: "Don't grieve, brother, here's another thousand — open a shop again." The brother,
overjoyed, restarted his business.

[Shot 23b–26a | ~200–225 s] Soon the middle brother did the same — bought goods, sailed off, and a year later
came back penniless as a beggar. The narrator again gave him 1,000 dinars from his profits to restart.

[Shot 26b–31 | ~225–273 s] Two or three years passed. Both brothers came together and proposed that all three make a
big trading voyage. The narrator refused at first because of their bad track record, but their pleading wore him
down. He had 3,000 dinars of profit: 1,500 would be buried and hidden as a safety reserve "if things go badly";
the other 1,500 split as 500 each for goods. The brothers happily agreed.

[Shot 32–37 | 273–332 s] They bought goods, boarded a ship, and after many days reached a beautiful, wealthy island.
They traded in its bazaar for great profit. One day a very poor woman in tattered clothes greeted him and asked him to
marry her: "O good man, marry me — though I am poor, I will be utterly faithful to you." Moved with compassion, he
married her for God's sake, bought her beautiful clothes and took her aboard the ship as his wife.

[Shot 37b–39 | ~325–344 s] After the marriage he discovered the poor woman was in truth extraordinarily beautiful,
and she loved him deeply. But seeing this, great envy arose in his two brothers — envy of his trading success and his
wife's beauty.

[Shot 40–43 | 344–382 s] Sailing home at night, while he and his wife slept, the two brothers crept in, lifted them
both and threw them into the dark sea! He woke only when he hit the water and began to drown. Suddenly his wife
seized him with supernatural strength, lifted him above the water, and flew through the air to a nearby deserted
island.

[Shot 43b–50 | ~380–448 s] Amazed, he hears her say: "O my husband, don't be afraid. I am not human — I am a good,
Muslim jinniyya. When I saw your goodness I fell in love with you, so I came in the form of a poor human woman and
married you. You treated me kindly. But your wicked, envious brothers tried to kill us — so tonight I will go, sink
their ship and kill them!" Terrified, he clutched her feet and begged: "For God's sake don't kill them! However badly
they treated me, they are my brothers; I cannot bear their blood being shed." After long pleading her anger cooled:
"Very well, because you ask so earnestly I won't kill them — but I will give them the punishment they deserve."

[Shot 51–55 | 448–496 s] She flew with him through the air that same night to his own house. In the morning he dug up
the 1,500 buried dinars and went to open his shop — and found these two black dogs tied up inside! While he stood
astonished, his wife appeared there: "Husband, these are your two brothers. I sank their ship in the sea (after saving
everyone else aboard), then put a spell on these two and turned them into two black dogs for ten years — punishment
for their treachery and envy."

--- BACK TO THE DESERT (Second Old Man addressing the jinni) ---

[Shot 56–57 | 496–520 s] "O mighty jinni! Today it has been exactly ten years. I travel with these two dogs to find
my wife and ask her to break the spell and turn them human again. This is my story with the two black dogs — is it
not more wondrous than the First Old Man's?"

[Shot 58–62 | 520–567 s] The giant jinni clapped in amazement: "Yes! Truly a perfect and wondrous tale. For its
excellence I forgive another third of this merchant's blood for your sake. Now two-thirds of his life is saved."
Then the Third Old Man, leading a mule (ބަޣަލު), stepped forward: "O mighty jinni! The story between me and this mule
is more wondrous than both. If I tell it, will you forgive the rest of his blood for my sake and set him wholly free?"

--- FRAME STORY (palace, dawn → court) ---

[Shot 63–69 | 567–636 s] At that point golden dawn light appeared on the horizon; the sweet sound of the Fajr call to
prayer began, and Shahrazad immediately broke off and fell respectfully silent. Shahryar, completely lost in the tale,
suddenly rose: "What happened next? What is the third old man's tale? Was the merchant fully saved?" Shahrazad smiled
gently: "O noble king, it is dawn; my time is over. But if I am alive tomorrow night I will tell the third old man's
tale and what became of the merchant — an even more beautiful, exciting story. But you will only know if you let me
live." The king's curiosity is overwhelming; he is certain he cannot bear not knowing.

[Shot 70–72 | 636–659 s] So today too the king decides not to have Shahrazad killed. He rises, puts on his royal
robes and goes to the court (darbar) to govern. The fourth night ends; through the power of her wise tales
Shahrazad's life is spared. The executioner's sword is postponed yet another day.

======================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
======================================================================
Night Four of the frame. Starts mid-tale: continues "The Merchant and the Jinni" — the First Old Man (with the gazelle)
finished his tale previously and the jinni has already forgiven 1/3 of the merchant's life. This episode tells the
complete "Tale of the Second Old Man and the Two Black Dogs" (2/3 forgiven). Ends on a cliffhanger: the Third Old Man
with the mule is about to tell his tale for the final third; dawn interrupts; Shahryar spares Shahrazad one more day.
Note: this version's ending differs from the classic — the old man says he is travelling with the dogs to find his
jinniyya wife so she can lift the spell now that ten years are up.
