## Synopsis
FRAME (palace, night): the fourth night falls; the palace is silent but King Shahryar lies awake, impatient to know how
the tale of the jinni and the old men ends. Near the end of the night Dunyazad asks her sister to finish the story of
the second old man with the two black dogs; Shahrazad asks the king's permission; he says "Tell it!".

TALE (desert, Shahrazad narrating): after the First Old Man's tale the giant jinni forgave one third of the merchant's
blood. The Second Old Man steps forward: the two black dogs are his own elder brothers; he is the youngest.

TALE-WITHIN-THE-TALE (the Second Old Man's own story, storybook glow): their father left 3,000 dinars, 1,000 each; all
three opened shops. The eldest sold up, sailed abroad, came back a year later as a ragged beggar; the youngest embraced
him, bathed and clothed him and gave him 1,000 dinars of his 2,000 profit. The middle brother did the same and was
helped the same way. Two or three years later the brothers pleaded for a joint voyage; the youngest buried 1,500 dinars
as a reserve and shared 500 each. On a rich island a poor woman in torn clothes asked him to marry her; out of
compassion he married her for God's sake, clothed her and took her aboard; she proved very beautiful and loved him. The
brothers grew envious, and one night at sea threw the sleeping couple overboard. She lifted him from the water and flew
him to a deserted island: she is a good Muslim jinniyya who loved him for his goodness; she vowed to sink their ship and
kill them; he begged mercy for his brothers; she relented but promised punishment. She flew him home that night. Next
morning he dug up the gold, opened his shop and found two black dogs tied inside; his wife appeared: she had sunk their
ship (saving everyone else) and turned the brothers into dogs for ten years.

TALE (desert): ten years are up today; he travels with the dogs to find his wife and have the spell lifted. The jinni
claps in amazement and forgives the second third. The Third Old Man with a mule steps forward and offers his tale for the
last third.

FRAME (dawn): golden dawn and the Fajr call; Shahrazad falls silent. Shahryar demands more; she promises an even better
story tomorrow if she lives. He spares her again, dresses and goes to his court. The executioner's sword is postponed
another day. Cliffhanger: the Third Old Man's tale (509).
