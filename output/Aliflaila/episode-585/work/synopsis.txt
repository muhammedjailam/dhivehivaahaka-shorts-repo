1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
======================================================================

[Shot 1 | 0–5 s] Title card: Chapter Eight, "The Fisherman's Cunning and King Yunan's Tale, part two".

[Shots 2–5 | 5–49 s] FRAME. The following night, in the deep silence before dawn when the cool pre-dawn breezes begin to blow from the east (or "from above/the horizon"), Dunyazad calls to her elder sister Shahrazad: "Dear sister, if you are not asleep, tell us what happened next — the evil, envious vizier of King Yunan plotting against Hakim Duban, and what came of it. My heart is impatient to know the fate of the fisherman and the jinni shut up in the jar." King Shahryar, eager to hear the rest, gives Shahrazad permission to continue.

[Shot 6 | 49–59 s] FRAME → TALE. Shahrazad, very courteously: "O noble mighty King, I will continue from where I stopped yesterday."

[Shots 7–8 | 59–79 s] INNER TALE (Yunan). In King Yunan's court, an evil vizier sees the honour and respect the king lavishes on Hakim Duban (the sage-physician) and is consumed by envy. He resolves to ruin Duban and bring down his standing before the king.

[Shots 9–12 | 79–116 s] One day, catching the king alone, the vizier secretly approaches: "I pray always for your power and reign, but it would be disloyal to hide a great fear in my heart. This Duban who supposedly cured you is a grave danger to your life — he is a spy sent by your enemies!"

[Shots 12–15 | 108–144 s] King Yunan, angry: "What are you saying? He is my greatest helper. He cured my terrible disease completely without my drinking any medicine or rubbing anything on my body. I am in his debt — why accuse him? Are you jealous and trying to defame him?"

[Shots 15–19 | 134–182 s] The vizier, cunningly: "Your pure heart blinds you to this deceiver's true nature. Think! A man who cured your great illness with a mere stick held in your hand (the polo-mallet cure) could just as easily kill you with some other such object or with a perfume/fragrant thing. He came from a foreign country to win your trust, secretly kill you and seize the kingdom. If you don't believe me, you will soon perish."

[Shots 19–21 | 172–206 s] Hearing this repeated, the seed of doubt is planted; fear grips the king's mind. "If there is truth in this, I must kill him at once — it is the only way to be safe." He immediately orders Duban summoned to the palace.

[Shots 22–25 | 206–241 s] Duban arrives happily, expecting a great reward — but sees the king's furious face and armed guards all around and realises something is wrong. The king: "Duban! You came to win my trust and then kill me. You are a spy. Today I have ordered your head cut off!"

[Shots 25–28 | 231–268 s] Duban, stunned and grieving: "What are you doing? I cured you and gave you new life — is this my reward? Do not kill me! Spare me and God will spare you; kill me and God will destroy you!"

[Shots 28–29 | 260–278 s] But the vizier's poison has hardened the king's heart; no pity remains. He orders the executioner (jallad) to behead Duban at once.

[Shots 30–33 | 278–311 s] Seeing there is no escape, Duban sighs deeply: "Before you kill me, let me go home, bid farewell to my family, and give away my precious medical books and gifts to the people. Above all I wish to present you the most valuable, wondrous book I own — one fit for your royal treasury."

[Shots 34–37 | 311–342 s] The king, curious: "What book? What is special about it?" Duban: "It holds the world's greatest secrets. Its greatest marvel: after my head is cut off, set it on a tray, open the book to a certain page, question my head — and the severed head will speak and answer every question you ask."

[Shots 38–39 | 342–361 s] The king is amazed and burns to see a talking severed head. He grants Duban one day's respite to go home and settle his affairs.

[Shots 39–43 | 353–403 s] Next day the whole court assembles. Duban arrives carrying an old, heavy book, a small tray and some small medicine bottles. He kneels before the king, sets down the tray and book: "Here is the book. After my head is cut off, put it on this tray and rub some of the medicine from this bottle on the cut to stop the blood. Then open the book, keep turning its pages, and question my head."

[Shots 44–45 | 403–423 s] On the king's command the executioner steps forward and severs Duban's head with his sharp sword; it is placed on the tray. Once the flowing blood is stanched, before everyone's eyes, the severed head miraculously opens its eyes and begins to speak to the king.

[Shots 46–50 | 423–466 s] The head: "O King, open the book!" The king eagerly takes up the heavy book, but the pages are stuck tightly together. To separate them he licks his finger and turns page after page — first, second, third — but they are blank. "Duban! Nothing is written here!" The head: "Keep turning, King. You will see very soon."

[Shots 51–53 | 466–494 s] The king keeps licking his finger and turning. What he does not know: Duban had smeared every page with a deadly, fast-acting poison. With each lick the poison enters his mouth and body and spreads into his blood.

[Shots 54–57 | 494–530 s] Suddenly the king's sight goes dark; indescribable pain fills his body. He convulses and collapses to the floor. The head on the tray cries out: "O tyrant king! You killed me while I was doing you good. Today your end has come — this is the reward of injustice!" Then the head closes its eyes and dies; within seconds King Yunan too dies from the poison. END OF L2 TALE.

[Shots 58–59 | 530–550 s] L1 TALE (Fisherman & Jinni, by the sea). The fisherman tells the jinni: "Great jinni! Your case is the same. You tried to kill me, so I used my wits to shut you back in that jar. I saved you and you tried to kill me. Now you will never escape this jar!"

[Shots 60–64 | 550–594 s] Inside the jar the jinni, utterly miserable, weeps and begs: "Good fisherman, forgive me! I swear, if you free me I will do you no harm, and I will find you a treasure no human has ever seen. Believe me!" The fisherman pauses to think: "Will you swear by the name of God and firmly promise to do me no harm?" The jinni at once swears by God's name never to harm him and to make him rich.

[Shots 65–68 | 594–626 s] Trusting the oath, the fisherman carefully removes the lead lid/seal. Immediately thick black smoke pours out of the jar and becomes the giant jinni again. The jinni kicks the brass jar into the sea; the fisherman is frightened — but the jinni looks at him and smiles: "Fisherman, follow me. I will keep my promise."

[Shots 69–74 | 626–676 s] The jinni leads the fisherman over great mountains and burning deserts until they reach a wondrous, beautiful lake set among four mountains. Its water is clear as crystal and in it swim fish of four colours — white, red, blue and gold. The jinni: "Cast your net here, catch one fish of each colour and take them to the king of this land; he will pay you a great deal. And remember — never cast your net here more than once a day." Then the jinni splits the ground open and vanishes into it.

[Shots 74–77 | 666–703 s] The fisherman casts once and catches exactly four fish of four colours. Overjoyed, he carries them in a water-filled basin/pot to the sultan's palace. The king, astonished by the marvellous fish, gives him four hundred gold dinars. The fisherman buys everything his family needs and goes home happy.

[Shots 78–81 | 703–738 s] But when the fish are being fried in the palace kitchen, the kitchen wall splits open and a very beautiful woman appears and asks the fish: "O fish! Are you true to your covenant?" The fish answer: "Yes! If you return, we return." Then the woman goes back into the wall and vanishes, and the fish in the frying pan are burnt black. END OF L1 for this episode.

[Shots 81–83 | 728–756 s] FRAME. At this point dawn light enters the room and the first rays of the sun appear. Shahrazad breaks off at once. King Shahryar, desperate to learn the secret of the four-coloured fish and the truth about the woman from the kitchen wall, spares Shahrazad's life for one more day. Cliffhanger.

======================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
======================================================================
This is Part Two of Chapter Eight ("The Fisherman and the Jinni"). The previous episode ended mid-way through the fisherman's embedded tale of King Yunan and the Sage Duban (the vizier's jealousy was introduced). This episode:
- starts with the frame (Dunyazad's request) and resumes mid-tale with the vizier's plot;
- completes the tale of King Yunan and Hakim Duban (Duban beheaded, his talking head kills the king with the poisoned book) — the "ingratitude repaid" lesson the fisherman uses on the jinni;
- returns to the fisherman: he releases the jinni on oath, the jinni leads him to the enchanted lake of four-coloured fish, he sells them to the sultan for 400 dinars;
- opens the next mystery (the Tale of the Enchanted King / Black Islands): a woman emerges from the kitchen wall, talks with the fish, and they are burnt black.
Ends on a cliffhanger at dawn; Shahryar spares Shahrazad another day. (The vizier's counter-tales of the original — the husband and parrot, the prince and the ghoul — are omitted.)

6. TRANSCRIPT PROBLEMS / NOTES
======================================================================
- Shot 4: "އުފާވެރި މަސްވެރިޔާ" ("the happy fisherman") — likely meant "poor/unfortunate"; harmless.
- Shot 13 vs 17: king says he was cured "without drinking medicine or rubbing anything on his body", while vizier says "with a stick held in the hand" (the polo-mallet cure from part one). Consistent with the original; show a polo stick/mallet if needed.
- Shot 17: "މީރުވަސްދުވާ އެއްޗަކުން" = "with something fragrant" (a perfume as murder weapon).

- Shot 43 "އުއްކަވަމުން" vs shot 48 "އުއްތަވަމުން" — both = turning pages (variant forms).
- Shot 58: "މަސްވެރިޔާ ދެއްވާ ދިމާއަށް" — garbled; should be "ދެވިއާ ދިމާއަށް" (toward the jinni).
