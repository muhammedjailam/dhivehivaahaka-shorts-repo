ALIFLAILA (އަލިފުލައިލާ) - EPISODE 599 - READER SYNOPSIS
70 shots, 0-632 s (~10.5 min). Ends with "ނުނިމޭ" (to be continued).

This episode continues "The Fisherman and the Jinni" cycle -> the tale of the four-coloured fish and the Ensorcelled Prince (the young King of the Black Isles, half turned to stone). Shahrazad narrates inside the frame story.

======================================================================
1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
======================================================================

[Shot 1-5 | 0-45 s] FRAME STORY - Shahryar's palace bedchamber, end of night / just before dawn.
Night darkness slowly lifts, cool pre-dawn breeze. Dunyazad wakes her elder sister Shahrazad: "Dear sister, if you are not asleep, continue the tale of the wondrous many-coloured fish and the mysterious woman who came out of the kitchen wall. I am extremely impatient to know what happened after the fish burned to ash." King Shahryar is also eager to learn the secret and grants permission. Shahrazad politely begins: "O noble, mighty King!"

[Shot 6-10 | 45-90 s] INNER TALE - Sultan's palace kitchen (recap of previous episode's ending).
The beautiful woman vanished back into the kitchen wall; the fish in the frying pan (tava) turned into black charcoal. The cook, seeing this, fainted from fright onto the floor. When she/he came to, the fish were burned to coal and inedible. Weeping, the cook went to the Grand Vizier and told everything word for word. The Grand Vizier, astonished, says he will not believe it until he sees it with his own eyes and immediately orders the fisherman brought.

[Shot 11-12 | 89-110 s] The vizier tells the fisherman to bring four fish of four colours again today, like yesterday. The fisherman hurries to the mysterious lake between the mountains, casts his net, catches four fish of four colours again and hands them to the vizier.

[Shot 13-20 | 110-180 s] Palace kitchen - the vizier's test.
The vizier himself cleans the fish, puts oil in the pan and fries them. As he turns them over (one side cooked), suddenly the kitchen wall splits in two. This time not the beautiful woman, but a terrifying, huge, very dark-skinned slave (އަޅު) emerges, so tall his head reaches the kitchen ceiling, holding a branch of a large green tree. In a thunderous voice he shouts at the fish: "O fish! Do you stand by your covenant?" The fish lift their heads and answer in clear human speech: "Yes! Yes! If you return, we return; if you depart, we depart." He strikes the pan with the branch, overturning it; the fish burn to ash. He goes back into the wall and disappears. The vizier trembles with fear, certain this is no ordinary event but a great sorcerous secret.

[Shot 21-27 | 180-241 s] Palace - the King's own test.
The vizier goes to the King (the Sultan in the inner tale; not Shahryar) and reports. The King says he wants to see this with his own eyes - the most extraordinary thing ever in his kingdom. He summons the fisherman and orders four more fish; the fisherman brings them a third time. The King personally goes to the kitchen, locks the doors, and alone with the vizier begins frying. Same thing happens: when the fish are turned the wall breaks open, the terrifying giant comes out and questions the fish; they answer; he overturns the pan and vanishes; the fish are burned black. The King is sure these are no ordinary fish but people who are victims of sorcery.

[Shot 28-31 | 241-281 s] Palace hall - King questions the fisherman.
"Where do you catch these fish?" - "From a lake between four mountains outside this town (ރަށް)." - "How far?" - "Half an hour's walk." The King is amazed: neither he nor any vizier ever knew of such a lake so close to his capital.

[Shot 32-34 | 281-309 s] Expedition, daytime.
The King orders an army (lashkar) prepared; guided by the fisherman, the King, viziers and soldiers march toward the mountains. Between four mountains lies a beautiful crystal-clear lake with the four-coloured fish swimming in it. The King orders the tents (ފޭލިގެ = royal tent/pavilion) pitched there.

[Shot 35-38 | 309-347 s] Camp at night.
At nightfall the King calls the vizier: his heart is restless to find the secret; tonight he will go alone into the mountains. No one is to leave the tent until he returns; if anyone asks, say the King is unwell/indisposed (އާލާސްކަން = feeling unwell/lazy) and resting in private. He takes his sharp sword (ތޫނު ކަނޑިކޮޅު) and walks into the dark mountains. Only desolation and silence.

[Shot 39-41 | 347-374 s] Dawn after walking all night.
At sunrise the next morning he sees from afar a huge, very dark building. Up close: a magnificent great castle built entirely of polished black stone, with the strength of iron. The castle's great gate stands open.

[Shot 42-45 | 374-409 s] Inside the black castle.
Finest silk carpets/curtains, gold and silver ornamentation everywhere, great gardens of fragrant flowering trees, fountains gushing like pearls, walls decorated with precious jewels. But no life, not a trace of a human to speak to. Deep, frightening silence. The King goes room to room calling out.

[Shot 46-48 | 409-435 s] Suddenly he hears a heartbreaking, moving sound of weeping - someone crying and lamenting their grief and helplessness in sorrowful verses (poetry). He hurries toward it: it comes from the great audience hall (darbar room) in the innermost part of the castle.

[Shot 49-57 | 435-510 s] The Ensorcelled Prince.
In the middle of the hall on a golden throne sits a very handsome young prince (ަމީރު), royal and noble face, wearing finest silk robes, eyes full of tears. The King greets him kindly (salaam); the youth returns it and weeping says: "O noble sir, forgive me for not standing to honour you; it is not from pride - you will understand the reason when you see." He lifts the lower part of his silk robe: the King's heart nearly stops - from the waist down to his feet the young man has turned entirely to black marble. He is half a living man and half a lifeless stone statue; he cannot move from the spot.

[Shot 58-62 | 510-558 s] The King, amazed and full of compassion, asks what great calamity befell him, and what secret links this desolate black-stone castle, the four-coloured fish in the lake and his condition - "tell me your sad tale!" The prince sighs deeply, wipes his tears: "My tale would soften hearts of stone and draw tears. The fish in that lake were not fish before - they were living people. This castle and this land suffered this great wrath because of the wickedness and sorcery of my own wife."

[Shot 63-70 | 558-632 s] FRAME STORY - dawn in Shahryar's bedchamber.
"Just as the prince was ready to begin his story..." - dawn light enters the room, the sun rises. Shahrazad stops and falls silent. Dunyazad: "What a wondrous and heartbreaking tale! Why did his wife do him such harm? And who are the four-coloured fish?" Shahrazad smiles: "The young man's tale is even more exciting and terrifying. But it is dawn. If I am alive tomorrow night, I will tell the truth about the wicked sorceress wife, how the youth turned to stone, and what the noble King did to save him." Shahryar, desperate to know the secret of the stone prince and the fish, decides to spare Shahrazad's life one more day, and goes to the palace darbar to attend to the day's affairs of state.
