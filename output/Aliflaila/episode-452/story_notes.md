## Synopsis
The opening of the One Thousand and One Nights. Just King Shahryar of the Sasanian lands sends his Grand Vizier with
a gift caravan to invite his younger brother Shah Zaman, King of Samarkand. On the night of departure Shah Zaman goes
back to the palace for a forgotten gift and finds his wife betraying him; he kills both and leaves in silent grief.
At Shahryar's capital he wastes away until, from a guest-wing window, he sees Shahryar's queen betray the king in the
garden — and his grief lifts. He tells Shahryar, who sees it with his own eyes, has the queen and her servants
executed and decrees a new bride each night, beheaded each morning by the Grand Vizier. Three years of grief; families
flee. The Vizier's learned elder daughter Shahrazad insists on marrying the king; her father gives in weeping; she
coaches little Dunyazad. On the wedding night Shahrazad weeps to have Dunyazad brought in; before dawn Dunyazad asks
for a story. TALE: a rich merchant rests under a tree by a spring, eats dates and tosses the stones; the ground splits,
a giant jinni rises from black smoke: a stone killed his invisible son, blood for blood. The jinni is about to strike
when dawn breaks; Shahrazad stops. The king spares her for one day; the Vizier rejoices. "The real game has only just
begun."
