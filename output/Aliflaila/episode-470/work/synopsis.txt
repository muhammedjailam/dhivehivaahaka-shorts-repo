ALIFLAILA (އަލިފުލައިލާ) - EPISODE 470 - SYNOPSIS
Transcript: 75 shots, 0-671 s (~11 min 11 s). Title card in narration: "ދެވަނަރޭ" = "The Second Night: The Merchant's Promise and His Return".

=====================================================================
1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
=====================================================================

FRAME STORY - Night 2 opens (Shahryar's palace bedchamber)

[Shot 1-2 | 0-27.5 s] Opening title "Second Night". Deep night; the whole great palace of King Shahryar is silent and everyone is asleep - except in the king's chamber. Shahryar lies awake, impatient to learn how the wondrous tale Shahrazad began last night ends.

[Shot 3-7 | 27.5-70.5 s] Dunyazad, sitting on the carpet beside the bed, softly calls her elder sister as the last part of the night approaches: "Dear sister, if you are not asleep, continue yesterday's beautiful story - tell us what happened to that merchant." Shahrazad looks to the king and very politely asks permission. Shahryar quickly says "Tell it." The line "Did that fearsome jinni cut off his head, or was he saved?" (shot 7) reads as the king's/listeners' eager question; Shahrazad smiles and begins.

INNER TALE (Shahrazad narrating) - "The Merchant and the Jinni", continued

[Shot 8-10 | 70.5-106.8 s] Recap: in the desert under the tree, the huge terrifying jinni raised its great sharp sword to behead the poor merchant. Lying on the brink of death, the merchant shut his eyes; he thought of his beloved wives, his little children and his heavy responsibilities.

[Shot 10-25 | 99-238.9 s] The merchant suddenly weeps and cries out: "O mighty jinni, wait! Don't kill me! Give me a little time!" The jinni, sword still raised, says angrily: "Why should I? You killed my most beloved son. I accept nothing but your life." Weeping, the merchant agrees - his son died by his hand, though unintentionally, and he is ready to give his life - but he holds other people's property and trusts; wives and small children depend on him; if he dies here suddenly, those goods are lost and his family will starve. He asks for one year's respite: to go home, pay debts, return trusts, divide his property among his children, bid his family a final farewell, then come back to this very tree. The jinni, fiery eyes fixed on him, asks what guarantee there is he won't flee. The merchant raises his hand and swears by God, Creator of heaven and earth, that exactly one year from today he will return: "I am a Muslim, and a Muslim's promise is more important than his life." The jinni accepts the oath: "One year. But remember - if you do not come, I will find you and destroy your whole family."

[Shot 26 | 238.9-246 s] The giant jinni turns into a huge column of smoke and vanishes into the ground.

[Shot 27-34 | 246-314.7 s] The merchant feels his soul return to his body. He mounts his horse and rides home in mingled grief and relief. After a long journey he enters his big house; wives and children rush to embrace him joyfully - but he bursts into loud sobbing that echoes through the house. His wives anxiously ask why. He gathers them and tells them word for word the terrible event in the desert, and that only one year of life remains to him. A black cloud of grief covers the house; the wives beat their chests and weep; the children's loud crying is heard across the neighbourhood. "Death's grief has come to that house."

[Shot 35-38 | 314.7-346.1 s] From that day his life changes. He settles his final affairs: returns all trusts to their owners, pays every debt in full, gives much wealth in charity to the poor and needy, frees his slaves, divides the inheritance fairly among wives and children, and writes his final will (wasiyyah).

[Shot 39-42 | 346.1-385.3 s] Days pass; in the year's last month the weeping in the house grows. The promised day arrives. That morning the merchant bathes and purifies himself and takes up his own burial shroud (kafan cloth). The final farewell is heartbreaking enough to crack a heart of stone; the children cling to his feet begging him not to go. Eyes full of tears, he leaves to keep his promise and rides toward the fearful desert.

[Shot 43-45 | 385.3-411 s] After many days' travel, on that exact day and hour, he reaches the fearful tree. He dismounts, sits in its shade, recites the Holy Qur'an, prays to God, and waits for death - heart full of fear that the jinni may come at any moment and behead him.

[Shot 46-55 | 411-493.6 s] As he weeps, he sees someone approaching from afar: a very old man (FIRST OLD MAN) leading a very beautiful gazelle/doe on a chain. The old man greets him ("O brother!") and asks in surprise: "This is a terrifying, desolate place haunted by jinn and devils. Why does someone in fine clothes like you sit here alone, so sorrowful, holding his own shroud?" Wiping tears, the merchant tells him everything. Amazed ("Glory be to God!"), the old man praises his truthfulness and faithfulness - only the truthful face their own death so bravely. He pauses his journey to stay, to see what the jinni does, and promises to do whatever he can if the jinni harms him. He sits beside the merchant.

[Shot 56-61 | 493.6-547 s] While they talk, another very old man (SECOND OLD MAN) approaches, holding two big black dogs. He asks why they sit in this dreadful place; the gazelle-man tells him the merchant's story. He too is amazed - "a man truthful beyond belief!" - and stays to see the ending. Both old men sit by the merchant to give him courage.

[Shot 61-67 | 534.9-601 s] Suddenly a violent storm sweeps the area: winds rise, a great dust storm comes at them, sand whirls up to the sky. Everyone trembles in fear. When the dust/smoke clears, the giant jinni stands before them - eyes blazing like fire, great sword raised. It seizes the merchant by the hand and drags him up: "Stand up! As you killed my son, today I will cut off your head! Your time is up!" The merchant weeps loudly; the two old men, unable to do anything, also weep in fear. The jinni lifts its sword highest and swings it down hard toward the merchant's neck... (tale cut off).

FRAME STORY - Dawn (bedchamber)

[Shot 67-70 | ~599-624.9 s] At that exact moment the first beautiful golden rays of dawn enter through the king's chamber window; the Fajr call to prayer (adhan) begins to be heard; Shahrazad breaks off and falls silent. Shahryar, holding his breath, wide-eyed: "Then what? Did the jinni kill him? What happened to the two old men?"

[Shot 71-75 | 624.9-671.2 s] Shahrazad respectfully bows her head: "O noble king, it is dawn; my time is up. But if I am still alive tomorrow night and the king permits, I will tell what happened - even more wondrous and beautiful." Curiosity swells in Shahryar's heart; he knows he cannot bear not knowing. So today too he decides to spare Shahrazad, rises and goes to his court (durbar/darbar). Closing line: "The second night ended. But Shahrazad's wise plan continues."

- THE MERCHANT'S CHILDREN - ކުޑަކުޑަ ދަރިން. Small children; cry loudly, cling to his feet on farewell. Shots 29, 34, 42.
- FIRST OLD MAN (gazelle sheikh) - މުސްކުޅި ފިރިހެނެއް / "ފުއްލާ ގެންގުޅޭ މުސްކުޅިޔާ". Male, very old. Leads a very beautiful gazelle/doe (ފުއްލާ) on a chain (ކަސްތަޅު). Calls the merchant "އަޚާ" (brother). Promises to help. Shots 46-61, 66-67.

- Mentioned only: the jinni's dead son (not seen); creditors/owners of trusts; the poor; freed slaves.

=====================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
=====================================================================
- This is the Second Night of the Nights frame. It resumes mid-tale: "The Merchant and the Jinni" (the first tale Shahrazad tells), picking up at the jinni about to behead the merchant for accidentally killing his son (in the classic version, by throwing date stones). This episode covers the merchant's one-year reprieve, his return home, settling his affairs, his faithful return, and the arrival of the first two old men (the sheikh with the gazelle and the sheikh with two black dogs) - who in the classic tale will each tell a story to ransom a third/part of the merchant's blood. A third old man (with a mule) is not yet introduced.
- Ends on a double cliffhanger: inner tale - the jinni's sword swinging down at the merchant's neck; frame - dawn and adhan interrupt, Shahrazad promises a more wondrous continuation, Shahryar spares her another day and goes to court. Closing: "Second night ended; Shahrazad's wise plan continues."

6. TRANSCRIPT NOTES / PROBLEMS
=====================================================================
- Shot 1 begins with the title "ދެވަނަރޭ:" (Second Night) fused with the subtitle "ވިޔަފާރިވެރިޔާގެ ވަޢުދާއި އެނބުރި އައުން" ("The merchant's promise and return") - read as a title card.
- Shot 7: the question "Did the jinni cut off his head or was he saved?" has no speaker tag; it sits between the king's "Tell it" and "Shahrazad smiled and began" - best attributed to Shahryar's eager question (or Shahrazad's rhetorical opener).
- Nested quotation marks (" and ') are used inconsistently across shots 10-67 because the whole inner tale is Shahrazad's quoted speech; inner dialogue uses single quotes.
- "އަނބިން" (wives, plural) - the merchant has more than one wife; "ރަށް" used for his home town (literally "island").
- "ފުއްލާ" = female deer/gazelle; "ކަސްތަޅު" = chain; "ކުއްތާ" = dogs.
- Shot 63: "ދުންގަނޑު ސާފުވި ވަގުތު" (when the smoke cleared) follows a dust storm - treat as dust/smoke cloud.
- Shot 67-68 boundary: the tale's ellipsis "..." and "ބަރާބަރު އެ ވަގުތު" (at that very moment) start the dawn transition in shot 67's tail.
- No garbled words noticed; names consistent (ޝަހްރިޔާރު, ޝަހްރަޒާދު, ދުންޔާޒާދު).
