## Synopsis
- FRAME (shots 1–12, night): Night Three falls over Shahryar's palace. Dunyazad asks her sister to continue; the
  impatient king asks whether the jinni killed the merchant.
- TALE (13–29, desert oasis): the jinni is about to strike the kneeling merchant when the First Old Man (with the gazelle)
  shouts "Stop!" The jinni rages (the merchant killed his son) but loves wondrous stories and agrees: if the old man's
  story is more wondrous, he forgives a third of the merchant's blood.
- TALE-WITHIN-THE-TALE (29–89, soft golden storybook glow): the gazelle is the old man's wife (his elder brother's
  daughter / cousin). Thirty childless years; he takes a young slave girl (card `young_mother`) who bears a beautiful
  son. The wife burns with jealousy. When the boy is ten the father travels for a year; the wife learns sorcery and
  turns mother and son into a cow and a calf, handing them to the herdsman. On his return she lies: the mother died,
  the boy ran away. A year of grief. At Eid al-Adha the herdsman brings the fat cow; it weeps at his feet; he cannot
  sacrifice it, but the wife insists and the herdsman sacrifices it — it was only bones (sorcery). The calf breaks its
  rope and weeps at his feet; he spares it despite the wife. Next day the herdsman reveals: his daughter knows the calf
  is the son and the cow was the slave girl. The daughter sets two conditions (marry the son; enchant the wife), breaks
  the spell with water and words; the son is restored, married to her, and the wife becomes the gazelle.
- TALE (90–94): the amazed jinni grants a third; the Second Old Man with two black dogs offers his tale for another third.
- FRAME (95–104, dawn): Fajr breaks, Shahrazad falls silent, the king begs for more, she promises the dogs' story
  tomorrow; he spares her again and goes to his court. "To be continued."
