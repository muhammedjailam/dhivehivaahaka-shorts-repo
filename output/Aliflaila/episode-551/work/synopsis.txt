Title card in narration: "ރޭ ހަތެއް" (Night Seven): "The fisherman's cleverness and the defeat of the jinni"

1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS (in order)
======================================================================

[Shot 1–4 | 0–33.8 s] FRAME STORY. The title is spoken: Night Seven, the fisherman's cleverness and the jinni's defeat. Deep darkness falls and stars come out. A heavy silence settles over King Shahryar's big, imposing palace. The king is restless and eager for the rest of the tale. On the sixth night Shahrazad broke off the story of the poor fisherman and the terrible jinni, and he badly wants to know what happens next.

[Shot 5–10 | 33.8–102.4 s] FRAME. In the last, quietest hour of the night, Dunyazad sits as usual on a costly carpet beside the bed. She softly asks her elder sister ("dear sister") to finish the story of the poor fisherman and the terrible creature that came out of the brass jar found in the sea. She says her heart is pounding to know whether he survives. Shahrazad looks at the king with her beautiful dark eyes, bows her head politely and asks permission to go on. Shahryar answers quickly: "Begin at once!" He wants to know why the jinni called out Solomon's name and what happened to the fisherman.

[Shot 11–13 | 102.4–126.4 s] INNER TALE begins (Shahrazad narrating). Shahrazad addresses the "mighty, wise king" and picks up where she stopped. The giant jinni has come down to the ground, begging Solomon to spare its life. The old fisherman, shaking with fear, sees this and feels a little calmer.

[Shot 14–17 | 126.4–162.6 s] INNER TALE, seashore. The fisherman realises the jinni is badly confused. He gathers his courage and, in a trembling voice, tells it that Prophet Solomon died 1,800 years ago and that this is a different age. He says he is only a poor fisherman who lives by casting his net.

[Shot 18–23 | 162.6–215.2 s] INNER TALE. The fisherman says he is the one who opened the jar and freed it. The giant jinni suddenly stands up tall. Its burning eyes widen, and its fear and pleading turn into extreme rage. It roars like thunder and the beach sand shakes: "You are not Solomon, just a lowly human? Then how dare you speak to me so rudely? Get ready, today is your last day. Choose how you want to die!"

[Shot 24–27 | 215.2–251.6 s] INNER TALE. The fisherman feels the ground split under his feet. Weeping, with his hands joined, he begs for mercy "for God's sake". He says he pulled it from the dark jar, saved it from the sea's depths and freed it, and asks whether death is his reward for a kindness. "What injustice is this?" The jinni shouts "Yes! Your reward for saving me is death."

[Shot 28–36 | 251.6–333.7 s] INNER TALE, with the jinni's backstory as a FLASHBACK (a tale inside the tale). The jinni says its name is Saakhir / Sakhr and that it is extremely powerful. It grew proud above the other jinn, devils and "devis" (demons) and disobeyed Prophet Solomon. When all other creatures obeyed, it rebelled. Solomon sent his grand vizier Asif ibn Barkhiya to capture it. Asif overpowered it, bound it and brought it before Solomon. Solomon commanded it to obey and to believe in God, but its arrogance made it refuse. In anger, Solomon had this thick brass jar brought and shut the jinni inside. The jar was stopped with a thick lead cap, and Solomon's blessed signet ring pressed a seal bearing God's Greatest Name onto the lead. His army was then ordered to throw it into the deepest part of the sea.

[Shot 37–46 | 333.7–430.9 s] INNER TALE, flashback continued. The fisherman stands frozen with fear. The jinni describes its centuries in the cold jar at the bottom of the dark sea:
 - First 100 years: it swore to give its rescuer the greatest wealth in the world for life. No one came.
 - Second 100 years: it would open every treasure hidden beneath the earth for its rescuer. No one came.
 - Third 100 years: it would grant any three wishes. Still no one came.
 - Fourth 100 years: its patience ran out and its heart filled with hatred. It swore to kill its rescuer at once, with only one choice offered: how to die.
"Today you came and saved me, so you must die. Your time is up. Quickly, how do you want to die?"

[Shot 46–51 | 422–473.4 s] INNER TALE. The fisherman sobs and pleads that he is a poor man with a wife and small children at home. He mentions a little daughter and son who are hungry, "like three starving chicks", and says they will starve without him and that God will punish the jinni. The jinni has no mercy. Its children are not its concern, it says, its oath cannot change and he has no option but death. "Choose quickly!"

[Shot 52–58 | 473.4–540.7 s] INNER TALE, turning point. The fisherman realises begging and crying won't save him. He gets an idea. In an inner monologue he thinks: this is just a stupid jinni with a big body and strength but no mind, while he is a human given intelligence by God, and he will use his wits to beat this giant ghost-like jinni. He stops crying, wipes the fear from his face and looks firmly at the jinni. "Fine. I accept you'll kill me and I'm ready. But before my soul leaves, I want to ask one question. Swear by the Greatest Name of God on that jar's seal that you'll answer truthfully. Then I'll stretch out my own neck to die."

[Shot 59–61 | 540.7–568.3 s] INNER TALE. Hearing God's Name, the jinni's huge body trembles, since jinn and devils know the power of that Name. It nods and swears by the Name to answer truthfully: "Ask quickly!"

[Shot 61–71 | 561–650.9 s] INNER TALE, the trick. The fisherman points at the jar and acts amazed. How could a giant whose body reaches the clouds and whose feet stand firm on the ground fit inside this small jar? The jar isn't even big enough for its hand, or even one toe. "You are clearly lying, I don't believe it at all." The jinni is astonished and its pride is hurt: "You don't believe it, even after seeing me come out of it with your own eyes?" The fisherman says firmly he only saw smoke, and he will never believe it unless he sees it go fully inside. "You can't get in there!"

[Shot 71–78 | 644–717.1 s] INNER TALE, climax. The jinni's pride and arrogance soar and it can't stand having its power doubted. "Watch closely, you foolish human! I'll show you how I go in!" Its giant body slowly dissolves back into a huge black column of smoke. The smoke rises, covers the whole sky, then swirls down like a cold wind into the mouth of the brass jar lying on the ground. It flows in until all of it is gone. From inside the jar the jinni calls: "Do you believe now? I'm inside the jar! Do you believe now?" Without answering, the fisherman quickly steps forward, picks up the thick lead lid from the ground, puts it on the jar's mouth and presses it down hard to seal it. Cliffhanger in the inner tale.

[Shot 79–84 | 717.1–779.5 s] FRAME, dawn. As Shahrazad reaches this point, the lovely golden light of dawn shows on the horizon. The sweet sound of the dawn (Fajr) call to prayer spreads across the whole capital. Shahrazad stops at once and falls politely silent. Shahryar is completely absorbed, eyes wide, and suddenly sits up. "What happened next? Did the fisherman lock the jinni in? What did the jinni do? Was the fisherman saved?" Shahrazad smiles calmly. It is dawn and her time is up. If she is still alive tomorrow night, she will tell what passed between the jinni trapped in the jar and the fisherman, and how he saved his life, which is an even more exciting and wiser story.

[Shot 85–88 | 779.5–819.7 s] FRAME, morning. The king's curiosity is at its peak and he knows he can't bear not knowing. So again today he decides to spare Shahrazad. He gets up, puts on his royal robes and goes to the court (darbar) to rule. Night Seven ends. Thanks to her storytelling skill, the executioner's sword is put off for one more day. Next comes the eighth night, which will show the result of the fisherman's cleverness. "To be continued" (ނުނިމޭ).

2. CHARACTERS
======================================================================

FRAME STORY
- ޝަހްރިޔާރު ރަސްގެފާނު: King SHAHRYAR. Male, adult (about 35–45). A powerful king who executes his brides, now hooked on Shahrazad's tales. Restless and impatient. At the climax he sits up with wide eyes. At dawn he dresses in royal robes and goes to court. Shots 2–4, 8–10, 79–87.
- ޝަހްރަޒާދު: SHAHRAZAD. Female, young adult (about 20–25). The king's wife and the storyteller. Narration mentions "beautiful dark eyes" and a "soft, beautiful voice". She is polite, bows her head, asks permission and smiles calmly at dawn. She is Dunyazad's elder sister. Shots 4, 8–11 (and as narrator voice through 78), 79–84, 86–87.
- ދުންޔާޒާދު: DUNYAZAD. Female, Shahrazad's younger sister (teen, about 14–17). She sits on a costly carpet beside the bed, eager, and calls Shahrazad "ދައްތާ" (elder sister). Shots 5–8.
- ޖައްލާދު: the EXECUTIONER. Only mentioned ("the executioner's sword postponed again"). Shot 87. Do not show.

INNER TALE ("The Fisherman and the Jinni")
- މަސްވެރިޔާ: the FISHERMAN. Male, old ("މުސްކުޅި", about 60s), poor, lives by casting his net. Has a wife and small children: "little daughter and son", compared to "three starving chicks", so probably three children. He starts out shaking, weeping and pleading with joined hands, then turns calm, firm and clever. At the climax he grabs the lead lid and seals the jar. Shots 7–8, 10, 13–27, 37, 46–78, 81–86 (mentioned).
- ޖިންނި / the JINNI, named ސާޚިރު (Saakhir, i.e. Sakhr). Male, ancient. A giant body "reaching the clouds", feet planted firmly on the ground, burning/blazing eyes, a thunderous voice that shakes the sand. It can turn into a black column of smoke. Proud, arrogant, merciless, stupid. Also called "ބިރުވެރި މަޚުލޫޤު" (terrible creature) and compared to a "ހިޔަނި" (ghost/phantom). Shots 1, 7, 10, 12–78, 81–84.
- ސުލައިމާނުގެފާނު: Prophet SOLOMON (Sulaiman). Only in the jinni's flashback and in dialogue. Shots 10, 12, 15–16, 29–36. SACRED FIGURE: NEVER DEPICT. Show only symbols such as the ring seal, a throne hall seen from far away or empty, or light.
- އާޞިފް ބިން ބަރްޚިޔާ: ASIF IBN BARKHIYA, Solomon's grand vizier. Male, a dignified elder. In the flashback he captures, binds and brings the jinni. Shots 30–31. He is a religious-tradition figure too, so show him from behind, in silhouette, or only as hands with chains.
- Solomon's ލަޝްކަރު (army/soldiers): they throw the jar into the sea in the flashback. Shot 36.
- The fisherman's wife and children are only mentioned (shots 47–49). They could appear as a brief imagined insert of a poor hut with hungry children.

Spelling variants: Shahryar is consistently ޝަހްރިޔާރު. The jar is "ރަންލޯ ފުޅި" (shots 7, 75) and "ބޯ ރަންވަންލޯ ފުޅި" (shot 34), both meaning a brass/yellow-copper jar, not gold. The jinni's name ސާޚިރު appears only once (shot 28).

4. SENSITIVE MOMENTS + SAFE VISUAL
======================================================================
- Shots 10, 12, 15–16, 29–36: PROPHET SOLOMON in the dialogue and flashback. Never depict him: no face, no body, not even a silhouette. Use symbolism: a glowing signet ring and a seal pressed into lead (shot 35, with NO legible Arabic or God's Name, just a glowing abstract emblem), an empty majestic throne hall with light, and the jinni kneeling before a radiant light.
- Shot 35, 58–61: GOD'S GREATEST NAME on the seal. Never render actual sacred text. Show a glowing seal/emblem with a soft light. The jinni trembles at the glow.
- Shots 30–31: ASIF binds the jinni. Show chains glowing around the jinni and Asif's figure from behind or as a dim silhouette. No violence beyond the binding.
- Whole inner tale (12–78): JINN / MAGIC / supernatural. The jinni is a giant smoke being, with smoke transformation in shots 73–76. Keep it fantasy-styled: a smoky, monstrous but not gory giant. Shot 29 also mentions devils and "devis". Avoid any demonic or occult symbols (no pentagrams, no idols).
- Shots 22–23, 27, 43–46, 50–51: DEATH THREATS against the fisherman ("choose how you'll die"). No weapon and no killing are shown. Use the looming jinni over the cowering old man, his face, his clasped hands, and low-angle shots.
- Shot 59: "I'll stretch out my neck to die" is spoken only. Show the fisherman with a calm, determined face, not a neck-offering pose.
- Shots 24–25, 46–49: the fisherman weeps and begs. That is an emotional reaction shot and fine to show. The hungry children insert should be modest and non-graphic (empty bowls).
- Shot 87: the EXECUTIONER'S SWORD is only mentioned (the execution postponed). Show no sword and no executioner. Use the sunrise over the palace, Shahrazad safe, or the king leaving for court.
- Shot 79: the FAJR CALL TO PRAYER. Show a minaret silhouette at golden dawn over the city. Respectful, with no people shown praying.
- Bedchamber scenes (1–10, 79–86): there is no intimacy in the text. Keep everyone fully and modestly dressed. Shahrazad and Dunyazad wear modest gowns and veils, with the king seated or reclining.
- There is no alcohol, adultery, blood or injury in this episode.

======================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
======================================================================
- This is Night Seven of the frame story (the title card says ރޭ ހަތެއް). It continues "The Fisherman and the Jinni", which broke off on Night Six just after the jinni came out of the jar and began begging Solomon for mercy.
- It starts mid-tale and has a short frame-story recap first.
- It covers the jinni's backstory: Sakhr's rebellion against Solomon, capture by Asif, sealing in a brass jar, and four centuries of vows that ended in the vow to kill its rescuer. Then the fisherman's trick: he doubts the jinni could fit in the jar and tricks it into going back in.
- It ends on a CLIFFHANGER: the fisherman has just slammed the lead lid back on with the jinni trapped inside. At dawn Shahrazad stops and the king spares her again. Night Eight will show "the result of the fisherman's cleverness" (the talk between the trapped jinni and the fisherman).

6. TRANSCRIPT NOTES / PROBLEMS
======================================================================
- Shot 1 "ރޭ ހަތެއް:" is a spoken episode title ("Night Seven: the fisherman's cleverness and the jinni's defeat"). After it, the text slides straight into "...the deep darkness of the seventh night spread over the world".
- Shot 4 says the tale was interrupted on Night Six ("ހަވަނަ ރޭ"), which is consistent.
- Shot 11 has "ފަށުވި ރީތި އަޑުން". "ފަށުވި" is probably a typo for "ފަސޭހަ/ފަސްވި", i.e. a soft/gentle voice.
- Shot 16: "1,800 years" since Solomon's death, a numeric detail.
- Shot 28: "ސާޚިރު" (Saakhir) is the jinni's name, i.e. Sakhr. It is close to "ސާހިރު" (sorcerer), so it is a slightly ambiguous spelling, but it reads as a name here.
- Shot 29: "ދެވިތަކު" means devis/demons (Persian div).
- Shot 34: "ރަންވަންލޯ" is a variant of "ރަންލޯ" (brass). "ބޯ ތިމަރަ" means a thick lead lid.
- Shot 48: "ކުޑަ މަންޖެއާއި ސޮރު" (little daughter and son), then "three chicks". The child count is slightly inconsistent; read it as three small children.
- Shot 54: "ހިޔަންޏެއް ފަދަ ޖިންނި" means a "ghost/shadow-like jinni".
- Shot 59: "ކަރުދިއްކޮށްލާނަމެވެ" means "I will stretch out my neck".
- Shot 75: "ހިމަ ރޮއްޖެއް" is an odd phrase, probably "a cold wind". It may be intended as "ހިމަ ވައިރޮޅިއެއް" (a whirlwind).
- Shot 88 ends with "ނުނިމޭ" (to be continued).
- Otherwise the text is clean, with no garbled stretches.
