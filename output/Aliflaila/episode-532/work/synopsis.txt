ALIFLAILA (އަލިފުލައިލާ) - EPISODE 532 - SYNOPSIS
Title spoken in shot 1: "ރޭ ހައެއް: ފަޤީރު މަސްވެރިޔާއާއި ރަންލޯ ފުޅީގެ ވާހަކަ"
= "Night Six: The Tale of the Poor Fisherman and the Brass Jar"
82 shots, ~758 s (12 min 38 s). Narration: Dhivehi (Thaana).

======================================================================
1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
======================================================================

--- FRAME STORY: the sixth night in Shahryar's palace ---

[Shot 1-2 | 0-23 s] Title card spoken ("Night Six: the poor fisherman and the brass jar"). Deep darkness of the sixth night spreads over the world, stars twinkle in the sky; a deep silence falls inside King Shahryar's great, awe-inspiring palace. The king's mind is full of eagerness for a new story.

[Shot 3-4 | 23-41 s] On the fifth night Shahrazad had promised the tale of the poor fisherman and the terrifying jinni; the king lies impatient to hear it. As the last, stillest part of the night approaches, Dunyazad - sitting beside the bed as usual - softly calls her elder sister.

[Shot 5-9 | 41-90 s] Dunyazad (eager, expectant): "Dear sister, if you are not asleep, tell us the new story you promised yesterday - the poor fisherman and the wondrous jar found in the sea." Shahrazad looks toward King Shahryar with her beautiful, deep-black eyes, bows her head respectfully and asks his permission. The king answers quickly, "Begin at once!" - he is desperate to know what the fisherman found in the sea and what terrible creature came out of it.

--- INNER TALE (narrated by Shahrazad): The Fisherman and the Jinni ---

[Shot 10-15 | 90-147 s] Shahrazad begins in her soft, lovely voice. Long ago, on the shore of an inhabited island/town, lived a very poor, aged fisherman with a wife and three children. They lived in extreme poverty and hunger - many days only a piece of dry bread and a sip of water. But he was diligent and kept a strict self-made rule: each day he casts his net into the sea exactly four times, never more, and goes home with whatever he gets.

[Shot 15-19 | 143-189 s] One morning, well before sunrise, the old fisherman sets out to the shore with his big net over his shoulder, hoping for sustenance for his family. At the water's edge he takes off his outer garment, wades into the water, says the name of God (Bismillah), whirls the net at arm's length and casts it. He waits; soon the rope grows taut and the net feels extremely heavy. He rejoices: "Today God has granted me a huge fish or many fish!"

[Shot 20-25 | 189-241 s] CAST 1. He hauls with all his strength - chest heaving, breath short, sweat pouring. Finally he lands it, but his joy turns to grief: no fish, only a big DEAD, ROTTING DONKEY. He says "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" and laments his bad luck. He removes the carcass, washes and cleans the net, and spreads it out again.

[Shot 26-33 | 241-315 s] CAST 2. He moves to another spot, says Bismillah, casts a second time. Again very heavy, even heavier. "Surely a big fish this time!" He dives/plunges into the water, grabs the rope, plants his feet and hauls for a long time. He opens the net: only a big clay pot (jar) full of mud, sand and seaweed/muck. Heartbroken and exhausted, he complains about the world's misfortune and his poverty, recites a line of verse (a lament poem), and weeping, throws the pot out, washes the net and clears the mud.

[Shot 33-37 | 305-350 s] CAST 3. He goes a little further to deeper water and, grieving and praying, casts a third time. Heavy again; after a long struggle he lands it - only broken glass bottles, bones, shells and seabed rubbish. His patience runs out.

[Shot 37-42 | 341-396 s] PRAYER. He falls to his knees, raises both hands to the sky and with tearful eyes pleads: "O Lord who decrees all things and provides sustenance! You know I cast my net only four times a day. This is my fourth and last. At home my wife and three children are hungry. Bless my net this time and grant my family good provision!" Gathering all his courage, he says "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim" and throws his fourth and final cast; the net spreads in the air and sinks into the salt water.

[Shot 43-47 | 396-445 s] CAST 4. After waiting, he pulls - the rope won't budge at all; the net is snagged on something enormously heavy on the seabed. Pulling with all his body achieves nothing, so he is forced to dive under, free the net from where it is stuck, and with great effort drag it ashore. Exhausted and panting, he sits beside the net to look inside.

[Shot 47-54 | 436-502 s] THE JAR. No fish again - but instead an extremely heavy, large, wondrously shaped jar of red-gold (brass/copper). It is beautifully polished, its mouth sealed with a thick lead stopper, and on the lead is stamped a large special seal - the noble seal of Prophet Sulaiman (Solomon). A smile of joy spreads over the old man's face. He lifts the very heavy jar: "Praise be to God! This is a very valuable brass jar. If I sell it to the metal (brass) sellers in the bazaar I'll get at least ten gold dinars - my family will eat for many days."

[Shot 54-59 | 493-547 s] He shakes it: it feels full but makes no sound. Curiosity rises: "What is so heavy inside? Maybe precious jewels. Before selling it I want to see." He takes the small knife tucked in his waist belt, wedges its tip under the lead stopper and pries with great force; after long effort the thick lead stopper comes loose and falls to the ground. He sets the jar on the ground and watches.

[Shot 59-62 | 539-576 s] At first nothing. Then a thick, pitch-black smoke begins pouring out, rising, spreading over the sky, hiding the sunlight and darkening the whole area, spreading over the sea's salt water and the land. Suddenly the smoke gathers, solidifies, and takes the form of a terrifying, gigantic jinni.

[Shot 63-66 | 576-614 s] Shahrazad addresses the king and describes the jinni: so tall that with its feet on the ground its head touches the clouds; head like a great dome; arms like huge long palm trunks; legs like the trunks/roots of giant trees; mouth like a deep dark cave; teeth like mountain boulders; nose like a great water-tank (garbled image); eyes like two huge blazing torches/lamps. A sight too terrible for a human mind.

[Shot 66-72 | 609-667 s] The old fisherman's strength drains away, his teeth chatter, and he falls backward in terror, speechless. The jinni looks at him with blazing eyes and roars with a thunderous voice that shakes the earth: "La ilaha illallah! There is no god but God, and Sulaiman is His prophet!" Then the jinni bows down to the ground and pleads (believing it is still addressing Sulaiman): "O Prophet of God Sulaiman! Do not kill me! I will never again disobey your command! I will obey you completely!"
-- inner tale breaks off (CLIFFHANGER) --

--- FRAME STORY: dawn ---

[Shot 72-73 | 657-678 s] As Shahrazad reaches this point, the beautiful golden light of dawn appears in the east. The sweet call to the Fajr prayer (adhan) spreads across the capital. Shahrazad immediately stops and falls respectfully silent.

[Shot 74-75 | 678-697 s] Shahryar, completely lost in the story, eyes wide, suddenly sits up: "Then what happened? Why did the jinni call out Sulaiman's name? Tell me quickly what happened to the fisherman!"

[Shot 76-78 | 697-726 s] Shahrazad smiles gently: "O noble king, it is dawn; my time is up. But if I am still alive tomorrow night, I will tell you the jinni's secret and how the poor fisherman faced the giant jinni with his cleverness - an even more thrilling and frightening story."

[Shot 78-82 | 718-758 s] The king's curiosity is overwhelming; he is sure he cannot bear not knowing. So today too he decides to spare Shahrazad. He rises, puts on his royal robes and goes to the court/durbar to rule. The sixth night ends; through Shahrazad's storytelling skill the executioner's sword is postponed one more day. Next: the seventh night, where the fisherman's cleverness will be revealed.

======================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
======================================================================
- This is Night Six of the Thousand and One Nights frame. The previous night (Night Five) ended with Shahrazad promising "the poor fisherman and the terrifying jinni"; this episode starts that new tale from its beginning (not mid-tale).
- Tale told: "The Fisherman and the Jinni" (opening part) - four casts, the brass jar sealed with Solomon's seal, the jinni released.
- Ends on a CLIFFHANGER: the jinni has just appeared, roared the Shahada and begged "Sulaiman" for mercy; the king wants to know why. Shahrazad stops at the Fajr adhan and promises for Night Seven "the jinni's secret" and how the fisherman outwits it. The king spares her another day and goes to court.
