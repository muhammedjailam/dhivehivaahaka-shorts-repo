= "The Fisherman's Trick and the Tale of King Yunan"

STORY LAYERS (keep these clear in the visuals)
- FRAME: Shahrazad tells stories at night to King Shahryar, with her younger sister Dunyazad present, in the palace bedchamber. (shots 1-5 and 66-72)
- TALE 1 (Shahrazad's story): The Fisherman and the Jinn, by the sea. (shots 5-31, picked up again at 66 and 70)
- TALE 2 (told inside Tale 1, by the fisherman to the jinn in the jar): King Yunan and the Sage Duban, in a Persian kingdom. (shots 32-65)

1. DETAILED SYNOPSIS
=====================================================================

[Shot 1-4 | 0-38 s] FRAME. The title is spoken first. Night lies silent over the whole palace and the stars of the coming dawn begin to show in the sky. Dunyazad calls to her elder sister Shahrazad and asks her to carry on with the story of the poor fisherman and the frightening giant jinn. She says she cannot wait to hear whether the jinn killed the fisherman or he escaped. King Shahryar is just as keen to hear the rest and gives permission.

[Shot 5-6 | 38-57 s] FRAME to TALE 1. Shahrazad politely addresses the king and says she will start where she stopped the night before. She gives a short recap: the fisherman spoke God's exalted Name.

[Shot 6-12 | 50-116 s] TALE 1, on the beach. When he hears the Name, the jinn shakes with fear and his huge body trembles. He admits the fisherman has called on a mighty Name and offers to explain everything. The fisherman says he wants to see with his own eyes how the jinn lay inside this brass jar for centuries. He says the jinn must be lying and that he really came from the bottom of the sea, not out of the jar. (The speaker changes in these lines are not marked; see section 6.) The jinn is too proud to let a human doubt his power, so he goes back into the jar to prove it. From inside he gloats: "Foolish fisherman, do you believe me now? Look, I am inside the jar! Do you see my power?"

[Shot 13-17 | 116-165 s] Without wasting a second, the fisherman puts the lead (ތިމަރަ) lid back on the mouth of the jar and presses it down. The lid is stamped with the seal of Prophet Sulaiman (Solomon). The jar seals at once and the jinn is stuck inside. He pushes and bangs from inside, but the power of the holy seal keeps him in. No force could open the lid. The jinn sees how foolish he was and how clever the fisherman has been, and he starts to weep loudly. His arrogance is gone.

[Shot 18-23 | 165-213 s] The fisherman picks up the jar and walks towards the sea behind him. The jinn calls out from inside: "Kind fisherman, what are you doing? Where are you taking this jar?" The fisherman says he will throw it into the deepest part of the sea, where the jinn can lie until Judgement Day. He will also warn everyone who comes to this shore that a vengeful jinn lies at the bottom and will kill without mercy anyone who frees him. He says it is his duty to protect the world from the jinn's evil.

[Shot 23-26 | 207-237 s] The jinn weeps and begs: "My master, forgive me! I only said those things as a joke. Forgive my foolishness! Save me and I will make you rich for life, bring happiness to your children and grant whatever you wish."

[Shot 26-31 | 228-282 s] The fisherman laughs. He says the jinn is a liar and cannot be trusted, and that the two of them are like King Yunan and the Sage Duban: if he lets the jinn go, the jinn will do to him what King Yunan did to Duban and will surely kill him. Puzzled, the jinn asks who King Yunan and Sage Duban were and what happened between them. He asks to hear the story before he is thrown into the sea. The fisherman sits down at the water's edge, puts the jar in front of him, and starts telling it.

[Shot 32-37 | 282-328 s] TALE 2, told by the fisherman. Long ago, in a great Persian kingdom (ފާރިސްކަރަ), there lived a king named Yunan. He was very rich and powerful, led a great army, ruled over hundreds of islands or lands, and had palaces built of gold and silver. But a great sorrow had come into his life. He had leprosy (ބޮޑުދަދު / ޖުޒާމު): white patches covered his body and his skin was decaying, until nobody dared to come near him.

[Shot 37-41 | 328-376 s] The best physicians and healers from every corner of the world tried many medicines and ointments, and none of them worked. As the king was close to giving up on life, a very learned old sage came to the land. This was the Sage Duban (ޙަކީމް ދޫބާން). He knew Greek, Persian, Latin and Arabic perfectly. He had read thousands of books on herbal medicine, astronomy, anatomy and the treatment of disease, and had mastered all of them.

[Shot 41-47 | 376-442 s] When Duban hears of the king's illness, he goes to the palace and comes before the king at court. He bows politely and says he knows the other physicians have failed, but with God's will he will cure the king completely, without any bitter medicine to drink and without any ointment on his body. The king is astonished and can hardly believe it. He promises Duban great wealth and money, and to make him his closest friend, if he can do this.

[Shot 47-53 | 438-496 s] Duban goes home, gathers his books and medicines, and skilfully makes a polo stick (mallet) with a hollow handle. He prepares a special, powerful medicine, pours it into the hollow handle, seals the top, and also makes a polo ball. The next day he gives the stick and ball to the king. He tells him to ride his horse to the polo field and play with this stick. Once his hand begins to sweat, the medicine will pass through the skin of his palm into his body. After the game he should bathe straight away and then sleep, and all will be well.

[Shot 54-57 | 496-532 s] The king does exactly that. He mounts his horse, grips the mallet and plays with great excitement, galloping after the ball. He sweats heavily, and the medicine passes from his palm through his skin into his blood. After the game he returns to the palace, bathes in hot water...

[Shot 58-62 | 532-584 s] ...and lies down to rest. The next morning he wakes, looks at his body and sees a miracle he can hardly believe. Every patch and every sign of the disease has gone, and his skin is clean and smooth like pure silver. The king weeps with joy. He calls Duban to the palace at once, gives him costly silk robes and makes him rich with gold dinars. He names Duban his closest companion, gives him the honour of sitting beside him at court, and gives him new gifts every day.

[Shot 62-65 | 580-611 s] But the king's happiness did not last long. At court there is a wicked, jealous vizier whose heart has turned black. When he sees the honour and respect the king gives Duban, a great envy grows in him, and he plots to destroy Duban by any means and ruin him in the king's eyes... (The fisherman's tale breaks off here.)

[Shot 66-72 | 611-674 s] FRAME. As the fisherman's story reaches this point, Shahrazad notices sunlight entering the room and stops at once. Dunyazad says in wonder: "What a beautiful, exciting story! What did the jealous vizier do to Duban?" Shahrazad smiles and says the rest is even more wonderful, but dawn has come. If she is still alive tomorrow night, she will tell how the wicked vizier poisoned the king's mind, the tragedy that came to Sage Duban, and whether the fisherman threw the jinn into the sea. King Shahryar badly wants to know what happens to Duban, so he decides to spare Shahrazad's life for one more day, and he goes to the palace court to carry out the day's business of state.

=====================================================================
- Jinn / supernatural (shots 2-31). Use a smoky, shadowy giant form or swirling dark smoke over the sea. Show the jinn re-entering the jar (shot 11) as smoke spiralling down into the jar mouth. Keep it non-gory and stylised.
- God's Name being invoked (shots 6-7, 43). Never write or show sacred text. Show it through reaction: the smoke shrinks back or trembles.
- Seal of Prophet Sulaiman on the lid (shots 13, 15). Never depict the Prophet. Show an ornate engraved metal lid with a plain geometric star or ring emblem: no readable script and no figure. A close-up of hands pressing the lid down works.
- Death threats: the jinn would "kill without mercy" (shot 22), "you will surely kill me" (shot 28), and the fisherman threatens to drown the jinn until Judgement Day (shots 20-21). These are verbal only. Show the fisherman holding the jar above the waves, or a stormy sea.
- The jinn weeping and begging (shots 17, 23-26). Show a trembling or rattling jar with wisps of smoke seeping from it, and the fisherman's sceptical face.
- Leprosy / disfiguring illness (shots 35-38). Show nothing graphic. The king can be wrapped in a cloak or veil with hands hidden, sitting alone. Courtiers keep their distance, and physicians shake their heads over bowls of medicine.
- Bathing (shots 53, 57). No nudity. Show a steaming marble bath-hall, a basin of hot water and attendants holding towels, or cut straight to the king resting in bed, fully clothed.
- Waking cured, "skin like silver" (shots 58-60). Show the king looking at his clean hands and forearms with tears of joy. Keep him fully robed.
- Frame execution threat (shots 69-72, implicit: "if I am still in the world tomorrow"). No sword and no executioner. Show Shahrazad's calm smile against the dawn window, with the king's thoughtful face.
- The vizier "poisoning the king's mind" (shot 69) is a METAPHOR, not literal poison. Show a whispering courtier in shadow, which is fine.
- No romance, intimacy, alcohol, idols or immodest clothing in this episode. The frame is set in a bedchamber: keep Shahrazad and Dunyazad modestly and fully dressed and seated, with Dunyazad present.

=====================================================================
5. PLACE IN THE LARGER STORY
=====================================================================
- The episode starts in the MIDDLE of "The Fisherman and the Jinn" (Tale 1). In the previous episode the fisherman netted a brass jar sealed with Sulaiman's seal, opened it, and freed a giant vengeful jinn who threatened to kill him. The fisherman then invoked God's Name and doubted the jinn could have fit inside the jar.
- In this episode the fisherman tricks the jinn back into the jar, and then begins the embedded "Tale of King Yunan and the Sage Duban" (Tale 2) up to the cure and the rise of the jealous vizier.
- Ending: a classic dawn CLIFFHANGER. Shahrazad stops at the vizier's plot. She teases that next time she will tell how the vizier poisons the king's mind, Duban's tragic fate, and whether the fisherman throws the jinn into the sea. Shahryar spares her for another day and goes to court.
- The next episode will probably continue with the vizier's slander, which in the classic story includes the vizier's "Tale of King Sindbad's falcon" and similar, and later Duban's poisoned book.

6. TRANSCRIPT ISSUES
=====================================================================
- Shot 1 starts with the episode title read aloud and merged into the first narration sentence. Shot 1 mentions both night silence and "dawn stars appearing", which suggests a late-night or pre-dawn setting. It is not a mistake.
- Shots 7-11: speaker attribution is unclear. Shots 7-8a are the jinn ("You called on a great Name; I will show you how it happened"). Then, unmarked, shots 8b-10a are the FISHERMAN ("I want to see you go into this jar... you are lying; you came from the sea bottom, not the jar"). Shot 11 is the jinn again, after re-entering the jar.
- The jinn is named in several ways: ދެވި, ދެވީ (shot 10) and ޖިންނި (shot 11). All refer to the same character.
- Duban is spelled ދޫބާން or ދޫބާން, with case forms ދޫބާނަށް, ދޫބާނާ and ދޫބާންގެ, and the title ޙަކީމް.
- ފާރިސްކަރަ is "Persian land". In the classic version this is "the city of Fars in the land of the Rum"; here it is simply Persia.
- Quotation marks are inconsistent. Nested quotes mix ' and ". Duban's speeches inside the fisherman's tale use ", and the transcript ends with a stray closing ." (the whole episode is wrapped as Shahrazad's speech).
- "ޕޯލޯ" (polo), "ދަނޑިގަނޑު" (mallet/stick) and "އޮއިންޓްމަންޓް" (ointment, an English loanword) are used. "ތިމަރަ މަތިގަނޑު" means the lead lid.
