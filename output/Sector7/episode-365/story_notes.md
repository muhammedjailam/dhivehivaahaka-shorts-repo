# Sector 7 — Episode 365 (prologue) — story notes

## Synopsis
Fifty years ago the most dangerous war in history burned the world to ash: first secret, then open tactical, then the
largest nuclear weapons were used; nobody won and the surface became a poisoned, uninhabitable desert. The rich and the
leaders of nations built four giant bunkers stocked for 50 years, and let in a few "lucky" poor people to run and repair
the machines — in effect as slaves. One of them is "The Sanctuary" (Aman Bingaraas), two miles under the ground near
Bremen, Germany, split into seven levels: Level 1 "Udubburi" at the top, with an artificial golden sun, fresh air, real
fruit and luxury; Sector 7 at the bottom, a living hell of rust, toxic drips, chemical fumes, roaring hydraulic machinery
and dim bulbs, where workers do 16-hour shifts and eat mind-dulling "grey paste" on steel bunks, never having seen a sky.

Aira (26) sweats in Sector 7's main engine room, maintaining the pipes that send clean water and oxygen up to Level 1.
A harsh Council overseer bangs a pipe with a steel rod and threatens to cut rations; her old co-worker Bashir flinches and
afterwards whispers: "Don't raise your eyes in front of them — to them we are scrap iron." But Aira burns with anger at
the injustice. On a break she takes out the steel pendant her father Malik, a brilliant engineer, put round her neck the
night he was taken to a "secret project" 13 years ago when she was 10 (the Council later declared him missing). The code
"50.12.01" engraved on it is her only hope he is alive; she vows to find out what happened to him and to see the real
world's light. Suddenly sirens wail and red lights flood the corridors: the control panel shows the main oxygen pipe at
Junction-9 — the "death zone" — has burst. Aira straps on her old oxygen mask and toolkit and goes down alone through
cold, flooded corridors with live cables sparking into the water and poison gas in the air, climbs over collapsed steel
and reaches the huge door of Junction-9. While welding the crack she glimpses a black military-grade encrypted box
hidden inside the pipe; on it is her father's handwriting: "The truth is not buried." She realises the pendant code is
its key. Keeping any knowledge of the surface is punishable by death, yet she hides the box at the bottom of her toolkit,
finishes the repair and slips away past the cameras, determined to find the truth her father left behind.

## Tone
Dark, gritty dystopian prologue: an epic, cold world-building opening (ash, gold vs. rust), then oppressive exhaustion
in the engine room, a tender sorrowful memory, sudden red-alarm urgency, a lonely brave descent, and a secretive
mystery ending. Underground light only: amber/sodium work lights, red alarm light, cold headlamp beams, welding sparks.

    luxury atrium with onlookers behind her at the deepest point of the bunker, which contradicted "unseen by the
    cameras". (beat_023 kept: the atrium is only a small blur in a corner.)
