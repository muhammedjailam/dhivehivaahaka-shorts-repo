# Emme Fahu Message — episode 327: story notes

## Synopsis
Dr. Mathews tells Amaan and Eethan the cycle is negative. Amaan goes numb; the doctor talks hormone levels, percentages
and statistics, none of which answers her only question ("why does it happen for everyone but us?"). Eethan calmly asks
practical questions (another cycle? what do you recommend?) and Amaan admires him for still looking for another door.
The drive home is silent: wipers, rain sliding down the passenger window, the city a grey blur. At a red light Eethan
rests his hand on hers; they squeeze each other's fingers.

That evening the apartment is heavily silent; the kitchen clock ticks loudly. Rain clouds gather over the city, the
first drops hit the sitting-room window. Amaan scrubs one spot on a mug for five minutes while Eethan unpacks the
groceries. He suggests a weekend away by the sea, a little cabin; she doesn't want to go, doesn't want to do anything.
"You don't have to be fine." — a broken laugh — "I'm NOT fine": five years of needles, medicine schedules, marks on
her arms, pregnancy news from people who don't even try; "you don't know how it feels when your own body fails you."
She pulls her hand away when he reaches for it, says they should stop the treatments. He says he'll support whatever
she decides; she flares ("just like that?"). He: "I'm choosing you." She: "I always cared." He admits his heart is
broken too — he wanted to teach their son to ride a bicycle, dance at their daughter's wedding — but only with her; a
dream that costs him her is worth nothing. For a moment she wants to step into his arms, but guilt wins: "One day you'll
wake up and know you married the wrong woman. You deserve someone who can give you a real family." His face drains:
"Do you really think that of me?" "I think I ruined your life." "You never ruined a single day of my life." No answer.
"I'm going for a drive." He takes the car keys from the table by the door ("I just need some fresh air"), puts his hand
on the handle, says "I love you" — her pride swallows the answer — and leaves. Amaan sinks to the kitchen floor,
crying, "I didn't mean it." Her phone vibrates on the counter: Eethan. She watches until it stops; it rings again; she
declines it. A minute later the screen lights again: a voice message from Eethan. She turns the phone face down: "I'll
listen later."
