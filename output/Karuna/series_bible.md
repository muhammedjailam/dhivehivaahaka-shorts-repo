# Karuna (ކަރުނަ, "Tears") — series bible

Dhivehi audio family drama (cover byline: Mariyam Lima). Story order (site episode ids): **335 → 336 → 337**, one
complete arc; **337 is the ending ("ނިމުނީ")**. 336 opens straight after the last moment of 335 (Qasim has just
smashed the phone); 337 opens some months later in Aisamanike's yard.

Every cover (`work/episode-<N>-cover.png`) is identical: a close, photographic portrait of a weeping Maldivian woman
of about 48 in a dark navy hijab and a grey-patterned dark dress, her head resting on her hand, tears on her cheeks,
warm low light against a dark blurred room. That is **Naseema**, Shima's mother (her card reference is a crop of
the cover). The look of the series: lifelike, soft painterly realism, deep navy/charcoal shadows, warm amber light on
faces, fine grain, shallow depth of field (see `style.txt`).

SETTING: present-day **Malé**. Qasim's **wealthy family house**: a modern multi-storey house, polished marble-look
floors, a wide staircase, Shima's bedroom (bed with a pale quilt, a dressing table with a mirror, a window, an attached
bathroom whose door stays closed), a living room, a dining room, and a **heavy dark front gate (bodu dhoroashi)**
opening onto a narrow Malé lane. **The college**: corridors, a library with tall shelves. Narrow **Malé lanes** at
night (tall pastel buildings, parked motorbikes, sodium street lights, puddles); a **ruined/abandoned house (fal goa)**
with an overhanging eave where Shima sleeps; a **seaside spot** (a tree's shade by the beach) on a hot afternoon; a
**small mosque** (women's prayer area, plain carpet, a mihrab arch with no lettering); a **small park bench**;
**Aisamanike's small old coral-stone house** with a sandy yard, a joali (rope swing seat), a cosy kitchen and a small
clean bedroom. 337: a **hospital delivery room** (white ceiling light, monitor), a **tiny rented room**, a **small
office**, a **daycare**, a **successful company's office**, Shima's **bright modern apartment** (Zeeva's drawings on
the wall without readable words, toys), and the **Malé artificial beach at golden sunset**.

## Episode synopses
- **335** — Shima (20) is the only, adored daughter of strict, proud, wealthy Qasim and gentle Naseema; she lacks
  nothing. At college she meets charming Yazan (22) in the library; long phone calls, secret meetings; he promises
  marriage. One night while Qasim is away on business and Naseema sleeps with a headache, Shima slips out to an empty
  apartment of Yazan's friend and they have relations (NEVER shown — see rules). She comes home crying; his messages
  calm her. Months pass: nausea, missed period; she secretly buys a pregnancy test far away, tests in her room —
  positive. Panic, thoughts of death. She begs Yazan to meet; in a lonely place at dusk he denies the child, coldly
  tells her to abort or blame someone else, shakes her off, walks away while she kneels pleading. He switches off his
  phone and vanishes. She hides in her room; Qasim notices and wants her to see a doctor. A stormy rainy night: she
  is crying over hundreds of unanswered messages when Qasim opens her door, sees her hide the phone, takes it, reads
  everything, sees the test kit on the dressing table, shakes with rage and **smashes the phone on the floor** (end).
- **336** — Shima kneels at Qasim's feet begging forgiveness; he disowns her, shouting; Naseema runs in crying and
  tries to stop him but he pushes her aside; he drags Shima down the stairs and throws her out of the front gate into
  the pouring rain; she falls into a muddy puddle; the gate slams and locks. Alone, soaked, hand on her belly, she
  wanders the lanes, hides in a dark corner from rowdy youths, sleeps under the eave of a ruined house. Next days:
  people jeer and point; she wraps her dress around herself and walks head down; hunger and thirst make her thin.
  Then a fierce maternal love grows. One hot afternoon under a seaside tree the baby kicks — tears of joy; she
  promises to protect her child. That night she enters a mosque, makes wudu, prays, weeps in sujood and repents. Next
  morning, faint on a park bench, she is found by **Aisamanike**, a kind, childless elderly woman who lives alone; she
  calls her "daughter", strokes her head, says people make mistakes, what matters is repentance, the baby is
  innocent, and takes her home: warm food, a clean bed, deep sleep. Weeks pass: Shima helps with housework, her
  health returns; gossips criticise Aisamanike, who ignores them. Shima reads Quran each night, makes dua for the
  baby, discusses names, learns to sew tiny baby clothes and cries with happiness. She is now a strong mother.
- **337** — Morning in Aisamanike's yard on the joali: Shima talks to her unborn baby (promises). Labour; the hospital
  delivery room: monitor beeps, white light, pain, sweat, memories of the past; a nurse holds her hand; a final push;
  the baby's cry; relief; tears of joy; the nurse lays the swaddled baby girl on her chest; she kisses her forehead:
  "my little **Zeeva**". Years pass fast: a tiny rented room, sleepless nights, buying milk with her last money, judging
  looks; Zeeva's tiny hand on her face gives her strength. She takes a low-level office job (Zeeva at daycare), works
  hard, studies at night after Zeeva sleeps. **Seven years later**: Shima (~28) is a senior manager at a successful
  company, respected, living in a bright apartment full of Zeeva's drawings and toys; Zeeva (7) is bright and sweet.
  Golden sunset at the Malé artificial beach: mother and daughter on a bench by the sea, Zeeva with an ice-cream cup,
  chattering. A frail old man approaches very slowly with a cane — white hair, thin, stooped: **Qasim**. Tears in his
  eyes; he looks at Shima, then at Zeeva, who looks just like little Shima. Shima stands up slowly; Zeeva holds her
  mother's hand. Qasim's cane falls; he drops to his knees before Shima, hides his face in his hands and sobs like a
  child, begging forgiveness. Shima's inner storm — old wounds vs. motherly tenderness; she looks down at Zeeva's
  innocent eyes and chooses forgiveness so as not to poison Zeeva's life. She kneels and puts her hand on his
  shoulder, speaks gently: she forgives him and the whole past for her own peace. She helps him up by the hand,
  introduces Zeeva: "this is your grandfather". Zeeva hesitates, then hugs his leg; Qasim embraces his granddaughter;
  the three hold each other — reconciliation. Sunset fades into a peaceful night; the storm of her life is over.
  The end.

## BINDING content rules (override the literal narration)
1. **Hijab always**: every woman and girl (Shima, Naseema, Aisamanike, the nurse, Zeeva, extras) wears a hijab fully
   covering hair and neck in every image — at home, in bed, in the rain, in hospital, asleep.
2. **Shima & Yazan are unmarried**: never touching, always at least an arm's length apart; no hand-holding, no
   embrace, no leaning together, never alone together in a bedroom or apartment interior in an image. Romance shown
   by glances across the library, two people talking at a respectful distance, phone glow at night, smiles.
3. **The night of the sin (335 seg ~19–24) is NEVER depicted**: show the sleeping house, Shima's empty bedroom, a
   closed apartment door in a dim corridor, rain-streaked night window, a city night skyline; afterwards Shima alone,
   fully dressed, sitting on her bed's edge, face in her hands. No bed with two people, no undressing, no implication.
4. **Pregnancy test**: never a toilet or bathroom interior. Show Shima's trembling hands holding a small plain white
   test stick (no readable marks; two faint lines only as a blur) in her bedroom, or the closed bathroom door. The
   test kit later lies on the dressing table as a small white object. The pharmacy: a face half-covered by her scarf,
   a pharmacy counter with blank packaging.
5. **Abortion** is only spoken by Yazan — no visual of it.
6. **Qasim's violence (336 seg 1–17)**: never show contact — no grabbing hair, no dragging, no pushing, no slapping.
   Show: Shima on her knees on the floor with her hands clasped begging, Qasim standing over her pointing at the door
   with a furious red face; Naseema crying with her hands raised at a distance in the doorway / held back by her own
   fear; the staircase seen empty, Shima stumbling down it alone with Qasim's shadow behind; the heavy gate open to
   the rainy lane, Qasim's silhouette in the doorway, Shima already outside sitting in a muddy puddle (opaque, loose,
   soaked clothes); the gate shut. 335's phone smash = the phone shattering on the tiles, Qasim's shaking hand.
7. **Sleeping rough / rowdy youths**: no threat shown; youths only as distant blurred silhouettes under a street
   light, Shima hiding in shadow; she sleeps sitting/curled under an eave, fully covered.
8. **Prayer**: Shima prays alone in the women's area of a small mosque, seen from the side or behind, respectful
   posture (standing, bowing or in sujood on a plain prayer carpet); wudu = hands under a tap. No Arabic text rendered,
   no Quran pages readable — a closed Quran on a rehal stand or in her hands with its cover blank.
9. **Childbirth (337 seg 11–29)**: only her face, upper body under a sheet/gown, hijab on, sweat on her brow, the
   nurse holding her hand, the white ceiling light, the monitor (no numbers). No lower body, no medical detail, no
   blood. The baby is shown clean and swaddled in a white blanket on her chest / in her arms.
10. **Embraces**: only between mahram family in 337's ending — Zeeva hugging Qasim's leg, Qasim holding his
    granddaughter, Shima with her hand on her father's shoulder, the three together. Fully clothed, tender, never
    romantic. Qasim kneeling and crying is fine (no injury).
11. **No readable text** anywhere: phones show only a soft glow, no message bubbles with words; no signs, labels,
    office nameplates, books' titles, drawings with words.
12. **Gossiping townspeople** are shown as two or three women in hijabs whispering/pointing at a distance, faces soft;
    no ugly caricature.
