# Karuna — Episode 337 (series finale) — story notes (v2 re-cut)

## Synopsis
Months after 335/336, heavily pregnant Shima sits on the joali in Aisamanike's yard one morning and promises her unborn
child a pure, happy life, protected from people's blame — even at the cost of her own. Labour begins; she has no fear,
only hope. In the cold hospital delivery room the heart monitor beeps, the white ceiling light blurs, sweat runs down
her face; through the pain she remembers being thrown out of the gate on the rainy night and society's blame. A kind
nurse holds her hand for the final push — a push out of the darkness of the past toward hope. A tiny cry breaks the
silence; all pain vanishes; tears of joy. The nurse lays the clean swaddled baby girl on her chest. "Mother's little
Zeeva" — she kisses her forehead and vows never to let her be lonely or hurt.
The years are hard: a tiny rented room, sleepless nights, buying milk with her last rufiyaa while going hungry, judging
looks at an unmarried mother; Zeeva's tiny hand on her face gives her strength. She starts in the lowest job in a small
office (Zeeva at daycare), is noticed for her honesty, studies at night. Seven years later she is a respected senior
manager living in a bright apartment full of Zeeva's drawings and toys; Zeeva (7) is clever, sweet and joyful.
At golden sunset on the Malé artificial beach, mother and daughter sit on a bench, Zeeva with an ice-cream cup. A frail,
white-haired, stooped old man approaches slowly with a cane: Qasim. Tears in his eyes, he looks at Shima, then at Zeeva,
who looks just like little Shima. Shima freezes; the memory of the gate slamming on the rainy night returns; she stands,
Zeeva holding her hand. Qasim's cane falls; he drops to his knees, hides his face and sobs, begging forgiveness: out of
fear of society he threw out his own blood; she has succeeded without him; seeing her and his angel granddaughter breaks
his heart. A storm inside Shima — old wounds vs. motherly tenderness; she looks into Zeeva's innocent eyes and chooses
not to poison Zeeva's life with hatred. She kneels, lays her hand on his shoulder and tells him gently that she has
forgiven him and the whole past for her own peace. She raises him by the hand: "Zeeva... this is your grandfather."
Zeeva hesitates, then hugs his leg; Qasim holds his granddaughter; the three hold each other. Sunset gives way to a
peaceful night; the storm of her life is over — the beginning of a happiness that never ends. "ނިމުނީ" (The End).
